# DEC-935: The paper run judges its broker facts at a clock read taken after the last of them

| **Decided by** | claude-code (builder), under DEC-176: the reading tightens no bound less and adds no risk |

## Decision

1. `mandate-paper`'s `run()` reads its run clock once, **after** the preflight's last broker
   read (account, positions, open orders, asset record, quote, minute bars), and judges,
   computes and records everything after the preflight at that instant: the shell judge (asset
   record, ETP classification date, quote, regular session, close window), the liquidity
   figures, the daily closes' trust check, the model's evaluation, the contexts, the watch's
   bound and the cycle's clock. The configuration's date is the clock's date at the start of the
   run, before the control stream is read.
2. No bound moves. Every age bound (the asset record's and the quote's `iex_quote_max_age_s`)
   is measured from that later read, so each fact is at least as old as it was at the start's
   clock, never younger; a fact stamped after that read (`AheadOfClock`, `quote_at > now`) is
   refused as before; the quote's own age check at its read, inside the preflight, is
   unchanged; the binding gate rechecks the quote and the session at its own clock immediately
   before submission, as before.
3. The configuration's date and the run clock's date cannot differ for a run that places: the
   UTC date changes at 19:00 or 20:00 New York, after the regular session's close, so the
   judge's regular-session check refuses any run whose later read falls on another date.

## Rationale

#1355's dry run refused every real run: the asset record is stamped at its own read
(`TradingClient::asset`), always after a clock read taken at the start, and the judge refused
it as ahead of the clock ("Validate: a current asset record"). The run's tests passed because
their clocks never moved between reads. Measuring from a read taken after every fact is the
only reading under which "a fact stamped after the clock is refused" and "every fact the run
read is eligible" hold together. It shortens no window and drops no check: against the start's
clock, any fact the run accepts now is older, so each age test is stricter, and the
future-stamp test applies to the same facts at a clock that is no earlier than any of their
reads.

The ETP classification date and the daily bars' last session are compared with the later
clock too; both come from records read after the start, so the start's clock could refuse a
record written while the run read it, the same defect. A later clock accepts a record dated
between the start and the read only where the record was already there to be read.

## Alternatives considered

- **Widen the judge with a skew tolerance.** Refused: it loosens a bound and lets a
  future-stamped fact through.
- **Re-read the clock inside the judge for each fact.** Rejected: the judge stays a function of
  its inputs (no clock read in the shell), and one read after the last fact gives each fact a
  clock taken after it with one instant for the whole judgment.
- **Stamp the asset record with the start's clock.** Refused: it would make the record look
  older or younger than it is, and its age would no longer be its own.

## Not covered

#1355 also shows "Reconcile: a current IEX quote" twice. That refusal is the quote's own age
check at its read in the preflight, which already measures from a clock read after the quote
arrives; a quote stamped ahead of this host's clock (clock skew) or older than 10 s refuses
there, by design, and this decision changes nothing about it.
