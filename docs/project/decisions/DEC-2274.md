# DEC-2274: The liquidity floor's and participation's inputs are read trusted from the stored bars, by one function, at each gate's own clock

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted (agent: a reversible engineering decision under DEC-79 and DEC-176. It turns two deferred checks on only where their inputs are trusted, and loosens no limit or safety rule) |
| **Date** | 2026-10-26 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | an agent, under DEC-79 (`claude-code`, lane SC); the coordinator approved the plan, with lanes D1 and X wiring their halves on top |
| **Story or claim** | E21-29 lane SC; DEC-2378 item 11 condition 2; DEC-2385 items 1, 3 and 6; trading-domain spec §3.2 item 5 and §9.6; DEC-470 item 4 |

## Context

DEC-2378 item 11 lets the cell's runtime dry-run an opening while it defers four checks whose
facts nothing journals. Two of them, the liquidity floor (§3.2 item 5: the prior close and the
20-day median dollar volume) and participation (§9.6: ADV and the trailing five-minute volume),
"turn on once the data service's daily and 1-minute bars are readable" (condition 2). Since
DEC-2385 the cell's data process stores those bars, write-once, in the artifact store. The runtime
and the executor both mount that store read-only. Only `mandate-shell` computes the figures today,
and the runtime may not link the shell (`xtask/layers.toml`).

## Decision

1. **One reading in `mandate-bars`.** `mandate_bars::liquidity(root, symbol, now)` returns the
   four figures (`LiquidityFacts`): the prior close, the 20-day median dollar volume and ADV from
   `trusted_daily_bars` through `mandate_liquidity::daily_liquidity`, and the trailing five-minute
   volume from `trailing_minutes` through `mandate_liquidity::trailing_volume`. It finds the
   instrument's daily and one-minute datasets under `root` by `mandate-marketdata`'s own layout
   (US equity, IEX, as the data process stores them). The shell's daily half
   (`trusted_daily_liquidity`) moves onto the same function unchanged, and every refusal keeps
   its words, as DEC-2385 item 1 did for the trust check. The runtime and the executor read the
   figures through this one function, so they cannot disagree on them, and the function stays
   under the mutation gate.
2. **Read trusted, not journaled.** Each gate reads the store at its own journaled clock: the
   runtime's dry run at its risk clock, and the executor's binding gate at its binding clock. It
   never reads the other's figures. The store's partitions are write-once and the clock is
   journaled, so a replay of the same partitions at the same clock gives the same figures. The
   chart names the root to the runtime and the executor as `MANDATE_BARS` only when the data
   process runs (`images.data`): in the cell, the artifact store's `datasets/cell-recorded`. With
   no root, nothing is readable.
3. **The dry run (lane D1, over `evaluate_deferring`).** When the read succeeds, the runtime fills
   the gate input's prior close, median dollar volume, ADV and trailing volume from it, and does
   not defer the liquidity floor or participation. `DecisionMade` then records the four figures
   and the clock they were read at (a field of D1's `DecisionMade` version 3 (journal spec v0.85, DEC-2388 item 10)). A refused read, an
   empty window, or a root that names no dataset for the instrument keeps both checks
   `deferred_to_binding`, never passed. The runtime's reader answers only for an opening or an
   increase; an exit, a protective order and a flatten never call it (`AGENTS.md` rule 13).
4. **The binding gate (lane X, G2b).** The executor's `BindingGateSource` fills
   `MarketSnapshot.trailing_5m_volume` and `adv_20d`, and the instrument snapshot's prior close
   and median dollar volume, from the same function at its binding clock. A refused read is
   `None`, which is an unavailable input, so the opening is refused (`AGENTS.md` rule 3). The
   executor stays the authority (rule 1). Exits never consult it.
5. **What this does not decide.** Eligibility's and the day-trade budget's facts (DEC-2378 item 11
   condition 2's other half), staging's bar root (DEC-2385 item 7's later slice), and the
   `DecisionMade` version 3 field's exact shape, which is D1's.

6. **What lands first.** Lane SC's change delivers items 1 and 2 and the runtime's reader of
   item 3 (`mandate_daemon::bars`), which answers only for an opening or an increase. Until D1
   and X wire items 3 and 4, both checks stay deferred in the dry run, as before. Each wiring
   lane adds its call site's test: a readable store, and an exit that never reaches the reader.
   The reader answers all or nothing, which keeps the floor deferred whenever the minutes are
   missing, as before the open. That only holds more, and D1 may split it once the floor's daily
   figures are recorded on their own.

## Rationale

- One function below both gates means the advisory and binding answers rest on the same reading,
  and neither keeps its own copy of the rule.
- Each gate reading at its own clock keeps the executor independent of the runtime's answer.
- The stored partitions never change, so nothing needs journaling to make a decision replayable.

## Alternatives considered

- **The runtime journals a liquidity event that the executor reads.** The executor would then
  trust the runtime's figures, and it must be the authority. Rejected.
- **The data process journals the figures.** It holds no journal role (DEC-2385 item 3).
  Rejected.
- **Each process computes its own figures.** Two readings could disagree, so the owner could be
  asked about an opening the binding gate then refuses on figures the owner never saw. Rejected.
