## Decision

1. **Each part of the long page sells one thing the visitor gets**, in a short headline and one or
   two plain sentences, and leaves how it works to the desktop's homepage and the app. It amends
   DEC-907 item 2, whose parts described the mechanism:
   - the opening keeps "Hire an agent. Keep the keys." and says the money stays in the owner's name;
   - "Your rules. No exceptions." (was the checks, in order);
   - "You make the big calls. From anywhere." (was "Your phone says an agent needs you. Nothing
     more."); the lock screen still shows only the push the product sends;
   - "Ask it why. It wrote it down." (was the agent's thread);
   - "Set the stakes. In dollars." (was the limits and the headroom), with the gap caveat beside it,
     word for word;
   - "It won't" keeps its verbs, each with a promise rather than a rule: borrow, sell short, trade
     options, chase a price. "Trade overnight" is gone, since crypto trades around the clock;
   - the request for a place is "Put an agent to work.".
2. **The bar lists no parts.** At the founder's request ("remove section names from the top, no
   anchor should be listed"), the long page's bar carries the logo back to the desktop, Sign in and
   Sign up, and nothing else; the page is read top to bottom, not jumped through.
3. **Selling stays inside the rules that already bind the site.** No performance figures, returns,
   testimonials or urgency; no exclamation marks; nothing the desktop already says, sentence for
   sentence; the footer's disclaimer and the gap caveat unchanged. `long-page.test.tsx` fails if a
   headline or line uses the machinery's words (limit order, safe point, headroom, passkey, regular
   session, buying power, margin, checks, thread, mode, "Nothing more").
4. **Each picture shows one moment, at a size it can be read.** Learned from getnick.ai, whose
   every claim sits beside one live widget drawn at its real size, at the founder's request ("learn
   from them"). The four parts' pictures were whole screens shrunk to about half, so their words
   were too small to read and the picture was the mechanism again. Each is now cut to the piece that
   makes the part's point, located by its elements rather than by pixels
   (`scripts/landing-shots.mjs`), and drawn no larger than it was taken (`shot.tsx`):
   - "Your rules. No exceptions.": the order marked Not allowed, and below it the check that stopped
     it, order size at most $1,000.00, after one that passed;
   - "Ask it why. It wrote it down.": a request waiting with why it asked, the questions to ask next
     ("Why did it ask?") and the box to ask in;
   - "Set the stakes. In dollars.": the ladder of limits in dollars, from the high-water mark down to
     the floor.

   The opening's agent page and the phone's request stay whole. Under the opening's buttons, as
   getnick.ai puts "1,000 credits. No card. No time limit." under its own, one quiet line says what
   it costs and what is at stake: "Free during the beta. It starts on paper money." Its pricing
   tiers, logo walls and backer badge were not copied: a price is the founder's to set, and the long
   page claims nothing it cannot show.
5. **The Scroll cue, and the owl that peeks over it, sit in the middle of the screen**, not the
   middle of the browser window, which sits off centre on the desktop; the founder saw both off
   centre. The cue stays on the status bar's row when that bar runs under the middle with room for
   it, and otherwise sits near the bottom edge (amends DEC-907 item 6).
6. **The opening stands on a pixel sea.** The founder found the plain paper behind the agent's page
   "too simple" and pointed to recent.design, where the strongest openings set the product in a
   scene rather than on a blank page. The agent's page now floats on a tide sea (`pixel-sea.tsx`)
   that starts just above it and runs below it: rows of pale waves, each a stroke with a sharp
   crest leaning forward over a steeper face and open water in its trough, white where a tall one
   breaks, small and close by the horizon and longer, taller and further apart nearer, as a sea
   recedes; a row of foam along the horizon; a moon in sun with its light laid on the water in
   broken bars; two pine islands in ink (pale grey in the dark theme); and, from 1024px, gulls
   clear of the words. The founder asked for better animation, then found waves that jumped a cell
   at a time neither smooth nor like waves. So each row glides continuously, the nearer rows faster,
   and heaves up and back on a swell that reaches each row a beat after the one above, so the sea
   rolls; every movement lands on a whole pixel (a device pixel on a dense screen), so it is smooth
   without the strokes blurring between pixels. The foam laps, the moon's light glints between two
   frames, each gull beats twice and glides, and the moon rises out of the water as the opening
   scrolls into view, ending half risen, where it rests with motion
   reduced. It is Hiroshige's bay, in the same flat tokens and square cells as the lock screen's pixel
   night and the thread, which turns pale where it crosses the sea; no gradient, glow or blend.
   It is decoration: hidden from assistive technology, still under reduced motion, and behind the
   picture, so the headline, buttons and the picture's words sit on paper or on the app as before.
   The owl perched on the picture wears its pale coat there. This spends tide on a second band of
   the long page, so it amends DEC-907 item 4: tide fills the part about asking you and the sea
   under the opening's picture, and is otherwise trim. `long-page.test.tsx` still fails on tide in
   any file outside the long page, and on a second tide section.

## Rationale

The desktop already explains what Owlhead is and how it works, and the app shows it. A visitor who
keeps scrolling is deciding whether they want it, so each part answers "what's in it for me":
control, money that stays put, a say in the big decisions, an answer to "why", and stakes in
amounts they can picture. The pictures carry the detail.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the mechanism and add a benefit line above it.** Rejected: twice the words, and the
  founder's point was that the mechanism is not the pitch.
- **Outcome claims ("grow your money", "never lose more than your limit").** Rejected: no
  performance claims exist or may be implied, and the gap caveat says a loss can pass a limit.
- **A soft gradient, a glow or a photograph behind the opening (item 6).** Rejected: the site bans
  gradients and glows, and a photograph would fight the app's picture for attention; the plate
  already carries the one painting. The pixel sea uses the long page's own language.
