## Context

The journal lets an agent propose an exit at the market: `IntentProposed` rule 3 refuses only an
opening or an increase at the market, and rule 2 then requires `limit_price` to be `null`. Trading
spec §5.1 allows a reducing or closing order at the market in the regular session, outside auction
windows, with current status data, and §5.6 prices a marketable exit by the exit ladder everywhere
else.

The executor core takes only limit orders from a handoff (`IntentBody::Order.limit` is a `Price`).
So the executor process holds every exit at the market it cannot price: an owner's exit with no
confirmed floor, and every risk or discretionary exit at the market. DEC-2033 item 4 recorded that
hold as a gap against rule 13, which lets only `paused` or `stopped`, an `Unknown` order, or the
broker hold an exit. This decision closes the gap.

## Decision

1. **A handoff may carry no limit, for an exit only.** `IntentBody::Order.limit` becomes optional.
   The core refuses a handoff that opens or increases with no limit (rule 12: openings are limit
   orders) as a non-canonical `limit_price`, before anything is journaled. An owner's exit at the
   market with a confirmed floor is still handed as a limit at that floor (DEC-2033 item 4); one
   without a confirmed floor, and every other exit at the market, is handed with no limit.
2. **`IntentReceived` records it as proposed.** An exit with no limit is received with `type`
   `market` and `limit_price` `null`; every other intent is received with `type` `limit` and its
   limit, as today. The journal's schema already allows a `null` `limit_price`; journal spec v0.65
   adds stream rule 245, which binds the two members at either version: `market` only for an
   exit, and `limit_price` null exactly for `market`. Its vectors are the `market_intents`
   section. `mandate-journal` enforces it in its own DEC-77 pair.
3. **The gate paces it from a reference price.** The risk gate sees a market order, priced for its
   checks at the exit ladder's first rung (§5.6 step 1). The core
   honours the risk gate's `marketable_limit_required`: a market order the gate bars goes as a
   marketable limit instead. A discretionary exit's paced price is its limit, as today.
4. **The core sends it at the market where §5.1 allows, and by the ladder elsewhere.** It goes as a
   market order only in the regular session, outside the closing window, on a fresh, sane quote
   that shows a bid (never into a book with no buyer), with no protection resting and no §5.4
   sequence running, and when the risk gate did not require a marketable limit. Otherwise the core
   prices it by the §5.6 ladder from the reference bid. An exit with no limit is never passive.
   On an instrument the snapshot gives no exit tier, it is laddered by §5.6's wider tier (other
   equities and crypto: 1%, 1% a step, 5% at most) rather than held, since no such hold is on rule
   13's list (review of #1791).
5. **Nothing to price from (Proposed for the founder).** §5.6 step 4 sends a risk or owner exit
   with nothing to price from at "its intent's own limit". An exit at the market has none, and a
   market order is barred without current data (§5.1). The default, until the founder decides: such
   an exit is held as §5.6 step 4 holds a discretionary exit today. An owner's exit with a
   confirmed floor never reaches this case, since it carries the floor as its limit (item 1). The hold is journaled once (`unpriced`), the owner is
   alerted, the gate re-checks it on every tick, it resumes from the next priceable quote or trade,
   and it is never dropped or refused. The alternative, a market order sent without current data,
   is the founder's to choose. This hold is a remaining gap against rule 13, recorded here, not
   accepted as a reading of the rule. While it lasts:
   - **The kill switch stays available.** The owner can always flatten: an agent's or the
     connection's kill switch closes the position without reading a model or a quote (trading
     spec §5.5), and the owner's own exit at a confirmed floor carries its limit (item 1).
   - **The hold ends at the first price of any kind:** a fresh sane bid, a sane bid within the last
     five minutes, or a last trade in session (§5.6 step 1).
   - **A test pins it:** a held exit at the market is handed on the first tick that follows a fresh
     bid (`tests/market_exits.rs`).
6. **Owner exits are paced only by participation caps.** The check E7-29 asked for: the risk gate
   slices an owner's exit by the participation caps and applies no collar, close window or other
   control to it (`mandate-risk` `pacing`), and `Purpose::exempt_from_pacing` leaving `OwnerExit`
   out changes only the session it may go in.
   - **A known open gap against rule 13.** The handoff carries no owner confirmation, so an owner's
     exit with a confirmed bid waits for the regular session like an unconfirmed one, where rule 13
     lets a confirmed one sell outside it. It is recorded here, not accepted as a reading of the
     rule. Backlog story E7-30 closes it by carrying the confirmation in the handoff, and starts
     immediately after E7-29's last slice.
7. **The executor process hands every exit.** DEC-2033 item 4's hold and its two alerts
   (`owner_exit_unpriced`, `exit_unpriced`) end: the process hands an owner's exit at the market at
   its confirmed floor, as today, and every other exit at the market with no limit, which the core
   prices.

## Rationale

The ladder already prices a marketable exit from the reference bid, independent of the handoff's
limit, and the risk gate already knows a market order and when it is barred. Item 5's hold is far
narrower than DEC-2033 item 4's: it applies only when there is no price of any kind, which is the
case §5.6 step 4 already holds for a discretionary exit.

## Alternatives considered

- **Price every market exit in the executor process before the handoff.** Rejected: the process
  has no quotes, and it would duplicate the ladder outside the core.
- **A separate `IntentBody` variant for a market exit.** Rejected: every match on the body would
  need a new arm, and the two variants would share all but one member.
