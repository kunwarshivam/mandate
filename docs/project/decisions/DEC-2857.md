## Context

An agent-scoped kill switch (the owner's, `flatten_and_pause`, the daily-loss flatten, the lifetime
floor) closes each instrument by selling the agent's sub-ledger, min(Lᵢ, N) (§5.5, §8.1). Rule 13
lets an `Unknown` order in the same instrument hold an exit, and the flatten is held by it in four
places:

1. **The close is not raised.** The executor raises `k-<switch>-<n>` only once nothing the switch
   reaches is live in the instrument (DEC-485 item 2), and an `Unknown` order is live.
2. **The gate holds it.** §5.3 rule 9 holds every new order in the instrument
   (`unknown_order_in_flight`) until the `Unknown` resolves.
3. **It waits for an opening.** An exit waits for the agent's own opening in the instrument, in any
   state the broker may hold it in (§5.3 rule 5), and goes to the gate after
   `unknown_absent_window_s`, where (2) holds it.
4. **It waits for protection.** §5.4's sequence waits until each protective order it cancels is
   confirmed gone, and a protective order whose placement went `Unknown` is never confirmed until it
   resolves.

None of these waits is bounded. The executor queries an `Unknown` order every
⌈`unknown_absent_window_s` ÷ (`unknown_absent_lookups` − 1)⌉ seconds without a cap. A broker with no
query by client order id (Robinhood, §5.7) resolves one only by list-and-match or by the owner's
hand (DEC-529 item 4). The owner is alerted once, `held_long`, after `max_intent_age_s`. Meanwhile
the agent's shares, which the switch was activated to sell, are not sold.

The account and workspace kill switch was never held: it uses the broker's cancel-all, `Unknown`
orders included, and close-position (§5.5).

## Invariants first

1. **Never short** (`AGENTS.md` rule 12). Whatever each `Unknown` order turns out to have done, the
   flatten's sells and the `Unknown` orders' sells together never exceed the long position, nor the
   agent's sub-ledger plus what its own `Unknown` buys fill.
2. **Never waits on an `Unknown`.** While the agent holds a quantity no `Unknown` order could
   still sell, the flatten sells it without waiting for any `Unknown` order to resolve.
3. **Ends flat.** Once every `Unknown` order in the instrument has resolved, and nothing else holds
   the flatten (its mode exemption, §5.4's cancels of confirmed orders, the session for an automated
   equity close, a limit it can be priced at), the agent's sub-ledger in the instrument is sold to
   zero, with no new approval.
4. **Every part is journaled with an idempotency key** (`AGENTS.md` rule 5). Each part is its own
   intent with its own id, journaled before it is sent; a replay raises the same parts.
5. **The owner still learns of a long wait.** A part still waiting on an `Unknown` past
   `max_intent_age_s` is journaled `held_long` and alerted once, with opaque ids only (rule 6).
6. **Nothing else changes.** The account and workspace kill switch, every other exit, every
   opening, and the gate's `unknown_order_in_flight` hold for anything but the certain part are
   unchanged.

## Decision

1. **Founder ruling.** An agent-scoped kill switch's flatten never waits on an `Unknown` order in
   the instrument. Rule 13's hold by an `Unknown` order no longer applies to the part of the
   flatten item 3 sizes. It still applies to every other exit and to the flatten's remainder
   (item 4), until the `Unknown` resolves.
2. **What an `Unknown` order could still sell.** For an `Unknown` sell, its quantity less what is
   recorded filled. Protective orders count too: a stop whose placement went `Unknown` may have
   triggered. Every `Unknown` sell in the instrument counts, whoever's it is.
   - Both legs of an `Unknown` OCO are counted, although at most one can fill. This sells less at
     once, never more, and item 4 sells the rest.
   - An `Unknown` buy could sell nothing. What it buys is the agent's to sell once it resolves
     (item 4).
3. **The certain part, `k-<switch>-<n>`,** is the switch's close as today (DEC-485 item 2), with
   qty the agent's sub-ledger. Each send of it, the first and every rung, sells at most the lower
   of two quantities:
   - the agent's sub-ledger, less every live non-protective sell the switch reaches (the agent's
     own and any `*` watchdog exit selling its lots, §2.3), less every `Unknown` sell of item 2
     among them;
   - the long position, less every live non-protective sell in the instrument, less every `Unknown`
     protective sell (item 2).

   This extends DEC-2360 item 1's cap, which is the sub-ledger alone. Neither quantity counts the
   part's own order. A send capped below its qty is journaled `rung_short` as today.

   The certain part is exempt from all four waits in the context, for `Unknown` orders only:
   - its close is raised while an `Unknown` order is live in the instrument;
   - the gate does not hold it `unknown_order_in_flight`;
   - it does not wait for an `Unknown` opening of the agent's;
   - §5.4's sequence does not wait for an `Unknown` protective order.

   It still waits, as today, for orders in other states: one still `Submitting`, a confirmed order's
   cancel, and §5.4's cancel of confirmed protection. Those are answered within a step or bounded by
   their own rules. When the cap is zero where the close would be raised, the certain part is not
   raised, and the remainder (item 4) carries the whole close.
4. **The remainder, `k-<switch>-<n>r`.** Whenever an `Unknown` order is in the instrument when the
   certain part would be raised (any side, anyone's, protective included), the executor raises a
   second intent in the same step.
   - Its qty is the agent's sub-ledger plus the unfilled quantity of the agent's own `Unknown` buys
     in the instrument, the most the agent can come to hold.
   - It is the switch's flatten in every other respect: the same purpose, floor, limit pricing,
     exemption from the agent's mode, and the same cap on each send as item 3.
   - The gate holds it `unknown_order_in_flight` as today, and §5.7's re-gating releases it, with no
     new approval, once no order in the instrument is `Unknown`.
   - Released, it sells what the cap leaves: the shares an `Unknown` sell did not sell, and the
     shares an `Unknown` buy did buy.
   - A close never has more than these two parts. A later `Unknown` in the instrument, the certain
     part's own included, keeps the remainder held, and its cap counts it.
5. **A send capped to zero.** A part whose cap (item 3) is zero when it would be sent sends
   nothing.
   - **If the agent's sub-ledger in the instrument is zero,** nothing of the agent's is left to
     sell, and the part is abandoned. The remainder is abandoned `OrderAbandoned` with the new reason
     `flatten_covered`, and the owner is not alerted: the `Unknown` sell or the certain part sold the
     shares, or a release since (DEC-2354) took them, and both are journaled. The certain part keeps
     DEC-2360 item 1's outcome: `released_to_owner`, with an `ExitStalled` alert.
   - **Otherwise** other sells hold what the agent has: the certain part still resting, or an
     `Unknown` sell. The part waits and is sent at the first pass whose cap is positive. If the
     certain part is cancelled or rejected unfilled, the remainder sells what it left.
   - `released_to_owner` keeps its meaning for every other exit: an exit that finds nothing left to
     sell beside a flatten is left to the owner, with an `ExitStalled` alert.
6. **The alert is kept.** `held_long` (the coordinator's ruling D2) and its one `ExitStalled` alert
   apply to the remainder unchanged: a remainder still held past `max_intent_age_s` is journaled
   `held_long` and alerted once, with opaque ids only. The certain part is never held by an
   `Unknown`, so it never raises that alert for one.
7. **Ids.** The certain part keeps `k-<switch>-<n>`. The remainder is `k-<switch>-<n>r`, a function
   of the switch and the close alone, so a replay or a retry derives the same id and the same client
   order id (`md-{intent}`, §2.3). Both are the switch's own flatten wherever the executor asks
   (DEC-485 item 15).
8. **Unchanged.** The account and workspace kill switch, every other exit and every opening, §5.3
   rule 9 for every other order, the sub-ledger, §5.4's cancels of confirmed orders, the automated
   switch's wait for the regular session (§5.5), and the switch's unpriced-close alert (DEC-2361).
9. **Tests first** (DEC-77). A tests PR follows this one, pending E7-4. It includes a property test:
   across random resolutions of random `Unknown` orders (buys and sells, the agent's and others',
   protective and not, each found filled, partly filled, cancelled or rejected, or confirmed absent,
   in any order), the agent's sub-ledger and the long position never fall below zero, and the
   sub-ledger ends at zero. Its oracle is a separate accumulator of the most each `Unknown` order
   could still sell and of the fills booked. It is shown to fail on three seeded bugs: a flatten
   that waits anyway, one that sells the full sub-ledger while a sell is `Unknown`, and one that
   raises no remainder after a buy resolves. The implementation PR follows with zero missed mutants
   and an independent review.

## Attack it

- **The `Unknown` sell had already filled.** The certain part sold only the held quantity less that
  sell's unfilled quantity, so the two together sell at most the position. If the certain part's
  send is still resting when the remainder is released, the remainder's cap counts it.
- **The `Unknown` order is a stop leg that fired.** Item 2 counts protective `Unknown` sells, so the
  stop and the certain part together sell at most the position.
- **A bad tick or a slow broker.** Neither changes what is counted; a slow resolution only delays
  the remainder, and `held_long` alerts the owner.
- **A malicious or careless owner sells by hand meanwhile.** The cap reads the long position at
  every send, so a send is never sized above what is held. An owner's sell that goes `Unknown`
  counts in the second bound.
- **Splitting to dodge a limit.** The parts are the kill switch's own sells; they reduce risk only,
  and their sum is capped by the sub-ledger.
- **A remainder released with nothing to sell is abandoned `flatten_covered`, not alerted.** It
  hides nothing: the record names the intent, and the fills that sold the shares are journaled.

## Consequences

- `AGENTS.md` rule 13 and trading spec §1's principle 4: an `Unknown` order holds every exit but
  the certain part of an agent-scoped kill switch's flatten.
- Trading spec v0.27: §5.3 rule 9 names the exception; §5.5's agent row and a new paragraph give
  the two parts; §5.7's rules point to them. (v0.26 is #2907's; whichever lands second renumbers.)
- Journal spec v0.114: §9.5's `OrderAbandoned` gains `flatten_covered`, with its reference vector,
  the reference validator's reason list, and its fixture. `mandate-journal`'s vector test pends the
  new case until the journal accepts the reason.
- Code (the pair after this one): `mandate-executor`'s `flatten_closes`, `flatten_room`, the gate's
  `unknown_order_in_flight`, `awaits_cancel`, `close_named`, and the remainder's abandon;
  `mandate-journal`'s reason list.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the hold and the alert.** Rejected by the founder: the agent's shares stay unsold, with no
  bound, while the switch says the agent is being closed.
- **Bound every exit's wait.** Rejected: an owner's or a discretionary exit can wait for the owner,
  and a bound that sells through an `Unknown` sell needs item 3's sizing anyway.
- **Escalate to cancel-all.** Rejected: an agent-scoped switch never uses cancel-all or
  close-position, since they touch other agents' and the owner's orders and shares (§5.5).
- **Sell the whole sub-ledger at once.** Rejected: an `Unknown` sell that already filled would
  leave the account short (rule 12).
- **Raise the remainder only once the `Unknown` resolves.** Rejected: nothing would be held, so
  nothing would be journaled `held_long` or alerted while the remainder waits.
- **Abandon a remainder with nothing to sell as `released_to_owner`.** Rejected: that reason means
  the shares were left to the owner and alerts the owner, and here they were sold.
