# DEC-482: On a phone, an open thread gets the screen

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-06 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | A Cursor agent, at the founder's request ("On phone, there is not much room to interact easily. The containers and stuff take up so much space that is becomes extremely difficult to do anything"), under DEC-79: a reversible interface decision that weakens no safety rule. The paper badge and Stop stay on screen (brief §5) |
| **Story or claim** | W4 web redesign (claim #297), amending [DEC-481](DEC-481.md) and, for an open thread on a phone, DEC-207 and [DEC-452](DEC-452.md)'s two-item header |

## Decision

1. **The thread's bar replaces the app header.** Below 64rem, while a thread (Chat or Desk) is
   open, the app header is hidden and the thread's own bar is the only chrome above the log. The bar
   is one row, about 56px: the back link to Messages, the agent's owl and name (which open the
   agent's page, in place of "Open agent"), its mode and mandate name under the name, a link to the
   other view (Desk from Chat, Chat from Desk, in place of the Chat and Desk tabs), and the paper
   badge. The header hides through CSS (`:has()` on the frame), so it hides only while a thread pane
   is on the page. An unknown agent, a workspace still loading, and the list of threads keep the
   header and its badge. From 64rem nothing changes.
2. **The paper badge and Stop are never hidden.** The badge moves with the header's job into the
   thread's bar; Stop stays at the end of the tab bar, and Home stays on it.
3. **The pinned request is 44px.** On a phone the waiting request pinned above the log is the order
   over the time it is skipped at, with the arrow. "If you do nothing" and "Review" are read out to
   screen readers and not drawn. The price and the time never truncate, down to 320px.
4. **The log is tighter on a phone.** Entries sit 16px apart (20px from 64rem), journal bubbles and
   request cards have less padding, a card's title is 16px, and the card's line "You approve or skip
   on the request itself" shows from 64rem only. The starter chips still go once something is asked.
5. **Only the reader stops the following.** The log stops following the latest entry only when the
   reader moves it up, by wheel, touch, key or scrollbar. A scroll position clamped by a frame resize
   (a phone's toolbar, a banner coming or going) or by shrinking content no longer counts as
   scrolling up (`useFollow` in `web/src/components/chat/follow.tsx`, amending DEC-481 item 2).

## Rationale

On a 390 × 844 phone the open thread spent about 280px on the app header, the thread's header, the
Chat and Desk tabs and the pinned request, which left the log 414px. On a 375 × 667 phone it left
192px. With one bar, the log has 553px and 376px, and 605px once the chips go. Messaging apps give an
open conversation one bar for the same reason. The app header's two jobs on a phone, the way home
and the paper badge, are covered by the Home tab and by the badge in the thread's bar.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the app header and compact only the thread's header:** saves about 80px, not 140px, and
  shows the paper badge twice in one column of chrome. Rejected.
- **Hide the header on scroll:** the badge would leave the screen while the owner reads, against
  brief §5. Rejected.
- **A segmented Chat | Desk control in the bar:** about 100px, which at 375px leaves the agent's name
  under 90px. Rejected for one link to the other view.
- **The pinned request on one line:** at 375px the order's price truncated. Rejected for two tight
  lines of the same 44px.
- **Hide the header by route in script:** an unknown agent's page would lose the badge. Rejected for
  the CSS rule that follows the thread pane itself.
