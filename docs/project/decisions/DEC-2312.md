## Context

Trading-domain spec §5.4 says of fractional positions: "only the whole-share part can be protected;
the fraction is unprotected and disclosed." §5.2's Alpaca table keeps fractional equity orders out
of OCO and bracket orders and makes them TIF day, and §5.3 rule 7 says the same. Three things are
unsaid:

- what a fractional equity entry carrying protective prices is sent as, on a profile that offers
  brackets;
- how much its protection covers;
- where the fraction is disclosed.

Today the executor sends such an entry as a bracket (`bracket_of`). The binding gate sees a
fractional bracket and refuses to decide it (DEC-129 item 27), so the entry is never sent. Journal
spec §9.5 closes `ProtectionChanged` whole at version 1, so no record can carry the fraction.

## Invariants first

- No opening is ever placed unprotected beyond the bounded interval §5.4 allows (`AGENTS.md`
  rule 2).
- A protective order never sells more than is held (`AGENTS.md` rule 12). It never carries a
  fractional quantity in a form the broker refuses for one (§5.2).
- What is unprotected is disclosed, never hidden (§5.4).
- A version-1 record replays exactly as before (journal spec §8).

## Decision

1. **A fractional equity entry goes as a plain DAY limit, never as a bracket.** "Fractional" means
   an equity quantity that is not a whole number of shares. This holds whatever protective prices
   the entry carries and whatever the profile offers; §5.2 allows no other form. The gate is shown
   the order it will actually be: a plain order, not a bracket. So §5.3 rule 8
   (`add_blocked_by_protective_order`) applies to a fractional add where protection rests, the
   conservative reading.
2. **Its protection follows its fill and covers the whole shares only.** As on a profile with no
   bracket (DEC-966), the interval starts at the entry's first fill. Once the entry is terminal,
   protection is placed for the whole-share part of what it leaves uncovered. A re-placement
   (`re_place`) covers the whole-share part of the held quantity. A position under one share has
   no whole share to protect: its interval ends with nothing placed, and the fraction is disclosed.
3. **`ProtectionChanged` version 2 discloses the fraction.**
   - It is version 1's members, then `unprotected_fraction` (`decimal?`), then `risk_clock`.
   - The value is the fraction of one share the position holds beyond its whole shares. It may be
     non-null only on `unprotected_start` and `placed`, and where non-null it is greater than 0 and
     less than 1 (journal spec rule 44).
   - The fold does not read it. Version 1 is never edited and stays registered.
   - The executor writes version 2 for every `ProtectionChanged` once its implementation lands,
     with the fraction null wherever nothing is disclosed.
4. **The order of the work.** First a spec change: journal v0.72, the reference implementation,
   and the `protection_fraction` vector section. Then the executor's DEC-77 pair:
   - `mandate-journal` registers version 2;
   - the executor writes it, sends fractional entries plain, and sizes protection to whole shares;
   - a test pins that `bracket_of` never builds a bracket for a fractional quantity.

## Consequences

- A fractional entry is no longer refused at the gate; it is sent, and its whole shares are
  protected once it fills.
- The owner sees the unprotected fraction on the records that start and place protection, not only
  in the position.
- Journal v0.72 is numbered after lane L1's v0.71 and is merged after it, with no rule renumbered.

## Alternatives considered

- **Send the whole shares as a bracket and the fraction as a separate plain order.** Rejected: two
  orders for one intent, a second idempotency key, and a partial fill of either leaves the
  position's protection out of step with what was asked.
- **Refuse fractional entries that carry protective prices.** Rejected: §5.4 already says how such
  a position is protected, and refusing would make §5.2's fractional support unusable for
  protected entries.
- **Disclose the fraction on an existing record, such as an alert.** Rejected: `expiry_unreplaceable`
  is an alert about a shortfall, and nothing else carries a quantity. The disclosure belongs on the
  record that says what is protected.
