## Decision

1. **Messages is a screen** (`/messages`): one thread per agent. On a desktop it has three columns:
   the threads, the open thread, and a rail beside it. On a phone it shows one at a time. Threads
   with a request waiting come first, under "Needs you", by the soonest deadline; the rest follow
   under "Earlier", by their latest entry. A row shows the agent's owl and opaque label, the time of
   its latest entry, one code-written line about it, the count of waiting requests, and its mode
   when not normal. A row never quotes model output.
2. **A thread is the agent's journal**, oldest first and grouped by day: fills, protection, mode
   changes, gate refusals and requests, each at the time it was recorded and in code's words
   (`threadItems`, `web/src/lib/messages.ts`). A request is a card with the order, its value against
   the order limit, its purpose, the rule that sent it and its deadline. The card links to the
   request (D6). While a request waits, it is also pinned above the thread with the static time it
   is skipped at.
3. **Chat and Desk are pages** (`/messages/<agent>` and `/messages/<agent>/desk`), route tabs like
   every other screen's. The desk lays out the agent's latest request, step by step, as journaled:
   the signal models, with their output quoted in a dashed frame and named by model and version;
   the order builder; the risk gate, with each figure as a distance to its limit; the owner's rule
   that sent the request; and the owner. A step whose fact is not recorded says so. With no request,
   the desk shows the latest gate decision.
4. **Asking the record.** The composer under a thread takes the owner's words. Code matches them to
   a fixed set of questions: what needs me, why it asked, why it was refused, how close it is to
   its limits, what it holds, what happened today and its status. Each is answered from the
   journal and the mandate (`interpret`, `web/src/lib/ask-record.ts`). An answer is labelled "From
   the record", links each record it read, and offers plain next questions. Limits are stated as
   headroom and caps, never as a result, so no answer carries P&L. Words that ask for advice or an
   order get a refusal that says why. No model writes an answer. Nothing asked is journaled or
   kept: conversations live in memory for the page's life, not in browser storage.
5. **Acting from a message lowers risk or hands off.**
   - Pause: the owner's words show a card with Pause and Not now. Nothing is sent until Pause is
     pressed. It needs no passkey and is never disabled. Its entry then appears in the thread like
     any other.
   - Stop, resume, kill switches and closing positions need a passkey and the list of what they
     touch, so the reply opens the Stop sheet.
   - Asking for a new agent hands the words to setup (A0), where they wait in the field, unsent.
   - Nothing else acts.
6. **The phone's tabs become Home, Messages, Agents and More** (amending DEC-207 item 2, from the
   approved phone mockup). The count of waiting requests moves to Messages. Approvals moves under
   More, and each request is still one tap away from Home's Needs you and from its thread. The
   desktop dock adds Messages after Home and keeps Approvals with its count.
7. **The rail** beside a thread shows the agent, its mode, its headroom (the same rows as the
   agent's page), what sends the owner a request, Pause, and Open agent.
8. **The Owlhead copilot** asks the same record from anywhere: "Ask Owlhead" in the header, or
   ⌘J / Ctrl+J. It opens as a panel docked to the right on a desktop and a sheet on a phone. It
   says which screen it is looking at, scopes its answers to the agent on screen, and gives the
   same answers and cards as a thread (items 4 and 5). It keeps no history after it closes.

## Rationale

The founder asked for one place to talk to the agents and a copilot that can do everything. The
brief's D14 already gives the thread its rules: no button in an agent's voice, answers assembled
from the journal with links, and nothing placed from a message. Doing everything from a message
safely means doing it through the screens that already carry the step-up and the full disclosure;
the copilot answers and routes. A deterministic matcher, not a model, writes the answers, because
the platform has no model in the web app's fixtures and an answer about money must be exactly what
the record says.

## Alternatives considered

- **A tablist for Chat and Desk:** rejected. Route tabs are links, so each view has an address and
  nothing hides behind a tab (the PageHeader convention).
- **A model writing replies in the agent's voice**, as the mockup shows ("I'd like to buy"): deferred
  to [DEC-480](DEC-480.md). Replies come from the record until the founder decides.
- **Keeping Approvals as a phone tab and adding Messages under More:** rejected. The mockup puts
  Messages on the bar, and a request opened from a thread is the same request.
- **Saving conversations:** rejected. Nothing the owner types is a record, and the app writes nothing
  to browser storage.
