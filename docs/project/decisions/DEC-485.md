## Decision

Trading-domain spec §5.5 gives the agent-scoped sequence: the final mode first, then cancel, then
confirm, then close, each step journaled; only the agent's orders (and an unattributed protective
leg or `*` watchdog exit in an instrument it closes), each by `client_order_id`; exactly the
agent's sub-ledger quantity; never `cancel-all` or `close-position`. These are the readings the
implementation takes where §5.5, §5.6 and §2.3 are silent.

1. **This slice is the agent scope.** `Command::KillSwitch` with the account or workspace scope,
   the only callers of `cancel-all` and `close-position`, answers `Unimplemented { story: "E7-4" }`
   before anything is journaled, and so does a `KillSwitchActivated` whose `scope` is anything but
   `agent` (absent included) at the fold, so the catalogue property's stub rule holds for the
   event until that slice interprets it whole. `BrokerOutcome::AccountWideAccepted` keeps its stub.
   Those scopes need the orders `close-position` creates recognised as the account's own rather
   than external activity (§7.1, §11), which is a slice of its own under ES-13. No code path builds
   an `AccountWideScope`, so `BrokerRequest::CancelAll` and `ClosePosition` stay unreachable.
2. **The flatten is the executor's own intent**, raised through `received` like the watchdog's
   exit (DEC-160 (11)), one per instrument: `k-<switch>-<n>`, where `<switch>` is the journaled
   `KillSwitchActivated` record's id and `<n>` the instrument's ordinal among the record's
   `closes` then `deferred`. A restart derives the same id, and `ClientOrderId::for_intent`
   accepts the `k-` prefix with the watchdog's grammar, so the order is `md-k-<switch>-<n>`. Its
   purpose is `Initiator::sell_purpose`: `risk_exit` for a mandate limit or an operator,
   `owner_exit` for the owner (§5.5), so the owner's sells keep the participation caps rule 13
   leaves them.
3. **`KillSwitchActivated` on the account stream** (journal spec §9.5 leaves the account stream's
   members open: "kill-switch scope, initiator, orders canceled, sells planned or deferred")
   carries `scope` (`agent`), `subject`, `initiator`, `purpose`, `cancels` (the ids it asks
   cancelled), `closes` (the instruments it sells once their cancels confirm), `deferred` (the
   equities an automated switch sells at the regular session), and the owner's confirmed `floor`,
   `bid` and `bid_size` or `null`, with `risk_clock`. Its `causation_id` is the `AgentModeApplied`
   it follows. The fold keeps each switch (`ExecutorState::switches`: agent, purpose, floor, the
   named instruments, and those not yet raised); a close leaves the pending set when its flatten's
   `IntentReceived` folds, so a replay raises nothing twice. The owner is alerted once, with the
   record's id and the key `kill_switch` (rule 6).
4. **The mode comes first and is originated**: `AgentModeApplied { agent, to, restriction:
   "kill_switch", originated: true }`, `paused` for a mandate limit and `stopped` for the owner or
   an operator (`Initiator::final_mode`, DEC-100), journaled before the switch, every cancel and
   every sell (planted bug 13). An intent handled after the switch is gated under it.
5. **What is cancelled**: the non-protective orders the switch reaches, in an instrument it
   closes (the agent's own, and a `*` watchdog exit), that the broker holds accepted or partly
   filled with no cancel outstanding, each by its own id. Every instrument the agent has a live
   non-protective order in is a close (item 7), so this is every such order of the agent's. A
   switch's own flatten is never reached, by this switch or by a later one: it is what the
   switches sell through (#668 round 1, blocker 1). After the switch's own step, the per-step
   cancel covers only the closes still pending, so a raised close's instrument is left to its
   flatten. One still `Submitting` is cancelled at the first step after the broker acknowledges
   it; an `Unknown` one is never
   cancelled blind (the gate holds the flatten on it, rule 13's hold). Protective orders are left
   to the flatten's own §5.4 sequence (cancel every resting protective order, attributed or not,
   DEC-160 (3)(d)), so an automated equity close deferred to the session leaves its protection
   resting until then, as §5.5 says.
6. **The close waits for those cancels.** A flatten is raised only once nothing the switch reaches
   is live in the instrument: raised earlier, the gate would count the sells still pending their
   cancel against the position and deny it `sell_exceeds_available`, which is terminal. Where
   nothing needs cancelling it is raised in the switch's own step, so RC-14's `cancel_all_orders`,
   `await_cancel_confirmed`, `close_all_positions` runs through §5.4's sequence: cancel the
   protection, confirm, re-gate, submit.
7. **Exactly the sub-ledger**: the agent's attributed lots in the instrument, its own orders'
   fills, buys net of sells (§8.1), capped at the long position and floored at zero, read when the
   flatten is raised. An instrument with lots or a live non-protective order of the agent's is a
   close; one with nothing of the agent's left once its cancels confirm raises nothing yet. A
   close leaves the pending set by exactly two journaled records (#668 round 1, minor 1): its own
   flatten's `IntentReceived`, and an `AgentModeApplied` to `normal` naming the `kill_switch`
   restriction, which lifts every close the agent's switches had not raised, so nothing sells a
   resumed agent's lots later. A `normal` for any other restriction leaves them pending. A close
   with nothing to sell stays pending while the restriction stands, on purpose: a late fill of
   an order the switch cancelled (`LateFillApplied`) gives the agent lots there again, and the
   close then raises its flatten for them. Pending, it costs one sub-ledger read per step and
   sells nothing.
8. **The flatten's own limit**, which §5.6 step 4 falls back to and which the ladder never
   undercuts: the lowest of the latest sane quote's mark and bid and the last sane bid's mark and
   bid, exactly as the watchdog's exit takes it (DEC-260 (11); one helper, `lowest_sane`, now
   serves both). With no sane quote, the resting protection's stop price in the instrument (#668
   round 1, major 2), a DEC-176 reading that adds no risk: it is a price the position was already
   set to be sold at, a limit sell there never sells below what the owner accepted, and RC-14's
   `kill_switch` variant, which observes no quote, needs it. Either is raised to the owner's
   confirmed floor, which stands alone when neither exists. With none of the three the close is
   not raised yet: no limit can be named, so it waits, pending, for the next sane quote. The limit
   is only the fallback: once raised, the ladder prices the flatten from the reference bid as for
   every exit (§5.6 step 1), and §5.6 step 4 goes at this limit only with nothing to price from.
9. **No market order.** §5.5 allows one "only in the regular session outside auction windows with
   current status data". The executor reads no trading-status feed yet (§4.4; backlog), so that
   condition never holds and every close goes through §5.6's ladder, as the spec routes it
   "otherwise". When the status feed lands, sending a market order where §5.5 permits one is a new
   decision.
10. **Deferral** (§5.5: "automated kill switches sell equities only in the regular session"): an
    automated (`risk_exit`) equity close is journaled in `deferred` and raised at the first step
    inside the regular session or its closing auction window; crypto goes at once. An owner's
    close is never deferred by the switch: it is raised at once and gated like any owner exit
    (DEC-260 (13): outside the session it is queued for the session or held `session_closed`).
11. **The owner's floor on the live paths** ([#373](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/373)
    round 1, minor 1). `exit_limit` and `next_rung` pass a flatten's confirmed floor to §5.6, and
    a rung the floor clamps is `at_floor`, so it rests and alerts rather than being cancelled and
    resubmitted at the same price every `exit_step_s`. The confirmed bid as the ladder's reference
    outside the regular session, and with it `extended_hours`, is the session slice's: the hand
    suite's clock is read as the regular session (backlog, the clock-0 item), and
    `hand::an_owner_exit_outside_the_session_prices_from_the_confirmed_bid` stays pending for it.
12. **The mode exemption** (§5.5, DEC-260 (3), #373 round 1, major 1). An intent is a flatten only
    when its `k-` id names a switch the fold carries (`kill::is_flatten`), so an intent that merely
    looks like one gets no exemption. A flatten is held only by the account's own state
    (`ExecutorState::account_mode`, rule 13's broker hold), never by the agent's or `*`'s mode;
    the gate's `mode_failure` applies the exemption only to an order that adds no risk.
    `climbs`, `remainders` and `lone_steps` read the same predicate, `kill::mode_holds`, so a
    flatten's ladder keeps stepping while the agent is paused or stopped and its step's
    confirmation does not end the sequence. Every other exit is held by the agent's effective mode
    as before.
13. **The shares a cancelled `*` exit covered** (the #648 and #655 reviews). A watchdog exit of no
    agent is sized to the whole covered position; the switch cancels it by id and sells only the
    agent's lots. Once the cancel is confirmed the watchdog sequence's exit is terminal, and as soon
    as no exit is working or waiting in the instrument (the switch's sell filled, cancelled or
    ended any other way) `settle` re-places the sequence's protection, per §5.4, for exactly what
    is held less what protective orders still cover: in the hand case, the ten nobody attributed.
    The re-placed OCO is the watchdog sequence's: its agent is the sequence's, `*`, and its prices
    are the ones the sequence recorded from the latest `placed` (stop 140, take-profit 170 in the
    case), named for the sequence's entry: the cancelled OCO named no entry, so the entry is the
    watchdog exit's own order, and the re-placement is `md-w-<record>-p<placement>` (§2.3). Should the switch's sell outlast `max_unprotected_s`, the
    bound ends it like any exit inside an interval (DEC-160 (19)): protection returns for
    everything held, the agent's lots included, and the owner is alerted
    `unprotected_interval_limit`. The position is then protected and the owner told, which adds
    no risk; raising the close again after a bound is a backlog item.
14. **Ladder remainders beside a flatten** (§5.5, DEC-410 item 5). The agent is paused or stopped
    first, so its parked lone ladders count nothing; the flatten goes whole and any other
    remainder counts only what it leaves. Nothing new is written for this: it falls out of item 12
    and DEC-410's derived count, and `protection::sequence_tests::a_kill_switch_between_rungs_never_over_sells`
    (pending E7-3 for the switch's stub) now runs live.
15. **Known gap, shared with the watchdog.** Journal spec §9.5's `IntentReceived` types
    `intent_id` as a ULID, which neither `w-<record>` nor `k-<switch>-<n>` is; the executor's own
    intents need their own registered form (backlog). A handed-over intent whose id starts `k-`
    or `w-` is refused at `Input::Intent` (`malformed_client_order_id`) before anything is
    journaled (#668 round 1, major 1): only the executor raises those ids. An intent is a switch's
    flatten only when its id names a journaled switch's close and it is that close's agent's sell
    of that close's instrument; only such an `IntentReceived` takes the close out of the pending
    set, and only such an intent has the mode exemption, the owner's floor, or stops the watchdog
    firing beside it (`protection::watching`, #668 round 1, minor 3).
16. **Where in the step the closes are raised.** `settle` raises them first, so a flatten that
    waits on a `*` exit's cancel is already waiting when that cancel confirms, and holds the
    watchdog sequence's re-placement until the agent's lots are sold (item 13); raised after the
    re-placement, it would place an OCO for the whole position and cancel it again at once. A
    bracket entry of the agent's with fills whose legs are not yet recorded `placed` counts as
    working for the close, so the flatten waits one step for them and then cancels them through
    its sequence before it sells: raised in the step the entry completes, it would sell beside
    legs the broker has just activated (rule 12).
17. **The pending tests the switch's stub held back** (DEC-77). Every property script that drew a
    kill switch died at its stub, so every executor property marked pending could only fail
    there. With the switch live they run to their own verdicts. Those that now pass have their
    `#[ignore]` lines deleted in this PR, as DEC-77's implementation PR does for every test its
    code makes pass. Those that pass vacuously or fail away from any stub on behaviour older than
    the switch stay pending, and the backlog names each one and what it needs: the `risk_clock`
    readers (#456 round 1), the `intent_id` the order id property reads (DEC-446 rule 45), the
    rule-5 carve-out (#286 round 1, minor 2), and five oracles whose minimal scripts have no kill
    switch in them. None of them is weakened or edited here.
18. **A second switch while the first's close is in flight** (#668 round 1, minor 2) applies its
    mode, journals its record and cancels what the first did not reach, never the first's
    flatten (item 5). Its own close in that instrument counts the agent's working flatten, sent or
    received and not yet sent, as working, so it waits rather than raise a second sell of the
    same lots. If the first flatten fills, the second close has nothing left to sell; if it ends
    unsold (rejected, abandoned, or ended by the interval's bound), the second close raises its
    own flatten for what the agent still holds.
19. **In-module pins** (`kill::tests`, under `#[cfg(test)]`, outside ES-13's count): the record's
    members for each initiator, the deferral and its release at the session for an automated
    equity close (and none for an owner's or a crypto close), which restriction's lift drops the
    pending closes, what the switch reaches and that an empty close raises nothing, the wait for
    a completed bracket's legs (and none for an entry cancelled unfilled), and the `k-` id
    grammar, the account state as the mode a flatten answers to, and the sub-ledger beside another
    agent's lots. #668 round 1 added five, each shown failing on the code it reviewed: the
    switch's own acknowledged flatten never cancelled beside a close still waiting on an opening,
    nor beside an equity close deferred to the session, nor by a second switch (which raises no
    second close beside it); an intent handed over under a `k-` or `w-` id refused with the state
    unchanged and the deferred close still raised at the open; and a journaled `IntentReceived`
    under a switch's id whose agent or instrument is not that close's neither a flatten nor the
    end of the close. Each pins a branch the suites in `tests/` do not reach.

## Rationale

Each reading reduces risk at once and leaves nothing silent: the mode before anything else, every
cancel by id, the close only once the cancels it depends on are confirmed (so it is never denied
against its own agent's pending sells), a limit someone saw or the owner confirmed, a deferred close
journaled and raised from folded state, and re-protection by the sequence machinery that already
owns the interval. A loud stub stands where the account-wide endpoints would start.

## Alternatives considered

- **The flatten's sells with `Purpose::Flatten`.** Rejected: §5.5 names them `risk_exit` and
  `owner_exit`, and `Flatten` is exempt from every pacing control where an owner exit keeps the
  participation caps.
- **Raise the flatten at the switch and let it wait on the cancels through `awaits_cancel`.**
  Rejected: the gate runs before that wait and would deny the flatten against the agent's own
  pending-cancel sells.
- **Exempt any `k-`-prefixed intent from the mode.** Rejected: an intent handed over with such an
  id would escape a stop. The exemption needs a journaled switch and an order that adds no risk.
- **Clear a switch's closes on any `normal`.** Rejected: lifting an unrelated restriction would
  silently drop a kill switch's flatten.
- **Deliver the account scope as a `cancel-all` effect without the fold's half.** Rejected: an
  account scope whose `close-position` orders are reconciled as external activity would pause
  every agent the switch meant to flatten.
- **Exempt the switch's sell from the interval's bound.** Rejected: the bound keeps every
  unprotected interval finite (interpretation 21), and ending the sell there leaves the position
  protected and the owner told.
