# DEC-516: Two lanes: the safety lane keeps every gate, the light lane merges on green CI and pictures

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Proposed: the founder asked for a faster process on 2026-10-08 ("the process has become the bottleneck"); items 2 to 6 are process and take effect now under DEC-79; item 1's light-lane merge rule waits for the founder's yes |
| **Date** | 2026-10-08 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | the agent, on the founder's direction, after the design plan's first fifteen changes shipped as one PR (#669) |
| **Story or claim** | `web/design/plan.md` section I; the tracker's Next, item 1 |

## What slowed #669 down

Fifteen plan items went out as one PR of 65 files because the session was pinned to one branch.
Each item was verified against 4,576 tests and a production build, several times, because the
suite pins the look as much as the behaviour; the decision numbers collided with `main` and cost an
hour to renumber; the tracker had to be read through 7,800-character lines to find the order of
work; and nobody could judge a screen change without reading its diff. None of that time built
anything. The safety gates (DEC-77, DEC-79, DEC-175) caught nothing on this PR because it touched
no safety-critical path; they cost the same as if it had.

## Decision

1. **Two lanes, by what a change touches.**
   - **The safety lane** is unchanged: every path AGENTS.md lists as safety-critical, `docs/specs/`,
     the schemas, the reference cases, CI, xtask, the workflows, `CODEOWNERS` and
     `xtask/layers.toml`, and in `web/` the safety surface (the Stop control and sheet, Approve and
     Skip and the approval flow, the kill-switch and release records, the step-up, notification
     payloads, sign-in). The DEC-77 sequence, an independent review on a different model, zero
     missed mutants and DEC-175's approval line stay as they are.
   - **The light lane** is everything else DEC-79 lets an agent decide: `web/` outside that surface,
     fixtures, documents, the design files, tooling that is not a gate. A light-lane PR merges when
     CI is green on its head and its description carries the author's checks and, for a screen
     change, the pictures (item 3). A review agent reads the diff **after** the merge, within a day,
     and posts findings as backlog rows or a fix PR; a finding that touches the safety surface or a
     non-negotiable reverts the merge. The founder reviews from the pictures and can revert
     (DEC-79). The light lane keeps the DEC rule: a decision file for a change of rule, before the
     change.
2. **One branch, one PR, one item.** A PR is one tick in a plan or one story, named in its title,
   small enough to read in a sitting; never two items. A session pinned to one branch restarts it
   from `main` after each merge rather than stacking the next item on merged history.
3. **Review from pictures.** `npm run shots` in `web/` captures every listed screen on the fixture
   scenarios at phone and desktop widths in both themes, and writes a contact sheet with the golden
   path first (sign in, Home with a request, the request, an agent, its decisions, the mandate,
   Stop). A light-lane PR that changes a screen attaches the sheet's images, before and after; the
   weekly critique the plan calls for uses the same output; the founder judges from them.
4. **Tests pin invariants** (DEC-511 item 5). The sort of the existing suite is the next light-lane
   PR, and a review refuses a new test that only notices the look changed. A light-lane PR runs the
   tests of the areas it touched and the type check; the full suite and the build run in CI, once.
5. **A session leaves one row and one paragraph.** The tracker's milestone row it touched and its
   Next list; one dated paragraph in the work log; the rest is the PR body and the decision file.
   The tracker stays one page; the Next list holds at most six items.
6. **Numbers.** Before taking a decision number a session fetches `main` and every `refs/heads`
   branch and takes the next integer after the highest in both, reserving it with the file in its
   first commit (DEC-344 step 2). Two sessions still colliding renumber the younger PR.
7. **What is measured.** For the light lane, the time from a PR's first commit to its merge,
   target under two hours; for the whole repository, the share of a session's commits that are
   fixes to tests that pinned the look, target zero. The tracker's Next list never grows past six.

## What does not change

Nothing in AGENTS.md's non-negotiables or safety-critical list. Live money, spending, legal text and
weakening a safety rule stay the founder's (DEC-79). Protected paths still need a DEC and ship
without code. A safety-critical change still takes tests first, zero missed mutants, the
cross-model review and the approval line, whatever lane the rest of a story runs in.

## Rationale

The gates exist to stop a mandate being breached, money being lost, or a record being wrong. A
mode's name, a chip's shape, a sparkline's range and a document's layout can do none of those, and
they are most of what the product needs now. Paying the full price for them bought nothing on #669
and would have cost the same on every one of the thirty-odd items still in the plan. Moving the
review after the merge on the light lane keeps the review (it still reads every diff) and keeps the
founder's revert; it removes the wait.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep one lane and shrink the PRs.** Smaller PRs help (item 2) but each still waits a review
  round for a change no gate can fail; the wait, not the size, was the bottleneck.
- **Drop the review on the light lane.** Rejected: a second reading catches what the author cannot
  see, and it costs nothing when it runs after the merge.
- **Let the light lane skip CI.** Rejected: green CI is the floor on every lane.

## Revisit when

The light lane's post-merge review reverts a merge for a safety-surface finding (the lane's
boundary was drawn wrong), or the measures in item 7 are missed for a month.
