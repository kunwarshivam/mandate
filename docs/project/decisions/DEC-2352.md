# DEC-2352: A held retirement is journaled every 24 hours, and the owner and operator are told

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted (agent: an engineering reading under DEC-79 and DEC-176 that gives DEC-2344 item 2's `retirement_held` alert a committed cause, approved by the coordinator on 2026-10-22; it adds a record and a notice, delays nothing, and changes no trading rule) |
| **Date** | 2026-10-22 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | `claude-code` (lane D1, the staging push), under DEC-176 |
| **Story or claim** | E8-17, E6-5, E21-33; journal spec §9.34 (v0.79); notifications spec §3.2 (v0.10); infrastructure design §8.2; DEC-2344 item 2; DEC-2351; claim #1901 |

## Context

DEC-2344 item 2 has a runtime retire an agent that ran only once the account stream's fold shows
no position attributed to it and no working order of its own, and says that a retirement that has
waited 24 hours raises the opaque alert `retirement_held` to the owner and the operator, and again
each 24 hours while it waits. A notice answers one committed cause, written by its stream's owner
in the cause's batch (notifications spec §3.2, §3.4; journal spec rule 130), and nothing records
that a retirement has waited (DEC-2351 item 5).

## Decision

1. **`RetirementHeld`** (agent stream, schema version 1, by the runtime): `held_since` (the risk
   clock at which the retirement began waiting), `period` (1, 2, ...), `waiting_on` (`position`,
   `working_order`, or both, in that order), and `risk_clock`; `config_refs` names the
   `mandate_version`. Its `causation_id` is the record that began the retirement: the owner's Stop,
   a `profit_stop` limit, a goal's `release`, or a passed `end_date`. It names no order, position,
   instrument, or quantity.
2. **Rule 264 (the record):** the runtime's own (`agent` actor), caused, a period from 1, journaled
   no sooner than `period` × 24 hours of risk clock after `held_since`, and `waiting_on` non-empty
   with each value once, in order.
3. **Rule 265 (the agent stream):** for each cause, its first `RetirementHeld` has period 1, each
   later one has the next period, and all carry the first one's `held_since`. Records between them
   are not read. The reference fold and an independent oracle judge it, and each check is shown
   catching its seeded bug.
4. **The writer.** The runtime writes period n at the first risk tick at or after `held_since` + n
   × 24 hours while the agent still holds something of its own, with `OwnerAlertSent`
   (`retirement_held`, subject this record) in the same batch, and none once its `AgentStopped`
   is journaled. A runtime restart folds its stream and writes no period twice.
5. **The kind `retirement_held`** (notifications spec §3.2): `safety`, `attention_needed`, the
   agent's owners. One notice per period (journal §9.15's closed set, 34 kinds to 35).
6. **The operator** sees it as infrastructure design §8.2's `retirement_held` row, SEV-3, from the
   same record, with RB-04 for a held `Unknown` order and the owner's release otherwise.
7. **Owners of the code.** The journal crate's parser and stream rule, the kind in `mandate-notify`
   and `mandate-journal`, and the runtime's writer are E8-17's and E6-5's rows; this decision is the
   spec alone (ES-22).

## Rationale

A daily record makes the alert's cause committed and replayable, so a restart neither drops nor
repeats it, and the rules let a verifier see a writer that skips or repeats a day. It carries only
what kind of thing is outstanding, never what it is, so the notice and the record stay opaque.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hang the alert on a risk tick.** Rejected: ticks are the scheduler's stream, and an
  `OwnerAlertSent` is written by its subject's stream owner in the subject's batch.
- **One record when the wait starts, and the dispatcher counts days.** Rejected: the dispatcher
  would raise alerts from time alone with no committed cause each day (NT-5).

## Risks

- Until E6-5's writer lands, no `RetirementHeld` is written; until then the only `AgentStopped`
  writer retires an agent that holds nothing (DEC-2344 risks).
- A runtime that is down writes no period; the agent's owner then has `agent_held` or the crash
  loop's alerts, and the next period after it returns is the one due, not the ones missed.
