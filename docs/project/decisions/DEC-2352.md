# DEC-2352: A held retirement is journaled every 24 hours, and the owner and operator are told

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted (agent: an engineering reading under DEC-79 and DEC-176 that gives DEC-2344 item 2's `retirement_held` alert a committed cause, approved by the coordinator on 2026-10-22; it adds a record and a notice, delays nothing, and changes no trading rule) |
| **Date** | 2026-10-22 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | `claude-code` (lane D1, the staging push), under DEC-176 |
| **Story or claim** | E8-17, E6-5, E21-33; journal spec §9.34 (v0.79); notifications spec §3.2 (v0.10); infrastructure design §8.2; DEC-2344 item 2; DEC-2351; claim #1901 |

## Context

DEC-2344 item 2 has a runtime retire an agent that ran only once the account stream's fold shows
no position attributed to it and no working order of its own, and says that a retirement that has
waited 24 hours raises the opaque alert `retirement_held` to the owner and the operator, and again
each 24 hours while it waits. A notice answers one committed cause, written by its stream's owner
in the cause's batch (notifications spec §3.2, §3.4; journal spec rule 130), and nothing records
that a retirement has waited (DEC-2351 item 6).

## Decision

1. **`RetirementHeld`** (agent stream, schema version 1, by the runtime): `held_since` (the cause
   record's own risk clock), `period` (its place in the sequence, from 1), `waiting_on`
   (`position`, `working_order`, or both, in that order), and `risk_clock`; `config_refs` names
   the `mandate_version`. Its `causation_id` is the record that began the retirement: the owner's
   Stop, a `profit_stop` limit, a goal's `release`, or a passed `end_date`. It names no order,
   position, instrument, or quantity.
2. **Rule 265 (the record):** the runtime's own (`agent` actor, never `system` or `user`), caused,
   a period from 1, journaled no sooner than 24 hours of risk clock after `held_since`, and
   `waiting_on` non-empty with each value once, in order.
3. **Rule 266 (the stream):** each record with its `OwnerAlertSent` as the next row of its batch;
   one sequence per agent, so a second cause while one is open starts nothing and the first
   continues; periods 1, 2, ... under the first one's `held_since`, each at least 24 hours of risk
   clock after the one before, under the version in effect; and none of a cause after its sequence
   ends. The reference fold and an independent oracle, which reads the ends itself, judge it, and
   each check is shown catching its seeded bug.
4. **The sequence ends with the retirement**, at records the verifier already reads, all of this
   agent's own, so none is added: its `AgentStopped`, or an owner's `resume` or `stop` scoped to it.
   A version applied to it ends nothing by itself, since a version may leave the agent retiring and
   the owner must keep hearing of it: the next record goes on under the new version, or begins a
   new cause from 1 if the version cancelled that retirement and the agent retires again. A
   rejected version, another agent's records, and any other command change nothing. A version
   cancels the retirement when, from replayable records alone, its cause no longer holds: a new
   `profit_level` above the return at the `profit_stop` trigger, (`agent_equity` −
   `capital_base`) / `capital_base` from its `RiskLimitTriggered`; or, against the version the
   sequence's first record names in `config_refs.mandate_version`, a later `end_date` or a changed
   `on_complete`. Never from the agent's current return; one that does not cancel leaves the
   runtime writing. An applied version may thus begin a new
   cause from 1: extra alerts, never silence. An owner's Stop ends the open sequence and begins its
   own, so repeated Stops each begin one; the owner is engaged. A redeploy is a new agent id on its
   own stream (DEC-1173), so nothing carries over to it. An open sequence silent for more than 48
   hours of risk clock with no end and no cancelling version since is a verifier finding,
   `retirement_held_silent`, not an append rule, so a cancelled retirement raises nothing.
5. **The writer.** The runtime writes the next period at its first risk tick at least 24 hours
   after the last one (the first, after `held_since`), while the agent still holds something of its
   own, in every agent mode, `paused` and `stopped` included. Periods count records: a runtime that
   was down writes the next one when it is back and the one after a day later, never the missed
   ones in a burst. It writes none once a version cancelled the retirement, and a restart folds the
   stream first and writes no period twice.
6. **The kind `retirement_held`** (notifications spec §3.2): `safety`, `attention_needed`, the
   agent's owners. One notice per period (journal §9.15's closed set, 34 kinds to 35).
7. **The operator** sees infrastructure design §8.2's `retirement_held` row, SEV-3, raised by the
   agent runtime from the same record, with RB-04 for a held `Unknown` order and the owner's
   release otherwise.
8. **Owners of the code.** The journal crate's parser and rules, the kind in `mandate-notify` and
   `mandate-journal` (lane D1, when the coordinator says), and the runtime's writer (lane LV) are
   E8-17's and E6-5's rows; this decision is the spec alone (ES-22).

## Rationale

A daily record makes the alert's cause committed and replayable, so a restart neither drops nor
repeats it, and the rules let a verifier see a writer that skips or repeats a day. It carries only
what kind of thing is outstanding, never what it is, so the notice and the record stay opaque.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hang the alert on a risk tick.** Rejected: ticks are the scheduler's stream, and an
  `OwnerAlertSent` is written by its subject's stream owner in the subject's batch.
- **One record when the wait starts, and the dispatcher counts days.** Rejected: the dispatcher
  would raise alerts from time alone with no committed cause each day (NT-5).

## Risks

- Until E6-5's writer lands, no `RetirementHeld` is written; until then the only `AgentStopped`
  writer retires an agent that holds nothing (DEC-2344 risks).
- A runtime that is down writes no period; the agent's owner then has `agent_held` or the crash
  loop's alerts, and when it returns it writes the next period in the sequence, so a period's
  number is its place in the sequence, not the days waited; its `risk_clock` says when.
