## Decision

1. **The first enterprise buyers.** The harness is sold first to small systematic and prop
   trading teams (strategy segment E) and to emerging managers and small advisers (segment D).
   Broker-dealers and API brokers adding agent access (segment F) come second. Banks and large asset
   managers come later. This settles the item DEC-149 left open for discovery. Discovery still has
   to confirm segment E, for which the repo holds no direct evidence, before any engineering beyond
   Stage A changes for it.
2. **The enterprise positioning line.** Internally and in sales material, the harness is "the
   pre-trade gate and the record for AI agents that touch real orders". Any agent may propose;
   Mandate decides from account state, journals the decision before acting, and hands approved
   orders to the firm's own broker or order system. Mandate does not sell an order management
   system, routing, low-latency execution, or a trading agent of its own to enterprises. No public
   text may claim that Mandate is compliant with, certified under, or approved by any rule or
   regulator (SEC Rule 15c3-5, FINRA, SOC 2, or any other) until the founder approves that wording
   with counsel (DEC-79).
3. **Two stages.** Stage A makes the harness sellable to the first buyers in item 1, who run it
   themselves on Alpaca or their own broker. Stage B makes it sellable to broker-dealers.
4. **Stage A is scheduled, without changing the milestone order or the work tracker's current
   "Next, in order".** The first paper trade and the first live trade stay first; Stage A follows
   them. Its parts, all already planned or proposed:
   - the workspace API and the MCP channel (E10-6, DEC-148), with E18-4 making the channel a policy
     enforcement point, in M8;
   - identity and step-up (E9-1 to E9-4) plus E18-9's AUDITOR and SUPERVISOR roles and ASK routing
     to supervisors, in M8; E18-9's SAML and SCIM stay after v1 (DEC-18);
   - E18-1, the tenant policy overlay that can only tighten a mandate, in M8;
   - E18-3, the signed per-period evidence pack and SIEM export, in M8 after M4's cold store
     (E5-7, E5-9);
   - M11, the hybrid installer, which already depends only on M8.
5. **Stage B stays Proposed, not scheduled:** tenant isolation as a checked invariant (E18-8),
   replay of gate verdicts (E18-13), a FIX or OMS connector (new story E18-16), more broker
   connectors, SOC 2, the penetration test (M13), contracts, and a support model. The other E18
   stories stay as they were.
6. **The web app serves both buyers.** It stays the retail platform and the proving ground
   (DEC-149) and becomes the enterprise console: Stage A's supervisor approvals, auditor views, and
   evidence export are web surfaces of E18-9 and E18-3. The landing page may present the
   enterprise offer with item 2's line, under item 2's limits on claims. It carries no pricing and
   no form that collects a visitor's personal data until the founder decides the outreach and
   data terms (item 7).
7. **Still the founder's, not decided here:** authorizing discovery calls and any outreach
   (DEC-79); when to start SOC 2 and its budget; counsel's review of the controls brief and the
   compliance mapping (E18-15); the support model (design-partner terms or a first hire); and any
   contact path on the landing page.

## Rationale

Research on 2026-10-16 found the horizontal agent-governance market crowded and well funded, and
found no product that is both built for agents and aware of trading state other than tools tied to
one broker or venue (Robinhood Agents, Public Agents, LTX BondGPT). Regulators' expectations
(SEC Rule 15c3-5's deterministic pre-trade controls under the firm's control, FINRA's 2026
oversight report on agent supervision and logs, IOSCO's May 2026 toolkit on decision logs) map onto
the gate, the autonomy rules, and the journal that are already built. The founder's positioning
memo ("Mandate Enterprise Positioning", 2026-10-16) holds the evidence and its sources.

Segments E and D come first because they can run the harness themselves without a new connector,
SOC 2, or a long security review, so Stage A reaches them with work that is mostly planned already.
Broker-dealers need the OMS connector, SOC 2 Type II's review period, and support coverage, which
is Stage B. That order matches strategy option 4's standing note: brokers as a lead motion wait
until the conformance suite has outside users.

Trading-software vendors (order management, pre-trade risk, broker infrastructure) are partners
while Mandate stays upstream of their systems; item 2's "does not sell" list keeps it there.

## Alternatives considered

- **Broker-dealers first.** Largest deals and the clearest regulatory need, but they need Stage B
  in full before the first sale; too slow for this team.
- **A horizontal agent-governance product.** Crowded (WitnessAI, Noma, Credo AI, Galileo, AIR
  Security, Pillar) and gives up the trading-state moat.
- **Leave the first customer open until discovery.** Keeps options open but gives the current work
  nothing to steer by; Stage A is useful to every enterprise segment, so scheduling it costs little
  if discovery points elsewhere.
