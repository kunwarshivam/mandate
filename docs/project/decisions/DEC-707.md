## Context

Slice D2 (DEC-704) sends each notice once per recipient and channel, and treats any recorded
attempt as the end of that send. Slice D4 adds §5.3's retries. DEC-725 item 6 fixes the schedule
(gaps after the previous attempt; the window from attempt 1; an attempt due exactly at the window's
end is made; the verdict decides, not the reason), and DEC-706 item 5 fixes which attempts are
terminal. Five points are left open: when the `abandoned` record is written, its attempt number,
what an attempt due inside the window but stepped on late does, how many records a step may make
for one send, and what an `action` notice does before slice D7 gives it its `not_pending` stop.

## Decision

1. **`action` before slice D7.** An `action` send keeps §5.3's schedule with no window and is never
   `abandoned` until D7 adds the `not_pending` stop, which ends it once its approval is no longer
   pending (§5.3). D4 drops no approval notice early. Today the step reads only `OwnerAlertSent`, and
   no alert causes an `action` kind (journal spec §9.15), so no `action` notice is issued before D7
   in any case; the reading only fixes what `due` answers for one.
2. **When a send is abandoned.** A `safety` or `info` send whose next attempt would fall after its
   window's end (attempt 1's `event_time` plus 24 or 6 hours) is journaled `abandoned` with reason
   `retry_window_ended` at the first step at or after that end, never earlier. Until then it waits
   for the end. So a `safety` send is never abandoned before 24 hours, nor an `info` one before 6.
3. **The abandoned record's number** is the attempt after the last one made (`n + 1`): the attempt
   no retry fits. Its `event_time` is that step's instant, like every record the step writes, and it
   carries no send, no provider id, and a `null` verdict (rule 134).
4. **A late attempt is still made.** An attempt due at or before the window's end is made at the
   next step, even when that step comes after the end (the dispatcher was down). The send is then
   abandoned at the following step if that attempt fails `retryable`. Which attempt is due decides,
   not when the dispatcher steps (DEC-725 item 6.2).
5. **One record per send per step.** A step makes at most one attempt or one `abandoned` record per
   send. After a lapse it makes one attempt, not one for each instant it missed, and the schedule
   runs on from that attempt.
6. **The time base** of every attempt is its own record's `event_time`, the dispatcher's clock at
   that step, never the journal's `recorded_at` (DEC-725 item 6.1).
7. **The read.** `mandate_dispatcher::due(rows, audience, now)` derives each send's next step from
   the committed `NoticeIssued` and `NoticeAttempted` rows and the configured audience alone, so a
   restarted dispatcher reads the same schedule. A stored version-1 `failed` attempt stays terminal
   (DEC-706 item 6). Every retry reuses the send's idempotency key (spec §5.1, DEC-710 item 5).

## Not decided here

- **`deferred_quiet_hours`** (slice D5), coalescing (D6), `not_pending` (D7), and address marking
  and `channel_lost` (D9).
- **An operator alert for a refused append.** A record the journal refuses (for example an adapter
  answer whose variant contradicts its reason's fixed verdict) stops the step `NotCommitted`
  (DEC-704 item 3, DEC-706). Infrastructure design §8.2 lists `Unavailable` and `Ambiguous` appends
  as an operator alert from writer metrics, but not `Invalid`, and the dispatcher has no
  operator-alert path it may use: `mandate-telemetry`'s dependents are process binaries only, and
  the dispatcher process (slice D10) does not exist. This is left open for the lane lead, not
  invented here.

## Rationale

Each item picks the reading that sends no later and drops nothing. Item 2 is §5.3's "stops when
... 24 hours" read so that no window is cut short. Item 4 follows DEC-725 item 6.2, whose test is
the instant an attempt is due, not the instant a step runs. Item 5 keeps a dispatcher that returns
from a lapse from sending a burst of the same notice. None touches trading state (NT-5).

## Alternatives considered

- **Abandon at the failure that leaves no retry in the window**, as `mandate_notify::next_attempt`
  answers `RetryWindowEnded` then. Rejected: the record would claim the window had ended up to 15
  minutes before it did.
- **Abandon an `action` send after 24 hours until D7.** Rejected: it could drop an approval notice
  while the approval is still pending.
- **Number the abandoned record as the last attempt.** Rejected: rule 126 numbers attempts, and two
  records with one number on a channel would break the contiguity NT-8's oracle reads.
