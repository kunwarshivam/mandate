## Context

E7-4's last slices are crypto's stop-limit sequence, the fractional disclosure, the account and
workspace kill-switch scopes, and the stop-only equity opening. Trading-domain spec §5.4 and §5.5
leave four things unsaid: what quantity crypto's stop-limit covers before the buy's fee is known,
how an account-scoped switch closes an equity outside the regular session, what the account
scope's cancel-all does to resting protection, and what an equity opening with a stop and no
take-profit becomes on a profile that offers brackets. A fifth is how a workspace scope reaches an
executor that owns one account.

## Invariants first

- A protective order never sells more than the position holds (rule 12, §5.4).
- Risk reduction is never denied; the kill switch is always available, touches only its scope, and
  never depends on model state (`AGENTS.md` rule 13).
- An agent-scoped switch never uses cancel-all or close-position (§5.5, DEC-485 item 1).
- No opening is ever placed unprotected (§5.4, `AGENTS.md` rule 2).

## Decision

1. **Crypto's stop-limit covers what the buy leaves held once its fee posts.** A crypto buy
   withholds its fee from the asset bought (§6.3), at the maker or the taker rate. The stop-limit
   is placed only once every fill the broker reported is applied, and it covers the bought
   quantity less the fee at the larger of the two rates, rounded half up at the quantity's ninth
   place (DEC-349 item 2). It can then never outlast the position (RC-07, RC-20). A stop placed
   before the rate is known never covers more than is held.
2. **An account-scoped switch closes equities outside the regular session with the agent path's
   ladder orders.** Alpaca's close-position sends a market order, which extended hours refuse. In
   the regular session, and for crypto at any hour, the account scope closes with close-position
   per instrument (§5.5's table). Outside it, each equity closes with the agent path's own
   `md-k-<switch>-<n>` orders, priced and floored as §5.6 says. §5.5's sequence line already says
   "market orders only in the regular session … otherwise the exit price ladder", so no position
   is left without an attempt to close.
3. **The account scope's cancel-all cancels resting protection too, and that is disclosed.** §5.5
   names the broker's cancel-all endpoint for the account and workspace scopes, and that endpoint
   cancels every open order, protective ones included. No protective order survives an account
   kill switch. An automated switch's "leaving protection in place until the session" therefore
   applies to the agent scope only. The owner is told the account scope cancels protection, in the
   switch's own alert and in the runbook.
4. **The workspace scope applies per account, through each account's own executor.** An executor
   owns one account stream and one connection. A workspace switch is applied by each account's
   executor to its own account, exactly as an account switch is, with the workspace id as the
   record's subject. No executor touches another account.
5. **A stop-only equity opening on a profile that offers brackets is refused, never sent.** Such an
   entry has no bracket to go as (DEC-346 item 2). It is refused: no order is sent, the refusal is
   journaled with its reason, and one opaque alert is raised. It is never placed unprotected, and
   it is never an error that stops the process. An opening may always be refused (`AGENTS.md`
   rule 2), and refusing it is the reading that adds no risk.

## Consequences

- The crypto slices land first, then the fractional disclosure, then the account and workspace
  scopes as four DEC-77 pairs (A1 to A4), then the stop-only refusal. Each gets an independent
  review.
- Item 1 ships with the first crypto slice. The add sequence's re-placement sizes the same way
  across fills.
- Item 3's disclosure lands with the account scope's implementation and RB-03.

## Alternatives considered

- **Size crypto's stop-limit gross and correct it when the fee posts.** Rejected: between the two,
  the stop would sell more than the position holds, which rule 12 forbids.
- **Have the account scope wait for the regular session to close equities.** Rejected: the owner's
  kill switch would then hold an exit the ladder can send now (rule 13).
- **Re-place protection after an account kill switch's cancel-all.** Rejected: the switch exists to
  flatten the account, and protection re-placed beside its closes would sell the same shares twice.
- **Treat a stop-only opening as an error, or send it plain and protect it after its fill.**
  Rejected: an error stops the process for an opening that can simply be refused, and a plain entry
  is unprotected until its fill posts.
