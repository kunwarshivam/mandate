## Context

DEC-2300 books a Robinhood sell's fill at its order's limit price (items 1 to 3) and explains a
better price Robinhood pays later (items 4 to 8). Its "what this needs" names a journal spec PR
first. Building items 1 to 3 showed three things DEC-2300 leaves to the build:

- **The fill id needs no journal change.** A fill's `fill_id` is free text in every schema that
  carries it (journal spec §9.5 names no format for it), and DEC-1151's `record:` id was added in
  the executor alone. DEC-2300 item 10's first bullet put "the `limit:` fill id" in a journal spec
  PR; item 1 below supersedes that bullet for the id alone. Only items 4 to 8, the
  explained-improvement record and its rules, need the journal spec.
- **The broker record's prices are not journaled.** `OrderStateChanged` carries the state, the
  filled quantity and the broker's id, not the limit or the stop. DEC-2300 item 1 books only when
  the record's `limit_price` equals the journaled limit and, for a stop-limit, its `stop_price`
  equals the journaled stop. On Robinhood a filled order's record reaches the executor only
  through the listing (DEC-862) or our own place answer: no production code sends the executor a
  `BrokerUpdate::Order`, the Robinhood connector answers `GetOrderByClientId` with `NOT_OFFERED`,
  and reconciliation's open-orders step adopts states, not filled quantities. The listing's
  `matches` already requires the record's limit to equal ours, but not its stop.
- **A resting stop's fill date is unpublished.** DEC-1151 dates a buy by its `day` order's
  submission. A GTC stop-limit can fill on any later day, and Robinhood publishes no fill time.

## Decision

1. **Items 1 to 3 are executor code alone.** `mandate_executor::limit_fills(state, positions)`
   derives the fills DEC-2300 item 1 books, beside DEC-1151's `recorded_fills`, with the id
   `limit:<client order id>:<cumulative filled quantity>` and the quantity the increment over what
   is booked. The shell's Robinhood snapshot (`without_activities`) books both. No journal schema
   changes. Items 4 to 8 follow with their journal spec PR.
2. **The prices are checked where the record is adopted.** The listing's `matches` also requires,
   for a stop-limit, the record's `stop_price` to equal the journaled stop. A record whose prices
   differ is not adopted, so its filled quantity never reaches the journal, nothing is booked, and
   the position stays unexplained and pauses as on `main` (DEC-2300 item 3). With this, every
   filled quantity a Robinhood sell reports comes from a record whose limit and stop equal ours,
   or from our own place answer. This only refuses more.
3. **The trade date.** A `day` sell submitted before 13:00 New York, the earliest regular close
   (an early-close day's), is dated by its submission's New York date, as DEC-1151 dates a buy. A
   GTC sell, the resting stop, and a `day` sell submitted later, which may have been queued to the
   next session, are dated by the New York date of the reconciliation that books it, never
   earlier than its submission's. A later date keeps its proceeds unsettled longer
   under the cash account's settled-cash rule, which is the side that adds no buying power. It can
   undercount a same-day round trip for the day-trade count, and it moves a realized loss to a
   later risk day, a delay, never a loss of the figure. The founder's Robinhood account is a cash
   account (DEC-529 item 11), whose gate binds on settled cash, not on a day-trade budget. Before
   any Robinhood margin account is admitted, this reading is revisited.
4. **The purposes are DEC-2300's.** `protective`, `risk_exit`, `owner_exit` and
   `discretionary_exit` only. A `flatten` fill is deliberately not booked by this rule, though
   `exempt_from_pacing` treats a flatten like a risk exit: it stays unexplained and pauses, which
   is safe, and adding it is a backlog follow-up for its own decision.
5. **Tests first** (DEC-77). The tests PR adds `limit_fills` as a stub and its pending tests,
   item 2's among them: a listed record of the resting stop with another stop price must leave
   nothing booked, which reaches the listing through `limit_fills` and so fails at its stub until
   both land.

## Rationale

The booking needs only what the executor already folds plus one tightening at adoption. Dating a
resting stop's fill at its booking errs on the side that adds no buying power.

## Alternatives considered

- **Journal the record's prices on `OrderStateChanged`:** a journal version for what adoption can
  check. Rejected while adoption suffices.
- **Date a resting stop's fill by its submission:** rejected. It could count proceeds as settled
  before they are.
