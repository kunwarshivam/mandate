## Decision

1. **#1005's four "unsure" pins are looks, not rules.** [#1005](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/1005)
   kept four pins because it could not tell whether they were look or rule. They are look, and are
   unpinned:
   - the agent chart's 200px on a phone (`e2e/phone.spec.ts`);
   - the header's 65px height (`e2e/command-bar.spec.ts`, both tests);
   - the dock's light-theme shadow (`e2e/dock.spec.ts`);
   - the glass alphas: the header's glass at 72% of the card, the dock's at 85%, and the dock's edge
     at 15% of the type colour (`e2e/glass-frame.spec.ts`, and the same 85% and 15% in
     `src/lib/tokens.test.ts`).

   Each may change without a decision. Under [DEC-511](DEC-511.md) item 5, tests keep the outcome
   each pin guaranteed, and assert only that:
   - **the chart is visible and drawn:** on a phone the agent's chart is on screen, within the
     phone's width, and its plot shows its line;
   - **the header covers no content:** the page starts at or below the header's bottom edge, at
     every width the header tests already cover;
   - **the dock is legible:** every dock label holds 4.5:1 on the dock's glass over anything that
     scrolls under it, and the header's text holds 4.5:1 on its glass. These contrast tests stay
     exactly as they are (DEC-511 item 5's "contrast pairs").

   The dark theme's flat dock ("in dark mode nothing" casts a shadow, DESIGN.md) is a rule DESIGN.md
   states, not one of the four, and stays pinned. This item amends [DEC-482](DEC-482.md) item 7's
   last sentence, "The agent's chart is 200px on a phone (300px before)": the agent's chart is
   compact on a phone, and its height is look. DESIGN.md's "at 72% so muted text keeps AA" becomes
   "opaque enough that muted text keeps AA": the contrast is the rule, the percentage is look.
2. **The brand owl never wears an agent's colour** (C-15). The brand owl is the product's logo and
   brand mark: the owl in the app header, the sign-in frame, the landing page's title bar, the
   Owlhead copilot's row and the all-clear line. It is not an agent's owl, and agents' owls are
   unchanged.
   - **It supersedes DEC-452's random colour choice for the brand owl.** [DEC-452](DEC-452.md)
     item 2 drew the brand owl "in one of the four feather colours an agent's owl may take", which
     [DEC-217](DEC-217.md) item 6 sets as `series-1` to `series-4`, "picked at random on each page
     load by a script in `<head>`" (the founder's "Choose the color at random each time"). That
     choice is superseded: the brand owl takes the `--logo` token (the type colour: ink in light,
     paper in dark), on every load, and never a series colour, in either theme. With the agents'
     colours gone there is nothing left to pick among, so no pick and no script choose its colour.
   - "An agent's colour" means any colour an agent's owl may take, not only the colours of the
     agents in the workspace at that moment. The logo also shows on the sign-in frame and the
     landing page, where there is no workspace, and an agent added later would otherwise collide.
     This is the stricter of the two fixes C-15 offers, and the one that can never put an agent's
     face on the brand.
   - **The rest of DEC-452 stands.** In item 2: the brand owl is the product's own pixel owl, awake,
     in the app header, the sign-in frame and the landing page's title bar beside the outlined
     wordmark; the server's markup and the first paint already carry its final colour, so it never
     changes colour after it appears; and the traced mark (DEC-203) stays the source of the favicon,
     the app icons and the share image. Items 1 and 3 to 5 are untouched. DEC-452's status line
     records the part superseded; nothing else in DEC-452 changes.
   - Implemented in [#1127](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/1127).

## Rationale

#1005 sorted the look-pinning web tests under DEC-511 item 5 and marked four kept pins "unsure",
for a reviewer or the founder to rule on. The founder ruled them look. DEC-511 item 5 says a test
pins the invariant, not the rendering, so each pin gives way to the outcome it was standing in for:
a phone chart that shows, a header that hides nothing, and a dock you can read. Two of those
outcomes already had tests (the contrast tests for the glass and the dock's labels), and the two
that did not (the phone chart's paint, the header's overlap) gain one each.

DEC-482 item 7 and DESIGN.md stated two of the numbers. A rule stated in a binding decision does
not lapse because its test goes, so this decision amends DEC-482 item 7 and the DESIGN.md sentence
in the same change (`AGENTS.md` rule 9).

On C-15: an owl is an agent in this product, so a brand owl in Agent 1's blue two rows from Agent 1
reads as a second Agent 1 (the critique's finding on Messages, Home and Agents). DEC-452's random
pick existed only to choose among the agents' four colours; once those are excluded, the brand's
own colour, the logo's, is what is left, and it is the colour the traced mark and the wordmark
already wear. The random choice was the founder's own instruction, so it is superseded in so many
words rather than left to lapse. The rest of DEC-452 is right and stays, so only the colour choice
is superseded, and DEC-452's status line says so (as DEC-208's row records "item 3 superseded by
DEC-215").

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the four pins and record them as rules.** The founder ruled them look; DEC-511 item 5
  would then have tests holding the look in place, which is what it removes.
- **Delete the four assertions with nothing in their place.** Two outcomes (the phone chart drawn,
  the header clear of the page) would then go untested.
- **C-15 by excluding only the workspace's agents' colours.** The pick before paint cannot see the
  workspace, the signed-out pages have none, and a new agent could take the logo's colour.
- **C-15 by superseding all of DEC-452.** It would retire Stop's place in the dock and tab bar
  (item 1), the redrawn owls (item 4) and the borrowed bevel (item 5), none of which the founder
  changed.
- **C-15 by a random pick among new, non-agent brand colours.** It would add colours to the
  palette only to keep a pick whose point was the agents' colours, and each would need its own
  contrast checks in both themes.
