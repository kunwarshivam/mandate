## Decision

1. **E1b ships in three parts.**
   - Part 1a: `mandate_shell::watch`. It holds `Watch { pause, bound, interval }`, `close_window_bound` (DEC-853 item 5) and `poll_interval` (DEC-853 item 6).
   - Part 1b: `run_observed_watched` and `ProductionCycle::run_observed_watched`. Its tests cover the cancel at the bound and the dry run.
   - Part 2: the filled entry, partial fills, the cap in item 5, and the paper adapter's wiring. It follows E1b-P (DEC-859), which moves the executor's `ProtectionChanged` writers to journal spec §9.5's closed version 1. Until then, the journal refuses the `ProtectionChanged placed` that a filled bracket entry drafts (DEC-446, "The cost").
2. **The bound refuses rather than guesses.**
   - There is no bound outside a regular session, nor at or after the start of its close window. 15:50 itself is refused.
   - A zero `exit_step_s`, `unknown_absent_window_s`, `unknown_absent_lookups`, `bracket_partial_fill_timeout_s` or `max_unprotected_s` is refused, not skipped (FT-8). The rule set's loader already refuses each of them.
3. **One wake.**
   - The watch pauses `interval`, reads the entry back by its client order id, hands the answer to the executor, and ticks the executor at the clock's second.
   - At the first wake at or past the bound, the watch hands the executor `CancelOpenings` for the deployment's agent and pinned instrument in place of the read.
   - It sends no request of its own but that read (rule 12, FT-1).
   - Records it appends carry the clock's time, not the run's start.
   - A reconciliation the executor asks for mid-watch is gathered and run through the reconciler, as at the start.
   - A run that submitted nothing (a dry run) watches nothing.
4. **The watch ends** when the broker reports the entry `filled`, `canceled`, `expired` or `rejected`. After a partial fill, it also waits until the executor's protection for the filled quantity is placed or its unprotected interval is journaled (trading spec §5.4).
5. **The cap.** Past the bound, the watch runs at most until the end of the regular session. If the broker has still not confirmed the cancel by then, the run ends failing closed:
   - the unresolved state stays journaled as the executor recorded it (the entry's `pending_cancel`);
   - the owner is alerted with a message key only (rule 6);
   - the order is left to the next start's reconciliation, which queries every `pending_cancel` order by id.

   The watch never places or cancels anything more. If part 2 finds no existing account-stream record that carries the unresolved state, it stops for a journal spec PR rather than inventing one.
6. **The paper adapter's fake.** Part 2's tests PR changes `mandate-paper`'s `tests/run.rs` before any wiring. Its clock advances on each pause, and its broker reads the submitted entry back as `filled`. Then the existing placing test still ends once the run watches.

## Rationale

Item 1 keeps every PR under 400 lines. It also keeps the part that depends on E1b-P out of the parts that do not. Items 2 and 5 resolve every unknown toward sending nothing (`AGENTS.md` rule 3). Reconciliation, not the watch, owns an order whose state the broker never confirmed (journal spec §5.2). Item 3 reuses the executor's one cancel path (DEC-853 item 2) and the start's reconciliation path, so the watch adds no new way to reach the broker.

## Alternatives considered

- **Cancel again, or kill-switch, when the cancel goes unconfirmed.** Rejected. A second cancel adds nothing the executor's own lookups do not, and the kill switch flattens the position.
- **Watch until the cancel is confirmed, however long.** Rejected. A broker that never answers would hold the process open forever.
- **One E1b tests PR.** Rejected. It measured 572 lines.
