## Context

E2-6 builds the workspace data service: spec §3.1's normalized records with `vendor_time` and
`received_at`, and a bounded hot cache that every runtime and the executor read. The gate already
takes `MarketSnapshot.quote: Option<SaneQuote>` and leaves the staleness threshold to the reader
("a quote older than the profile's threshold never reaches the gate: the fold supplies `None`",
`mandate-risk`'s `usable_quote`, DEC-163 item 1). The data service is that reader. The spec says
what must hold; it leaves a few mechanics open, and these items pick the reading that hands the
gate no quote whenever it is in doubt.

## Decision

1. **The crate and its slices.** The service is `mandate-dataservice` (safety-critical, layer 8,
   above `mandate-alpaca`, whose stream messages it will normalize, and `mandate-risk`, whose
   `SaneQuote` it produces). Under ES-13's limit E2-6 lands in slices: (1) quotes and the hot
   cache, this decision; (2) instrument status and LULD bands (§3.5); (3) the bounded push queues
   with conflation and the subscription set's priority and overflow report (§3.2, §3.6); (4) the
   Alpaca stream messages normalized from recorded fixtures; (5) the journal spec change that adds
   vendor and receive times to `MarkUpdated` (§11 question 2), in its own PR first (ES-22).
2. **"The last accepted quote" of DEC-433 item 8 is the order watermark:** the last quote, per
   instrument and feed, that was in order and not future-stamped, whether or not it was sane. A
   quote stamped at or before it never marks. A crossed or too-wide quote in order moves the
   watermark and withdraws the mark, so an older sane quote arriving after it cannot mark.
3. **A future-stamped quote withdraws the mark and does not move the watermark.** Its vendor time
   is not trusted, so it cannot hold back later quotes as out of order; but the instrument has no
   mark until a later sane, in-order quote.
4. **Quote age counts UTC nanoseconds**, from the earlier of the two times (DEC-433 item 5), not
   regular-session time. Wall time is never shorter than session time, so a quote is stale at least
   as soon. This governs only the quote handed to openings; the gate's `stale_mark` restriction,
   which never flattens, keeps its own clock (mandate spec §5.2).
5. **A connection loss withdraws every mark** on the connection until a quote received after it
   marks (DP-7: a mark comes only from a live quote after resync). Watermarks stay, so a replay of
   an earlier quote never restores a mark. E2-5's connection states will call it.
6. **The spread limit is a fraction of the bid:** sane means `bid ≤ ask ≤ bid × (1 + max_spread)`,
   the bound rounded down (`Price::collar_bound`). Measured against the bid, the spread reads at
   least as wide as against the mid, so the limit binds no later.
7. **The host clock starts not known to be in tolerance.** No quote is fresh for an opening until
   the caller reports an in-tolerance clock (journal spec §5.4's `ClockOffsetRecorded`), and none
   while `ClockToleranceExceeded` stands (DEC-433 item 5). `AGENTS.md` rule 3: the unknown state is
   the safe one.
8. **Duplicates in this slice are detected against the watermark only**; any other repeat is out
   of order and never marks either. The "never stored twice" half of §3.3 lands with the store.
9. **The cache is bounded by an explicit subscription set** with a capacity: a quote for an
   instrument not subscribed, or on another feed, is refused; subscribing past the capacity is
   refused, so the instrument left out has no quote and openings in it are denied (§3.2). Which
   instruments come first is slice 3's.

## Rationale

Every item resolves a gap toward handing the gate no quote, which denies openings and leaves exits
untouched (rules 3 and 13, DP-4); `last_marked` keeps the last sane quote for exit pricing however
old (trading spec §8.2). None changes a spec, a gate rule or an envelope field, so DEC-176 lets the
agent take them.

## Alternatives considered

- **Only sane quotes move the watermark.** An older sane quote could then mark after a later
  crossed one, pricing an opening from before the market went bad. Rejected (item 2).
- **A future-stamped quote moves the watermark.** One bad stamp an hour ahead would make every real
  quote for the next hour out of order. Rejected (item 3); it stays safe either way, since both
  deny openings.
- **Count session time for openings.** The spec's session-time rule is for `stale_mark`'s
  restriction, which must not fire overnight; for an opening, wall time is the tighter reading.
- **Start with the clock in tolerance.** Rejected: nothing has shown it yet (item 7).
