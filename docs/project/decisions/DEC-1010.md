# DEC-1010: The scheduler stream's records are closed in journal spec v0.47 §9.18, before the scheduler process writes them

|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted (agent: closes a spec gap nothing has written into, under DEC-79 and DEC-176; every reading takes the side that adds no risk, and none loosens a rule) |
| **Date** | 2026-10-17 (UTC) |

| **Story or claim** | E21-29; claim #1490; journal spec §2, §4.4, §4.6, §5.1, §5.4, §9.18; infrastructure design §3.4, OPS-14 |

## Context

E21-29 builds the scheduler process, the single writer of each workspace's `clock:` stream.
Journal spec §9 catalogues the stream's records. §5.4 says the scheduler records "sources and
stratum" in `ClockOffsetRecorded` and journals `ClockToleranceExceeded` past a 50 ms tolerance, but
no payload schema is closed, and `mandate-journal` checks none.

Nothing writes the stream yet, and journal §8 never edits a written shape, so the first records
would fix their members by accident. ES-22 puts the spec change in its own PR before the code.

## Decision

1. **Five records are closed at schema version 1, on the scheduler stream only.** §9.18 closes
   them with rules 143 to 148:
   - `StreamOpened` (`stream_type` `scheduler`, `workspace_id`), written once at seq 1;
   - `ClockAdvanced` (`clock`, a whole-second `risk_clock`);
   - `TradingDayStarted` (`date`);
   - `ClockOffsetRecorded`;
   - `ClockToleranceExceeded`.

   The executor's account-stream copies keep §9's row until E7-4 slice 5 writes and closes them.
2. **The writer.** The workspace scheduler writes them as a `system` actor with `clock_source`
   `scheduler` (rule 143), under §5.1's fencing.
3. **The measurement.** Offsets and delays are signed `decimal` seconds, since §4.4 keeps integers
   non-negative. `ClockOffsetRecorded` carries:
   - `trigger`;
   - `samples`: the servers that answered, ascending by `server`, each with `stratum` 1 to 15, an
     `offset` and a `delay` of at least 0;
   - `offset`: the median by magnitude of the samples' offsets, and of two samples the larger, or
     `null` when none answered;
   - `tolerance`: default `0.05`;
   - `within_tolerance`, from rules 145 to 147.

   A measurement no server answers is out of tolerance, so an unknown clock is never read as a
   good one (`AGENTS.md` rule 3).
4. **The tolerance record.** `ClockToleranceExceeded` follows each out-of-tolerance measurement in
   the same batch, with its `offset` and `tolerance`, and names it as `causation_id` (rule 148).
5. **What reads it: design §3.4's start-up check, read the stricter way.** While the workspace's
   last measurement is out of tolerance, or before the first, the long-running executor process
   takes no new opening.
   - Risk exits, protective orders, the kill switch and the ticks are never held by a
     measurement (`AGENTS.md` rules 3 and 13).
   - Broker `event_time` stays authoritative for executions.
   - Design §3.4 says so in the same change.
   - The executor's reading is E21-30's.
   - One-shot runs that read no scheduler stream are not changed.
6. **The flagged interval.** It runs from the last in-tolerance measurement before a
   `ClockToleranceExceeded` to the next in-tolerance one, over the whole stream whatever the writer
   epoch. The earlier start flags more records, never fewer. Exports compute the flag, and no risk
   input reads it.
7. **The ticks never stop or run backwards.** The scheduler writes one tick per second on its own
   monotonic clock, each the later of the previous tick plus one second and the host clock's whole
   second.
   - A host clock that steps back, or a restart within the last tick's second, continues one second
     at a time rather than freezing the risk clock, so no time-based exit or timer is held.
   - A forward step is followed, and the measurements detect it.
   - `TradingDayStarted` follows the first tick of each new trading date, in its batch.
   - `append` does not compare ticks or dates across records at this version; the writer holds
     them and its tests show it, as §9.17 does for sequences.
8. **Version and vectors.** The change takes journal spec v0.47, since open changes hold v0.45 and
   v0.46. It adds no reference vectors, as §9.15 and §9.16 add none. `mandate-journal`'s tests
   cover every member type and rule when the registration lands with the scheduler code (ES-22).

## Rationale

The records exist to make the clock's quality auditable after the fact (§5.4, design §11's
clock-skew row) and to keep a bad clock from adding risk.

Closing them before the first write keeps them replayable forever under §8. Every reading here
takes the side that reports a bad clock or withholds risk:
- an unanswered measurement is out of tolerance;
- the median by magnitude picks the worse of two servers;
- the flagged interval starts early;
- an out-of-tolerance clock withholds new openings and never an exit.

## Alternatives considered

- **Write the scheduler with open payloads and close them later.** Rejected: §8 never edits a
  written shape, so the first payloads would become the schema without review.
- **Integer nanoseconds.** Rejected: §4.4 keeps integers non-negative, so a host running behind
  could never record its offset.
- **Stop ticking while out of tolerance, or wait for the host clock after a step back.** Rejected:
  either freezes the risk clock that exits and timers run on.
- **Let an out-of-tolerance clock gate nothing.** Rejected: design §3.4 makes the offset a start-up
  check that refuses trading. Withholding openings only is the reading that keeps exits free.
- **Flag from the out-of-tolerance measurement onward.** Rejected: the drift may have started at any
  time after the last good measurement, so it would flag too few.
- **The signed median, or the mean.** Rejected: a signed lower median of two servers is asymmetric
  (+100 ms and +10 ms read as 10 ms), and one bad server moves a mean.
- **Reference vectors for the stream.** Deferred: `reference/journal` models no scheduler stream, and
  §9.15 set the precedent of covering a closed stream with `mandate-journal`'s own tests.
