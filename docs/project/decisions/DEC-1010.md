## Context

E21-29 builds the scheduler process, the single writer of each workspace's `clock:` stream. Journal
spec §9 catalogues the stream's records, and §5.4 says the scheduler records "sources and stratum"
in `ClockOffsetRecorded` and journals `ClockToleranceExceeded` past a 50 ms tolerance, but no
payload schema is closed, and `mandate-journal` checks none. Nothing writes the stream yet. Journal
§8 never edits a written shape, so the first records would fix their members by accident. ES-22
puts the spec change in its own PR before the code.

## Decision

1. **§9.18 closes five records at schema version 1 on the scheduler stream only.** These are
   `StreamOpened` (`stream_type` `scheduler`, `workspace_id`), `ClockAdvanced` (`clock`, a
   whole-second `risk_clock`), `TradingDayStarted` (`date`), `ClockOffsetRecorded` and
   `ClockToleranceExceeded`, with rules 143 to 148. The executor's account-stream copies keep §9's
   row until E7-4 slice 5 writes and closes them.
2. **The writer.** The workspace scheduler writes the records as a `system` actor with
   `clock_source` `scheduler` (rule 143), under §5.1's fencing.
3. **The measurement.** `ClockOffsetRecorded` carries:
   - `trigger`: `startup`, `before_session` or `periodic`.
   - `samples`: the servers that answered, with `server`, `stratum` (1 to 15), `offset_ns` and
     `delay_ns`, strictly ascending by server.
   - `offset_ns`: the lower median of the samples, or `null` when none answered.
   - `tolerance_ns`.
   - `within_tolerance`: rules 145 to 147.

   A measurement no server answers is out of tolerance, so an unknown clock is never read as a good
   one (`AGENTS.md` rule 3).
4. **The tolerance record.** `ClockToleranceExceeded` follows each out-of-tolerance measurement in
   the same batch, with its `offset_ns` and `tolerance_ns`, and names it as `causation_id`
   (rule 148).
5. **The flagged interval.** It runs from the last in-tolerance measurement before a
   `ClockToleranceExceeded` to the next in-tolerance one. Starting at the last good measurement
   rather than the bad one flags more records, never fewer. Exports compute it, and no risk input
   reads it; broker `event_time` stays authoritative for executions (§5.4).
6. **The ticks.** The scheduler ticks once a second. It never writes a tick at or before the last
   one, and it keeps ticking while out of tolerance: the tick is the clock time-based exits run on,
   so stopping it could hold a risk exit, and design OPS-14 says an offset is measured, journaled
   and flagged, never acted on. `append` does not compare ticks or dates across records at this
   version; the writer holds them and its tests show it, as §9.17 does for sequences.
7. **Version and vectors.** The change takes journal spec v0.47, since open changes hold v0.45 and
   v0.46. It adds no reference vectors, as §9.15 and §9.16 add none. `mandate-journal`'s tests
   cover every member type and rule when the registration lands with the scheduler code (ES-22).

## Rationale

The records exist to make the clock's quality auditable after the fact (§5.4, design §11's
clock-skew row) without letting a bad clock move a risk decision. Closing them before the first
write keeps them replayable forever under §8, and every reading here picks the side that reports a
bad clock: an unanswered measurement is out of tolerance, and the flagged interval starts early.

## Alternatives considered

- **Write the scheduler with open payloads and close them later.** Rejected: §8 never edits a
  written shape, so the first payloads would become the schema without review.
- **Stop ticking while out of tolerance.** Rejected: it would freeze the risk clock that exits and
  timers run on, which can add risk, and design OPS-14 has the offset only flag records.
- **Flag from the out-of-tolerance measurement onward.** Rejected: the drift may have started at any
  time after the last good measurement, so it would flag too few.
- **The mean of the samples.** Rejected: one bad server moves a mean; the lower median needs most
  servers to be wrong.
- **Reference vectors for the stream.** Deferred: `reference/journal` models no scheduler stream, and
  §9.15 set the precedent of covering a closed stream with `mandate-journal`'s own tests.
