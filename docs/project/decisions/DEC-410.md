## Decision

1. **A remainder counts only what is left, and only while something will send it** (§5.6 step 5). A ladder is between rungs from the confirmation of a step's cancel that leaves part of its rung unsold, until it sends its next rung or ends.
   - **What it counts is derived, never stored.** Whenever it is read, it is the stepped rung's unfilled quantity, capped at the position less the open non-protective sells and less the remainders counted ahead of it. Nothing is trimmed and nothing is journaled to change it, so a restart reads the same figure from the same folded state.
   - **Two ladders share the room in their exits' intent id order.** One instrument holds at most two: a sequence's and one without a sequence. Id order is used because it is deterministic, so a replay shares the room the same way. Both belong to exits already allowed, so neither order adds risk, and the total sold is the same either way.
   - **A sequence's remainder counts while its ladder climbs:** its agent neither paused nor stopped, and its unprotected interval not past its bound. A ladder that did not climb at the confirmation has ended (DEC-409). A pause or stop after the confirmation suspends the count, and it counts again on resumption.
   - **A parked sequence past its bound ends.** If a parked sequence's interval reaches its bound, its ladder ends. When protection returns, it is not carried on as a lone ladder.
   - **A lone ladder's remainder counts unless it is parked while its agent is paused or stopped.** It counts again when the agent resumes.
   - **It ends** only at one of these:
     - its next rung is sent, whole or short (item 4);
     - nothing is left when its next rung is due (item 4);
     - its exit's intent is abandoned;
     - for a sequence, its ladder stops climbing at the confirmation or at the bound.
   - **A new sequence for the same exit** takes the lone ladder in, stepping and parked as it was, and the sequence's rules then apply.
   - **The gate's `available` is unchanged.** The gate reads the counted remainders beside it (`between_rungs`) only to size a discretionary exit (item 3).
2. **The plan gives way, never the sell.** Any sell the gate allows that is not a discretionary exit is checked against the open orders alone, as on `main`, and goes whole. Nothing is done to the remainders: they count only what it leaves, at once. If the sell ends unsold before a ladder sends, its room comes back by itself. No new hold reason exists, and rule 13's four holds stand.
3. **A discretionary exit is paced beside a plan** (**Proposed**; the founder decides).
   - Beside remainders it is sized to what the open sells and the counted remainders leave (`GateDecided sized_qty`, folded into the intent's quantity). Rule 13 allows that as pacing.
   - **With nothing left, it is denied `sell_exceeds_available`, but only once it is otherwise releasable now.** One held `session_closed`, `session_unknown` or `exit_unpriced` keeps that hold and is decided again at release, when the remainder is either still counted or gone. Only the open-orders half of rule 4 comes before the holds, as ruling D1 intends.
   - **The denial is terminal.** The intent is not retried, and a later proposal is judged afresh.
   - **Why it is the founder's.** On `main` that reason fires only for open orders, and rule 13 says a discretionary exit is never denied. Denying one for a plan is new.
   - **Meanwhile the code takes the option that adds no risk, which is this one.** Allowing the sell would over-sell (rule 12). Letting a discretionary exit take a risk exit's planned shares would move them from the more urgent exit to a paced one and delay risk reduction.
   - **The cost is bounded.** If the ladder then fails to send, its remainder stops counting, and the next discretionary proposal goes.
4. **The next rung sends what the remainder counts when it is due.**
   - A rung smaller than the stepped rung's unfilled quantity is journaled as short: `ProtectionChanged rung_short`, with the exit's intent, the quantity not sent, and the reason, `position_taken`.
   - With nothing left, no rung is sent and the ladder ends, journaled the same way. A ladder with nothing left so never sends later, for example into a position bought afresh.
   - That record is the disclosure a stored trim used to give.
5. **The kill switch.** E7-3's agent-scoped flatten stops the agent first, so its parked ladders count nothing. The flatten goes whole, and any other remainder counts only what it leaves. Nothing is sent to the broker for a plan, and no other agent's orders are touched.
6. **Every state, walked.** Take one ladder parked overnight with 4 unsold of 10 held, and a sell of 8 beside it that took the room.
   - **The sell fills:** the position is 2, the open sells 0, so the remainder counts 2 and the rung at the open sends 2, journaled short by 2.
   - **The sell part-fills 5, then ends:** the position is 5, so the remainder counts 4 again and the rung sends 4.
   - **The sell is rejected, cancelled, abandoned, or expires at the close unsold:** the open sells drop by 8, so the remainder counts 4 again and the rung sends 4. This is the script that showed the stored trim's answer false (item 7).
   - **The ladder sends at the open:** what it counts then, after the exits held overnight are released in the same step.
   - **After a restart:** the stepping and parked flags, the cancelled rung, the open orders, the position and the modes are all folded, so it counts the same.
   - **After a pause and resume:** a parked lone ladder counts nothing while its agent is paused, and what is left when it resumes. A sequence's ladder paused after the confirmation is suspended and counts again on resumption; one paused at the confirmation has ended (DEC-409).
   - **Two agents in one instrument:** a stopped agent's parked ladder counts nothing, so another agent's risk exit goes whole (round 1's script, pinned).
7. **Attacked:**
   - **Can a remainder stay counted that nothing sends?** No.
     - A sequence counts only while it climbs, and `steps` sends its next rung then.
     - A parked one waits only for the open, or for its agent; a paused or stopped agent stops it counting.
     - The bound ends it, and the old ladder may send no rung after (the oracle checks).
   - **Can the ladder and the sell beside it together sell less than the exits asked for?** Only by what was already sent when the rung went. Round 2's review showed the stored trim's "No" was false: a risk exit of 8 trimmed a parked remainder of 4 by 2, the 8 was then rejected, and the ladder sent 2, so 2 shares nobody sold. That "No" is withdrawn. With a derived count, the room comes back the moment the sell ends unsold, and the ladder sends 4. What remains is the case where the sell is still open when the rung goes: the rung goes short, journaled, and if the sell then ends unsold, the shortfall is the sell's own failure, disclosed in the record. That window cannot be reached today: a remainder lasts only while its ladder is parked for the open, nothing beside it is allowed while it is parked, and at the open the sell and the rung go in the same step. The oracle asserts that no sell ever works beside a remainder that is not parked, so a change that opens the window turns the property red.
   - **How the shortfall ends.** The unsent shares are never left bare. Once the short rung ends (filled, cancelled, or at its floor and stepped out), no exit is working or waiting in the instrument. §5.4 then re-places protection for exactly the position left, no more and no less. The pinned script ends with 8 held and 8 protected.
     - **Who sells them:** the next evaluation of the condition that raised the risk exit, which proposes a new exit; the re-placed stop, if the price reaches it; the triggered-stop watchdog after it; or the owner. Nothing retries the short rung, so no stale plan sells later.
     - **At the session's close:** the re-placed protection is GTC and rests overnight like any other.
     - **At a restart:** the `rung_short` record, the ended ladder and the protection all fold, so the executor resumes exactly there and sends nothing for the shortfall.
   - **Can a rung send more than the position allows?** No. It sends at most the position less the open sells and the remainders ahead of it, read in the same step.

## Rationale

Round 1's review measured what the first version cost:
- it made a fifth hold on an exit;
- it sized a risk exit down beside a stopped agent's ladder that would never send (`main` sells all 10 on that route, the first version 6, or holds everything);
- its hold had no end, since the held exit was itself among the `waiting` that kept the interval open.

Adding a hold to rule 13 or shrinking a risk exit weakens a safety rule, which DEC-79 reserves for the founder. A remainder is a plan, not an order, so letting it give way costs nothing at the broker. Counting only what will be sent removes the phantom remainders.

Round 2's review measured what the stored trim cost: a trim was never given back when the sell that took it ended unsold, so the two exits together sold less than the position. A count derived whenever it is read cannot drift from the state it is read from. It keeps the Σ at every input, needs no record to be right twice, and gives room back by itself.

## Alternatives considered

- **The first version (#494 at its first head): size the newer sell, or hold it `exit_between_rungs`.** Not taken.
  - **The review's script:** one account holds 10 AAPL. Agent A's lone ladder parks at 20:00:05 with 4 unsold, and A is stopped. Agent B's risk exit for 10 is sized to 6; with A's remainder at 10, it is held with no end.
  - **The reasons:** it needs a fifth hold on an exit, it shrinks a risk exit, and its wait is unbounded.
  - **What it would take:** an amendment to rule 13, MI-1 and the four-holds property, and a bound on the hold. Those are the founder's to make (recorded on #494 for them).
- **Round 1's version of this decision: trim the remainder by the shortfall, journaled `remainder_trimmed`, folded into a stored total, and trim the overhang at every step.** Withdrawn in round 2 (the coordinator's ruling, 20:02Z). The stored total was never given back when the trimming sell ended unsold. Item 7 has the script.
- **Keep the stored trim and give it back when the trimming sell ends having sold less than it took.** Not taken. It needs a second record symmetric with the first, and both must be right on every path. The derived count gives the same result with nothing stored.
- **The newer exit ends the parked ladder outright.** Not taken: where they overlap only in part, the derived count sells more.
- **Take discretionary exits' remainders first when two ladders share an instrument.** Not taken. Neither order changes the total sold, and id order is deterministic without reading purposes.
