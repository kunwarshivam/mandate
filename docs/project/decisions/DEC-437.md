## Decision

The [identity spec](../../specs/identity.md) v0.1 is a draft. Its invariants are ID-1 to ID-15.

### Accepted (agent)

1. **No inheritance from org roles to workspace roles** (spec §4.1). Acting in a workspace needs a
   workspace membership; the retail sign-up creates one. Least privilege, and it keeps the V-047 count
   a count of workspace members.
2. **The permission matrix** (§4.2), with PX-11 (b) for pause and stop, DEC-141 and DEC-191 for
   clients, and read and export only for service accounts in v1. A blank cell is a denial (ID-2). Round
   2 adds the receive column (§4.1): which notice classes of the notifications spec each role may be
   sent, a viewer never `action` (settlement X1). The round-2 ruling adds a Host CLI column (pause and
   kill-switch engage at agent, connection, and workspace scope, in the deployment's own workspace
   only), a platform-operator column for the break-glass operational set, and rows for the owner
   request, the dry run, and the chat thread (operator; client for request and dry run), so this
   matrix is the only authority the workspace API cites.
3. **`workspace_users` is derived** from the control stream's membership events (§5.3): distinct
   `user` principals whose membership is `active` and past cool-off, read at validation and again at
   application; unreadable counts as one. This restates DEC-411 item 2 in terms of memberships and adds
   only the cool-off, which refuses more.
4. **The 24-hour cool-off, one rule** (§8.3, cited from §5.1 and §5.2): it applies exactly when the
   workspace's effective `independent_approval_required` is true at the grant and the grant adds
   operator or approver to a member of an existing workspace; every other grant has none, and a
   workspace's founding membership never has one (so a retail sign-up counts as one user at once). A
   passkey enrolled through recovery has its own 24-hour enrolment cool-off (§10.1). It only delays
   what can count as the other party or present step-up.
5. **Action-bound step-up** (§7.2): the WebAuthn challenge is the hash of a single-use record naming
   the principal, workspace, action kind, and action digest, valid 300 s, with user verification
   required; `live` accepts `passkey` only. It adds the action binding to mandate §6.1's evidence and
   relaxes none of the runtime's checks. The raw assertion is kept as an artifact for re-verification.
6. **The risk-reduction path** (§6.4): a session whose refresh failed because the identity provider
   is unreachable (transport failure or 5xx only; a refusal is a deprovision and ends the session at
   once, §11.1), a workspace-local passkey verification opening a 15-minute pause-and-kill-switch
   session (refused after a deprovision signal), or the host CLI acting as its own registered
   principal with a ULID (`HostCliRegistered`), so an identity-provider outage never blocks pause or
   a kill switch (ID-10) and no event is committed without a principal (ID-1). It grants nothing that
   mandate §6.1 does not already allow without step-up.
7. **Session limits** (§6.2): 5-minute access tokens, membership re-checked uncached on every request,
   rotating refresh tokens with reuse detection, idle and absolute limits that organizations may only
   shorten; DPoP-bound tokens for non-browser clients.
8. **A client is its user** for independence (§8.2, ID-6), so a user's own connected agent can never
   be the second party.
9. **Membership, credential, session, client, service-account, host-CLI, and break-glass events** (§12.1)
   are owed to journal §9 by E9-7's tests PR, with schemas. They include `SessionOpened` with a
   `first_seen_device` flag and the `deprovisioned` reason on `SessionRevoked`, the subject events of
   the notifications spec's `new_device` and `deprovisioned` kinds (round-2 ruling, M-2). The journal spec is not edited here.
10. **A client is its own actor kind** (§12.2; settlement X2, matching DEC-436 item 9): `actor.kind`
    `client`, `actor.id` the client, `actor.on_behalf_of` its user. A client is never recorded as a
    `user`, so mandate §6.4 check 3 refuses its answer from the record alone, and the independence
    checks compare `human(…)` through `on_behalf_of` (§8.2). The journal §3 and mandate spec edits are
    owed by E9-9's tests PR, and no client token is issued before they land.
11. **Revoking a broker connection needs step-up** (§4.2): it ends the executor's ability to re-place
    protection and run risk exits, so it is not a risk reduction; the kill switch is.
12. **Last owner and last admin** (§5.2): a change leaving an organization without an active org owner,
    or a workspace without an active workspace admin, is refused unless it names the successor.
13. **Organization deletion** (§5.2): refused while any agent is deployed and not stopped or any
    connection is unrevoked; then a 30-day closing period; records kept for their retention period.
14. **Backlog**: acceptance criteria for E9-1, E9-2, E9-4, and E9-5, and new rows E9-7 to E9-11, all
    safety-critical and tests first (DEC-77); E9-12 holds round 1's minors (freeze rule).

### Proposed (founder)

15. **Identity provider for managed mode, and passkey custody.** Recommendation: keep Supabase Auth
    (DEC-211) as the managed OIDC issuer, and make workspace services the WebAuthn relying party for
    step-up and the risk-reduction path, so no hosted service sits in a core path (HLD §4). A paid plan
    at launch is spending. *Conservative interim:* paper only; step-up stays `cli_confirm` (DEC-155).
16. **SAML 2.0 in v1.** Recommendation: OIDC only in v1; SAML later with a library chosen then.
    *Interim:* OIDC only.
17. **Deactivating the second user under `independent_approval_required`** (DEC-411 item 6's open half).
    Recommendation: never refuse a deactivation; agents keep running as DEC-411 item 6 says; flagging
    them `policy_nonconforming` remains the founder's product call. *Interim:* deactivation proceeds,
    because refusing it would keep a departed person's access, while proceeding adds no trading risk
    (a latch only holds; the founder's 2026-10-03 ruling lets a risk-reducing version through V-047 in
    a lone workspace).
18. **Platform-assisted organization recovery** and its identity-verification text. Recommendation: a
    7-day waiting procedure with notice to every member, wording reviewed by counsel. *Interim:* none.
19. **Break-glass customer approval wording and terms.** Recommendation: counsel-reviewed text in the
    terms; the mechanics of spec §10.3 stand. *Interim:* no break-glass in any customer deployment.
20. **A staff-only break-glass path when the customer cannot be reached** (#556 round 1, B1). Whether
    platform staff may ever act in a customer's workspace without the customer's approval is a safety
    rule and a terms question. Recommendation: none; if one is wanted, pause only (it places no
    order), two staff, the customer told at once, never a flatten. *Interim:* none; break-glass needs
    the customer's approval, with no other route (spec §10.3, ID-12).
21. **A halted state after a scope kill switch** (#556 round 1, M4). Spec §4.4 would leave a
    connection-, workspace-, or org-scope kill switch's scope halted until re-enabled with step-up.
    Trading spec §5.5 and mandate §6.1 have no such state. Recommendation: adopt it with those specs
    edited in the same change, and decide with item 17 whether re-enabling needs a user other than the
    one who engaged it. *Interim:* no halted state; engaging stays one action without step-up, and
    trading again means redeploying, which needs the deploy step-up.

## Rationale

The trading specs already say what a committed owner command, approval, or acknowledgment does;
none says what must be true before one is committed. This spec fills that gap with rules that only
refuse more than today's specs require, so every agent reading is a tightening. What remains open is
vendor and spending choice, legal text, and one product question the founder already holds.

## Alternatives considered

- **Org roles inherit into every workspace.** Rejected: an org admin would silently count toward
  V-047 and hold trading permissions everywhere.
- **Step-up as "signed in recently".** Rejected: a stolen session would carry it, and one gesture
  could cover several actions, against mandate §6.4.
- **Supabase as the only passkey verifier.** Rejected for step-up and emergency sign-in: a hosted
  service would sit in a core path, and its outage would block the kill switch's sign-in.
- **A client recorded as its `user` with `client_id` beside it** (round 1's reading). Rejected in round
  2 (settlement X2): mandate §6.4 check 3 would pass a client's answer on the record, leaving an API
  refusal as the only guard.
- **A failed refresh kept reduction permissions whatever the cause** (round 1). Rejected (M2): a
  deprovisioned person would keep the kill switch for up to 12 hours.
