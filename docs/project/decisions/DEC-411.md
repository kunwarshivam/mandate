## Decision

1. **The rule.** Mandate spec V-047: when the workspace's policy requires independent approval
   (`independent_approval_required`, §4.3), the workspace has at least two users, or the mandate is
   refused at validation, and again when a version is applied (V-002's pattern). Under that policy
   deployment, a high-water-mark reset, and lifting a fired tripwire each need a user other than the
   requester (§4.3, §5.8, §6.7). A one-user workspace could not deploy the mandate, acknowledge its
   drawdown ladder, or lift a tripwire it fired, so the owner is told at validation, where the reason
   can be shown, rather than at deployment or at the first latch. §5.7's single-user path for
   loosening a latched lifetime floor (after a waiting period) is therefore never reached under the
   policy, and §5.7 says so (#528 round 1, M1).
2. **What validation reads.** The effective value of `independent_approval_required`, after the
   hierarchy (§4.3: once `true` at a level, every child is `true`), and the workspace's user count,
   the fact V-020 already reads (`workspace_users`). Users are the workspace's active members: a
   pending invitation or a deactivated account is not one. Any user counts, not only approvers: §4.3
   asks for "a user other than the requester", and V-024 already bounds the approvers. **A count that
   is absent or not known counts as one user** (rule 3, as §6.7 says of a missing name; #528 round 1,
   M2). §4 now lists everything validation reads. V-047 is a validation-time check of the count; it
   is not proof that the two users are two people.
   **Not covered: two users, one approver who is the author** (#528 round 1, M3). With two users of
   whom only the author holds the approver role, V-047 and V-024 pass, but an ask needing an
   independent approver cannot reach its quorum and times out (§6.4). Extending V-047 to approvers
   would go past the founder's decision and needs inputs validation does not have, so it is recorded
   beside item 6's backlog question, not decided here. MC-V70 shows only that V-047 passes, not that
   the ask path works.
3. **The number.** V-045 is reserved by DEC-196 for E8-8's delegation total and V-046 is the review
   date (DEC-188), so this rule is V-047.
4. **The cases.** MC-V69 (required, one user: V-047 alone), MC-V70 (required, two users) and MC-V71
   (not required, one user) differ from the base only in those two facts, and `check_cases.py`
   checks that. They are pending: the Rust harness's validation context has no
   `independent_approval_required` yet, so it refuses the cases' context by name until the code
   story adds it (DEC-77: tests, then the implementation, then the status rows).
5. **The reference.** `ref.py`'s `semantic()` adds V-047. `fuzz_independence_floor` checks it
   against an oracle that names the lone workspaces as a set, an absent count among them, and checks
   that V-047 changes no other verdict. Three seeded bugs in `mutants.py` must be caught: a stated
   policy read by its presence rather than its value, a one-user workspace counted as enough, and an
   unknown count read as two users. The reference models V-047 at validation only, as it does V-002:
   neither rule's re-check when a version is applied is modelled, so the apply-time half is owed by
   E10-1 with its own case (#528 round 2, major 1), and V-002's gap is a backlog row.
6. **Not covered: a workspace that drops below two users, or turns the policy on, after a version
   is confirmed.** Validation runs on a version, so a running agent whose workspace loses its second
   user, or whose workspace turns `independent_approval_required` on with one user, keeps running.
   Nothing it latched can be lifted until a second user exists, which adds no risk (a latch only
   holds). Under the policy no new version validates there at all, a risk-reducing one and the
   conforming version §4.3 asks for included, because V-047 refuses every version; the only exits
   are adding a user or stopping the agent. That stays refused, the conservative side (#528 round 1,
   minor 5); whether to exempt a reducing version is put to the founder with this item. §6.4's
   one-person sentence and §6.7's lone-workspace sentence describe this state, and MC-W50, MC-W53,
   MC-W54 and MC-W56 pin it. Whether removing the second user should be refused, or flag the agents
   `policy_nonconforming` as a `PolicyChanged` does, is product behavior, so it is recorded in the
   backlog as a founder question rather than decided here.

## Rationale

Refusing at validation is the earliest point the owner can see the conflict, and it is the
conservative side: a mandate that could never deploy or never be lifted is not confirmed.

## Alternatives considered

- **Refuse at deployment.** Rejected by the founder: the owner learns late, after confirming.
- **Allow it and let the latch wait for a second user.** Rejected by the founder: a one-user
  workspace could never deploy under maker-checker, so the mandate would be confirmed for nothing.
- **Count only approvers.** Rejected: §4.3 asks for another user, not another approver, and V-024
  already bounds approvers.
