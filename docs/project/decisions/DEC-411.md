| **Story or claim** | E10-1 (the MC-V cases); claim [#439](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/issues/439) (stream H) |

## Decision

1. **The rule.** Mandate spec V-047: when the workspace's policy requires independent approval
   (`independent_approval_required`, §4.3), the workspace has at least two users, or the mandate is
   refused at validation. Under that policy deployment, a risk-increasing change, a high-water-mark
   reset, loosening a latched lifetime floor, and lifting a fired tripwire each need a user other
   than the requester. A one-user workspace could deploy nothing and lift nothing the mandate
   latched, so the owner is told at validation, where the reason can be shown, rather than at
   deployment or at the first latch.
2. **What validation reads.** The effective value of `independent_approval_required`, after the
   hierarchy (§4.3: once `true` at a level, every child is `true`), and the workspace's user count,
   the fact V-020 already reads (`workspace_users`). Any user counts, not only approvers: §4.3 asks
   for "a user other than the requester", and V-024 already bounds the approvers.
3. **The number.** V-045 is reserved by DEC-196 for E8-8's delegation total and V-046 is the review
   date (DEC-188), so this rule is V-047.
4. **The cases.** MC-V69 (required, one user: V-047 alone), MC-V70 (required, two users) and MC-V71
   (not required, one user) differ from the base only in those two facts, and `check_cases.py`
   checks that. They are pending: the Rust harness's validation context has no
   `independent_approval_required` yet, so it refuses the cases' context by name until the code
   story adds it (DEC-77: tests, then the implementation, then the status rows).
5. **The reference.** `ref.py`'s `semantic()` adds V-047. `fuzz_independence_floor` checks it
   against an oracle that names the lone workspaces as a set, and checks that V-047 changes no
   other verdict. Two seeded bugs in `mutants.py` (a stated policy read by its presence rather than its value, a one-user workspace counted
   as enough) must be caught.
6. **Not covered: a workspace that drops below two users, or turns the policy on, after a version
   is confirmed.** Validation runs on a version, so a running agent whose workspace loses its second
   user, or whose workspace turns `independent_approval_required` on with one user, keeps running.
   Nothing it latched can be lifted until a second user exists, which adds no risk (a latch only
   holds). Whether removing the second user should be refused, or flag the agents
   `policy_nonconforming` as a `PolicyChanged` does, is product behavior, so it is recorded in the
   backlog as a founder question rather than decided here.

## Rationale

Refusing at validation is the earliest point the owner can see the conflict, and it is the
conservative side: a mandate that could never deploy or never be lifted is not confirmed.

## Alternatives considered

- **Refuse at deployment.** Rejected by the founder: the owner learns late, after confirming.
- **Allow it and let the latch wait for a second user.** Rejected by the founder: a one-user
  workspace could never deploy under maker-checker, so the mandate would be confirmed for nothing.
- **Count only approvers.** Rejected: §4.3 asks for another user, not another approver, and V-024
  already bounds approvers.
