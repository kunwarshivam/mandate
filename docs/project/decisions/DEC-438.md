## Decision

### Accepted (agent)

1. **The payload is `{notice, text}` and nothing else.** `notice` is a random 128-bit notice id,
   never a journal event id (item 10); `text` is one of four keys, `approval_needed`,
   `attention_needed`, `account_changed`, `brief_ready`. The notice's kind is journaled and shown in
   the workspace, never sent (spec §4.2). This generalizes mandate spec §6.4's approval payload to
   every notice without adding a field. *(Round 1: the notice id replaced the subject's ULID, and
   `account_changed` was added, settlements X3 and X4.)*
2. **Three classes.** `action` (approvals), `safety` (anything that holds, restricts, or protects
   money, and every risk-limit alert), and `info` (nothing the agent may do changed), with the
   channels, quiet hours, retries, and limits of spec §3.1. The `safety` list is PX-16 (b)'s
   interrupt list plus the protection alerts G5 already treats as safety.
3. **`web_inbox` is a pull channel**, read exactly as `cli_inbox` is under DEC-156 item 6: delivered
   in the request's own batch, never suppressed, so check 4 never depends on a provider. It adds no
   risk: a grant still needs an authenticated listed approver, step-up, and checks 1 to 12.
4. **The journal is the outbox.** The dispatcher sends only about committed events, keyed
   `(subject, kind, recipient, channel)`, at least once; a crash re-sends rather than loses (spec
   §5.1).
5. **Records, one writer per stream** (journal spec §2). The dispatcher is the single writer of a
   new notice stream, `ntf:{workspace_id}`, holding `NoticeIssued` and `NoticeAttempted`, and writes
   no other stream. `OwnerAlertSent` is written by the owner of the subject's stream, in that stream
   and the subject's batch, with the kind; it records that an alert was raised, not its delivery.
   `ApprovalDelivered` records only the pull channels. The dispatcher runs as its own process, so no
   trading process does provider I/O. Journal spec v0.12 records the stream; E8-9's tests PR closes
   the schemas (spec §5.1, §5.5). *(Round 1, M1: the earlier draft had the dispatcher write
   `OwnerAlertSent` on the control stream from the runtime's and executor's shells, which would
   have made a second writer of that stream.)*
6. **Escalation in v1 is a fan-out.** Every configured push channel at once, plus one approval
   reminder when a quarter of `timeout_s` remains and at least 60 seconds remain. An ordered chain
   waits for SMS and phone (E8-7) and a schema field (spec §12 item 5).
7. **Quiet hours by class.** `action` push suppressed, as mandate spec §6.4 says, never deferred past
   a deadline; `info` deferred to the window's end; `safety` never affected.
8. **Safety notices are coalesced, never dropped**: one message per recipient and channel per 60
   seconds, the first sent at once, every subject recorded. Send quotas never refuse `safety`.
9. **Retries.** At once, 15 s, 60 s, 5 min, then every 15 min; `action` stops when the approval stops
   being pending, `safety` after 24 hours, `info` after 6 hours.
10. **Links carry no authority and no event reference.** `<origin>/n/<notice id>` only: no token,
    session, magic sign-in, query string, or tracking parameter. The notice id is random, not the
    subject event's ULID, whose leading bits are its creation time; the workspace API resolves it
    (workspace API spec §3.9, settlement X3). Mandate spec §6.4's payload sentence and §6.7's
    tripwire sentence are amended to match: a tightening under DEC-176, since it only sends less.
11. **Email rules.** Fixed no-reply sender; replies discarded unread; no greeting by name; open and
    click tracking off; no remote images; `List-Unsubscribe` on `info` mail only.
12. **Chat is outbound only.** No buttons or commands; the only inbound message acted on is the
    Telegram linking code, which can only record a chat address; link unfurling off; a Slack webhook
    URL is a credential kept in the vault.
13. **Web push is encrypted to the browser** (RFC 8291, RFC 8292). Managed deployments send directly;
    the relay forwards for egress-restricted deployments and native push, checks a 512-byte cap and
    the urgency enum, stores no ciphertext, and logs opaque ids and counts only.
14. **Provider interface.** One adapter per channel; failure reasons are a closed enum; provider
    error text is never journaled or logged, since a provider may echo the message.
15. **Lost addresses.** A hard bounce, complaint, rejected address, or failed channel credential
    marks the address `unreachable` and alerts the user on every other channel; it never changes
    trading state.
16. **No push about a cancellation.** A canceled or timed-out approval shows its terminal state in
    the inbox and on the landing screen; the brief lists it.
17. **Alert keys become a closed enum.** `NotificationRef.message_key: &'static str` in
    `mandate-runtime` and `mandate-executor` is replaced by the kind enum, which maps to a text key
    (rung 1 of the trust ladder).
18. **The HLD's flow C example is corrected** to generic text with no agent name (PX-6 (b) was
    accepted; 09-product-experience §7 item 6 asked for this), and HLD §12 item 6 now cites DEC-19.

### Accepted in round 1 (agent)

27. **Identity and account-security notices** (settlement X4). A new credential, a sign-in from a
    device not seen before, an identity-provider deprovision, a recovery, a role
    grant, a deactivation or removal, break-glass, a risk-increasing version, a new delegation, a
    new connection, going live, a newly connected client, and a lost notification channel are
    `safety` notices with the text key `account_changed`, sent to the recipients spec §3.2 names
    (identity spec ID-14, §5.2, §8.3, §10.1; DEC-439 item 5). They are `safety`, not `info`,
    because they are the only out-of-band signal of a takeover or a sock-puppet grant, and `info`
    would wait for the brief.
28. **One notice per cause** (round 1, M3). A user's kill switch is one notice, caused by its
    `OwnerCommandIssued`, however many stream owners journal `KillSwitchActivated` for it; those are
    effects, and are de-duplicated on the command's id. A subject that fits two kinds takes the first
    in the catalogue's order (spec §3.4).
29. **Who receives what** (round 1, M2; settlement X1). The identity spec's receive column decides
    which roles may receive each class; the catalogue's recipients only narrow it. Until it merges,
    the interim reading of spec §3.3 holds: viewers never receive `action` or `safety`, and auditors
    and billing admins receive nothing pushed.

### Proposed for the founder

Until each is decided, the most conservative option holds: `cli_inbox` only, no vendor, no spend.

19. **Channel staging (HLD §12 item 6).** DEC-19 already fixed v1's channels (web push, email, one
    chat; SMS and phone next; native mobile later). Recommendation: email and one chat channel at
    M7, as the milestone says; `web_inbox` with the web app at M9; web push with the relay at M10;
    SMS and phone under E8-7. Mark HLD §12 item 6 decided.
20. **Which chat channel at M7.** Recommendation: Slack through an incoming webhook into a channel
    the owner owns. It needs no platform credential, no vendor contract, and no spend, and nothing
    flows back. Telegram (a platform bot) at M10 for retail.
21. **Email provider.** Recommendation: at M7, the owner's own SMTP submission account, its credential
    in the vault, paper only, with no vendor and no spend. At M10, a transactional email vendor
    chosen for: DKIM on our domain, open and click tracking that can be turned off, signed
    webhooks, short message retention, and US data residency. Because payloads carry no content
    (NT-1), the choice is low-risk and can be reversed by swapping one adapter.
22. **Web push and relay hosting.** No vendor: browser push services charge nothing. The relay runs
    in the global control plane from M8, on the hosting DEC-434 item 13 chooses.
23. **Sending domain.** Recommendation: a notification subdomain of the product domain with SPF,
    DKIM, and DMARC `p=reject`. The founder controls the domain's DNS.
24. **Email footer and unsubscribe wording.** The `[[EMAIL-FOOTER]]` placeholder, and whether
    `action` and `safety` mail count as transactional for unsubscribe purposes under email law, are
    for counsel and the founder (DEC-79, legal text). Recommendation: treat them as transactional;
    keep `List-Unsubscribe` on the brief only.
25. **An owner nobody can reach.** Recommendation: a live agent whose owners have had no reachable
    push channel for 24 hours holds new openings, with exits and protection untouched, until an
    owner re-verifies a channel. It only tightens, but it is a new restriction and needs a mandate
    spec change, so it is proposed and not built. Paper agents are unaffected.
26. **Mobile access to on-site approval services (HLD §12 item 7).** Recommendation for v1: approvers
    of hybrid and on-prem deployments reach the customer's own workspace app over the customer's
    network or VPN. No relay content proxy with end-to-end encryption in v1; revisit with the
    native app.

## Rationale

Mandate spec §6.4, the M7 brief, `AGENTS.md` rule 6, and 09-product-experience already fix the hard
rules: an opaque payload, a grant only inside the workspace, timeout to `skip`, quiet hours for push
only, and risk-limit alerts that ignore them. Items 1 to 18 apply those rules to every notice and
every channel, each time taking the reading that sends less, keeps the trade path independent of
delivery, or keeps an alert from being lost. None loosens a spec, an invariant, or a non-negotiable,
and each can be reversed by a later decision.

Items 19 to 26 choose vendors, spend money, set legal wording, or add a restriction, which DEC-79
reserves for the founder.

## Alternatives considered

- **Kind-specific texts** ("A kill switch was used", "A risk limit was reached"). Rejected: they tell
  the provider and the lock screen what happened to the account. Three texts tell them only that
  something did.
- **Approve from a chat button or an email reply.** Rejected: the approval would be decided outside
  the workspace deployment, by whoever controls the chat or mail account, without step-up (rule 6,
  HLD flow C step 5).
- **Magic sign-in links.** Rejected: they turn a compromised mail account into a compromised
  workspace.
- **Dropping duplicate safety alerts in a storm.** Rejected: coalescing bounds the messages without
  losing any subject (NT-6).
- **Deferring quiet-hours approvals to the window's end.** Rejected: the request may expire first,
  and mandate spec §6.4 says suppressed.
- **Routing every channel through the relay.** Rejected: it would make the global control plane a
  dependency of every alert. Only web push and native push need it.
