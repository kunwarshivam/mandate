On Robinhood, a sell's fill has no published price. The order record carries its filled quantity
alone, the position is gone or reads zero, and no activity feed exists (connections spec U-R8;
the contract's `orders[]` fields are unpublished). DEC-1151 books an entry's fill from the record
and the position's average buy price. That works only for a buy on a flat ledger, and DEC-1151
item 4 forbids inventing a price. So the first sell, R0's protective stop or an owner exit, is
never booked. The ledger still holds the share the broker no longer does. The reconciliation pauses
the agent, and the next start fails closed (DEC-1151, DEC-1152). That is safe, but the
platform's books stay wrong, and nothing returns the agent to a correct state.

## Decision (proposed)

### The booking

1. **Every Robinhood sell's fill is booked at its order's limit price**, the worst price the order
   allowed, with no owner step. A stop-limit's price is its limit price, never its stop trigger.
   The reconciliation books it from the broker's word, as DEC-1151 books an entry, when all of
   these hold:
   - The order is ours: a record carrying our `ref_id`, or carrying no `ref_id` and the broker
     order id the Robinhood connector holds for our key, the id the place answered, which it
     cancels by and restores from the journal (DEC-1150 item 7).
   - It is a sell that reduces risk: `protective`, `risk_exit`, `owner_exit` or
     `discretionary_exit`.
   - Its broker record reports a cumulative filled quantity above what is booked for it and no
     more than the order's quantity.
   - The ledger holds the instrument long at least the increment, with no unattributed fill.
   - The broker lists the position reduced by exactly the increment since the last booking.
   - The broker record's `limit_price` equals the limit the journal holds for the order, and for
     a stop-limit its `stop_price` equals the journaled stop. An order can be edited in the
     Robinhood app, so a record whose prices differ from the journal's, or that carries none, is
     not the order we placed: it derives nothing (item 3), and the agent pauses as on `main`.
     Booking at the journaled limit when the broker's limit is lower would overstate proceeds.

   Each increment is booked at the limit, under the id `limit:<client order id>:<cumulative
   quantity>`. The same record read again gives the same id, so the executor's fold books it once
   (as DEC-1151 item 2 does for `record:` ids), and the reconciliation books it before it compares
   positions (DEC-1151 item 3).
2. **A sell with no limit price is never booked, and is never denied.** A market sell, a
   stop-market, or any exit whose request carries no limit is sent as rule 13 requires: no exit is
   ever refused for this. What is refused is only the booking of its fill, under the reason
   `fill_price_unbounded`. The position is then unexplained, so the next reconciliation pauses the
   agent after the fill, as on `main`. On Robinhood this does not arise: its profile's only forms are
   limit openings and the one stop-limit protection (trading spec §5.2, DEC-529 item 2), and every
   exit, an owner exit included, is a limit on the exit price ladder (§5.6). The rule keeps it
   closed if a later profile adds an unlimited form.
3. **Anything else derives nothing**, as DEC-1151 item 4 says for entries: a record that is not
   ours, a purpose that does not reduce risk, a quantity that does not fit, a ledger that is not
   long, a position that is not reduced by exactly the increment. The position stays unexplained,
   and the reconciliation pauses the agent as today.

### The better price Robinhood pays

4. **The bound per order.** A sell limit fills at its limit or better, so Robinhood's settled cash
   can later exceed the ledger's model, which booked the limit. Without a rule, DEC-146's band
   would count every improved sale as a cash mismatch and pause on the second run. Each order
   booked at its limit carries a bound q × (b − limit), where q is the quantity booked at the limit
   and b is the highest bid among the risk-mark quotes (trading spec §8.2: 0 < bid ≤ ask, spread
   within the profile's limit, fresh at their `as_of`) in the window:
   - The window opens at the `GateDecided` that admitted the order, the one its `OrderSubmitted`
     references, so the quote the order was admitted on counts. It closes at the first broker
     record showing any of the order filled. The quotes are those carried in the account stream's
     `GateDecided.quotes_used` (bid, ask, `as_of`, feed; journal spec §9.5).
   - For a stop-limit, b is also capped at the stop trigger: the order rests until the price
     falls to the stop, so a bid above it at placement says nothing about the fill.
   - With no counting quote, or b ≤ the limit, the bound is 0, and any improvement on that order
     is a mismatch as today.
   - b bounds what can be explained, not what Robinhood pays. An improvement beyond the bound is
     real money the platform cannot explain, and it is a mismatch as today.
5. **Explaining it, once.** At each reconciliation:
   - Both sides are **settled cash**: the broker's settled cash and the model's settled cash,
     each before reservations. Unsettled proceeds are compared on neither side.
   - d = broker settled cash − model settled cash − the improvements explained since the last
     `AccountSnapshotRecorded` re-anchor. A re-anchor sets the model to the broker's figure, so it
     ends the sum: what was explained before it is in the anchor and is never counted again. It
     also ends every bound of an order booked before it: whatever improvement that order earned is
     in the anchor, so its unused bound can never explain a later, unrelated d.
   - The **previous reconciliation** is the latest reconciliation journaled for the account before
     this one, whatever its result; with none since the last re-anchor, it is the re-anchor. A
     skipped run or a restart only widens the window the credit check reads, never narrows it.
   - An improvement is explained only when 0 < d, d is no more than the sum of the remaining
     bounds, and no other credit can be in d: no `DividendPaid`, `CashInLieuPosted`, deposit or
     interest credit is journaled for the account between the previous reconciliation and this one.
     A run with such a credit explains nothing, and its difference is judged by the band as today.
   - The explained amount is allocated to the orders with a remaining bound in order of their
     `OrderSubmitted` seq, each up to its remaining bound. The record lists each order's share.
     Remaining bounds are folded from those records alone, never kept beside them.
   - A bound expires at its order's settlement date plus 2 trading days. An improvement that has not
     appeared by then is not one Robinhood paid on that order.
   - **A sell that filled worse than its limit is never booked at the limit nor explained.** A
     limit cannot legitimately fill below it, so a negative d while any order booked at its limit
     still holds a bound means the booking overstated proceeds: it pauses the agent at once, with
     no in-band tolerance (rule 3; stricter than DEC-146's band, a DEC-176 tightening). A
     position-reduction mismatch pauses as item 3 says.
   - Everything else is a mismatch, as today: d above the remaining bounds, a negative d outside
     the band with no bound open, a run with another credit. It pauses on the second run
     (DEC-146).
6. **What remains, accepted.** A positive d inside the bounds can still net a real improvement
   against an unpublished debit (a fee, an adjustment), or absorb a credit the journal does not
   record. That is accepted, and bounded: the cash *shown* can be wrong by at most the remaining
   bounds, which expire, and no risk figure reads it (item 7). The test
   `an_unrelated_credit_inside_the_bound_is_not_explained` pins the credits the journal does record.
7. **No risk figure ever reads an explained improvement.** It is credited to cash for
   presentation only: the cash shown and the P&L shown. It is its own type, in a crate that
   `xtask/layers.toml` places above every risk and accounting crate, so `mandate-spec`,
   `mandate-risk`, `mandate-accounting` and `mandate-executor`'s gate cannot import it. Every risk
   measure keeps the sale at its limit price: E, H, E₀, C, N and L; the drawdown ladder, the daily
   loss and the lifetime floor; the retirement carry; goal completion; sizing; buying power and the
   gate's cash checks; and the surveillance and aggregate reports. A reader not listed keeps the
   limit price until a decision moves it. So an improvement can never lift a limit, release a rung,
   enlarge an order, complete a goal, lighten a carry or add buying power.
8. **Replay.** The bookings at the limit and the explained improvements, with their per-order
   shares, are journaled records, so the risk figures, the remaining bounds and the cash shown are
   all folded from the journal alone.

### Tests, and what this needs

9. **Every "never" and "always" has a named test with an independent oracle, each shown to catch
   a seeded bug:**
   - `a_sell_is_booked_at_its_limit_once_under_any_read_order`: generated orders of record reads,
     partial fills and restarts; a separate accumulator sums the booked increments. Seeded bugs:
     the stop booked instead of the limit; an increment booked twice.
   - `a_record_whose_prices_differ_from_the_journal_derives_nothing`: an order edited in the app,
     a lower limit or another stop, and a record with no price. Seeded bug: booking at the
     journaled limit when the record's limit is lower.
   - `an_unlimited_exit_is_sent_and_its_fill_is_not_booked` and
     `the_agent_pauses_after_an_unbooked_fill`: item 2, both halves.
   - `anything_else_derives_nothing`: over item 3's cases.
   - `ours_is_the_ref_id_or_the_held_broker_id`: DEC-1150 item 7's cases, with a lookalike id.
   - `an_improvement_within_its_bound_is_explained_and_beyond_it_pauses`: the oracle recomputes
     each order's bound from the journaled quotes and the window itself. Seeded bugs: the admitting
     gate's quote left out; a post-fill quote counted; the ask used for the bid; a stale or crossed
     quote counted; a stop-limit's bid not capped at the stop.
   - `a_repeated_run_explains_an_improvement_once`: runs repeated before and after a re-anchor,
     including a re-anchor before any improvement is explained. Seeded bugs: d computed without
     the improvements already explained; a bound left open across a re-anchor.
   - `a_fill_worse_than_the_limit_is_a_mismatch_not_an_improvement`: a negative d inside DEC-146's
     band while a bound is open pauses at once. Seeded bug: a negative d counted as an
     improvement, or accepted within the band.
   - `the_previous_reconciliation_spans_a_skipped_run_and_a_restart`: a credit journaled between
     a skipped run and this one still blocks the explanation.
   - `an_unrelated_credit_inside_the_bound_is_not_explained`: a dividend, cash in lieu, a deposit
     and interest, each inside the remaining bounds. Seeded bug: the credit check dropped.
   - `a_bound_expires_two_trading_days_after_settlement`.
   - `remaining_bounds_fold_from_the_journal_alone`: replay against a live run, allocation by
     `OrderSubmitted` seq. Seeded bug: allocation by fill time.
   - `no_risk_figure_reads_an_improvement`: over item 7's list. The oracle computes each measure
     with every sale at its limit and asserts equality. Seeded bug: buying power reading the cash
     shown.
   - `perturbing_explained_improvements_changes_no_risk_figure`: any generated journal, with
     explained-improvement records added, removed or rescaled at random, gives identical risk
     figures.
   - The fuzz varies stale, crossed, wide, last-trade-only and missing quotes; settlement timing;
     partial and whole fills; cash snapshots present or absent; re-anchors; repeated runs;
     dividends, cash in lieu, deposits and interest inside the window; orders edited in the app;
     and bounds reaching expiry.
10. **What this needs, in order:**
    - a journal spec PR (protected, ES-22): the explained-improvement record with its per-order
      shares, the `limit:` fill id, and the rules above;
    - the trading spec's §11 cash row: settled cash on both sides, the band read against the model
      plus the improvements explained since the last re-anchor;
    - the presentation type's crate and its `xtask/layers.toml` row;
    - then DEC-77 pairs in `mandate-executor` (the booking at the limit, the improvement and the
      band), with zero missed mutants;
    - and a line in the R0 runbook: a Robinhood sell books at its limit, and the cash shown may
      rise later as the improvement settles.

## Rationale

The limit is the one price the platform knows the order could not have done worse than, so
booking it understates proceeds and never flatters a limit. The founder chose it over an owner
attestation for that reason. What remains is reconciliation hygiene: Robinhood will often pay
better, and an improvement bounded by the quotes the run itself saw is explainable without
trusting anyone's figure. Keeping every risk measure at the limit means even an explained
improvement changes nothing the agent's limits read.

## Alternatives considered

- **An owner-attested price, bounded and kept out of risk figures until verified** (this DEC's
  earlier draft). It is more accurate, but it adds a manual step to every sale and a shadow equity
  figure. The founder declined it.
- **Derive the price from the change in cash.** Settlement timing and other cash movements are
  unpublished (DEC-1151, U-R8), so the arithmetic is ambiguous, and rule 3 refuses ambiguity.
- **Add `get_equity_tax_lots` or a P&L tool to the allowlist.** Their fields are unpublished, and
  adding a tool changes the pinned contract (M2).
- **Treat every improvement as a mismatch.** It would pause on the second improved sale, for no
  risk.
- **Widen DEC-146's band instead.** It would hide real mismatches of the same size.
