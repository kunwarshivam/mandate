On Robinhood, a sell's fill has no published price. The order record carries its filled quantity
alone, the position is gone or reads zero, and no activity feed exists (connections spec U-R8;
the contract's `orders[]` fields are unpublished). DEC-1151 books an entry's fill from the record
and the position's average buy price. That works only for a buy on a flat ledger, and DEC-1151
item 4 forbids inventing a price. So the first sell, R0's protective stop or an owner exit, is
never booked. The ledger still holds the share the broker no longer does. The reconciliation pauses
the agent, and the next start fails closed (DEC-1151, DEC-1152). That is safe, but the
platform's books stay wrong, and nothing returns the agent to a correct state.

## Decision (proposed)

### The booking

1. **Every Robinhood sell's fill is booked at its order's limit price**, the worst price the order
   allowed, with no owner step. A stop-limit's price is its limit price, never its stop trigger.
   The reconciliation books it from the broker's word, as DEC-1151 books an entry, when all of
   these hold:
   - The order is ours: a record carrying our `ref_id`, or carrying no `ref_id` and the broker
     order id the Robinhood connector holds for our key, the id the place answered, which it
     cancels by and restores from the journal (DEC-1150 item 7).
   - It is a sell that reduces risk: `protective`, `risk_exit`, `owner_exit` or
     `discretionary_exit`.
   - Its broker record reports a cumulative filled quantity above what is booked for it and no
     more than the order's quantity.
   - The ledger holds the instrument long at least the increment, with no unattributed fill.
   - The broker lists the position reduced by exactly the increment since the last booking.

   Each increment is booked at the limit, under the id `limit:<client order id>:<cumulative
   quantity>`. The same record read again gives the same id, so the executor's fold books it once
   (as DEC-1151 item 2 does for `record:` ids), and the reconciliation books it before it compares
   positions (DEC-1151 item 3).
2. **A sell with no limit price is never booked.** A market sell, a stop-market, or any exit
   whose request carries no limit is refused `fill_price_unbounded`. Nothing is booked, and the
   agent stays paused as on `main`. On Robinhood this does not arise: its profile's only forms are
   limit openings and the one stop-limit protection (trading spec §5.2, DEC-529 item 2), and every
   exit, an owner exit included, is a limit on the exit price ladder (§5.6). The rule keeps it
   closed if a later profile adds an unlimited form.
3. **Anything else derives nothing**, as DEC-1151 item 4 says for entries: a record that is not
   ours, a purpose that does not reduce risk, a quantity that does not fit, a ledger that is not
   long, a position that is not reduced by exactly the increment. The position stays unexplained,
   and the reconciliation pauses the agent as today.

### The better price Robinhood pays

4. **The improvement.** A sell limit fills at its limit or better, so Robinhood's settled cash
   can later exceed the ledger's model, which booked the limit. Without a rule, DEC-146's band
   would count every improved sale as a cash mismatch and pause on the second run. The rule:
   - **The bound per order.** Its explainable improvement is at most q × (b − limit), where q is
     the quantity booked at the limit and b is the highest bid among the quotes journaled for the
     instrument between the order's `OrderSubmitted` and the first broker record showing any of
     it filled. Those quotes are carried in the account stream's `GateDecided.quotes_used` (bid,
     ask, `as_of`, feed; journal spec §9.5). Only quotes that were risk-mark quality at their
     `as_of` count (trading spec §8.2: 0 < bid ≤ ask, spread within the profile's limit, fresh).
     With no counting quote, or b ≤ the limit, the bound is 0.
   - **Explaining it.** At each reconciliation, the cash difference d = broker cash − model cash
     is compared with the unexplained bounds of the orders booked at their limit. If
     0 < d ≤ that sum, d is journaled as an explained sale improvement, allocated to those orders
     oldest first, each up to its bound, and each order's remaining bound shrinks by its share. The
     band check then reads the model plus the explained improvements, so an improved sale never
     counts toward DEC-146's pause.
   - **Everything else is a mismatch, as today.** A d above the remaining bounds, or a negative d
     outside the band, is an unexplained difference and pauses on the second run (DEC-146).
     Robinhood credits settled cash at T+1, so an improvement explained at a later run is
     explained then. One that never appears is never explained, and nothing is lost: the books
     already hold the limit.
5. **No risk figure ever reads an explained improvement.** It is credited to cash for
   presentation only: the cash shown and the P&L shown. Every risk measure keeps the sale at its
   limit price: E, H, E₀, C, N and L; the drawdown ladder, the daily loss and the lifetime floor;
   the retirement carry; goal completion; sizing; buying power and the gate's cash checks; and the
   surveillance and aggregate reports. A reader not listed keeps the limit price until a decision
   moves it. So an improvement can never lift a limit, release a rung, enlarge an order, complete a
   goal, lighten a carry or add buying power.
6. **Replay.** The bookings at the limit and the explained improvements are journaled records,
   so both the risk figures and the cash shown are folded from the journal alone.

### Tests, and what this needs

7. **Every "never" and "always" has a named test with an independent oracle, each shown to catch
   a seeded bug:**
   - `a_sell_is_booked_at_its_limit_once_under_any_read_order`: generated orders of record reads,
     partial fills and restarts; a separate accumulator sums the booked increments. Seeded bugs:
     the stop booked instead of the limit; an increment booked twice.
   - `a_sale_without_a_limit_is_never_booked`.
   - `anything_else_derives_nothing`: over item 3's cases.
   - `ours_is_the_ref_id_or_the_held_broker_id`: DEC-1150 item 7's cases, with a lookalike id.
   - `an_improvement_within_its_bound_is_explained_and_beyond_it_pauses`: the oracle recomputes
     each order's bound from the journaled quotes and the window itself. Seeded bugs: a post-fill
     quote counted; the ask used for the bid; a stale or crossed quote counted.
   - `no_risk_figure_reads_an_improvement`: over item 5's list. The oracle computes each measure
     with every sale at its limit and asserts equality. Seeded bug: buying power reading the cash
     shown.
   - The fuzz varies stale, crossed, wide, last-trade-only and missing quotes, settlement timing,
     partial and whole fills, and cash snapshots present or absent.
8. **What this needs, in order:**
   - a journal spec PR (protected, ES-22): the explained-improvement record and its rules, the
     `limit:` fill id;
   - the trading spec's §11 cash row: the band read against the model plus explained
     improvements;
   - then DEC-77 pairs in `mandate-executor` (the booking at the limit, the improvement and the
     band), with zero missed mutants;
   - and a line in the R0 runbook: a Robinhood sell books at its limit, and the cash shown may
     rise later as the improvement settles.

## Rationale

The limit is the one price the platform knows the order could not have done worse than, so
booking it understates proceeds and never flatters a limit. The founder chose it over an owner
attestation for that reason. What remains is reconciliation hygiene: Robinhood will often pay
better, and an improvement bounded by the quotes the run itself saw is explainable without
trusting anyone's figure. Keeping every risk measure at the limit means even an explained
improvement changes nothing the agent's limits read.

## Alternatives considered

- **An owner-attested price, bounded and kept out of risk figures until verified** (this DEC's
  earlier draft). It is more accurate, but it adds a manual step to every sale and a shadow equity
  figure. The founder declined it.
- **Derive the price from the change in cash.** Settlement timing and other cash movements are
  unpublished (DEC-1151, U-R8), so the arithmetic is ambiguous, and rule 3 refuses ambiguity.
- **Add `get_equity_tax_lots` or a P&L tool to the allowlist.** Their fields are unpublished, and
  adding a tool changes the pinned contract (M2).
- **Treat every improvement as a mismatch.** It would pause on the second improved sale, for no
  risk.
- **Widen DEC-146's band instead.** It would hide real mismatches of the same size.
