## Context

A connection's token exchange and executor log in with OpenBao's Kubernetes auth, each with a role
made for that connection and bound to its ServiceAccount (DEC-2060). One templated policy per kind
covers every connection, so no policy is written per connection; but each role is. DEC-2630 item 7
gives that power to one dedicated provisioner process, and `mandate-vault` already has its policy
(`provisioner_policy`) and each role's request (`role_request`). Nothing calls either today: neither
`mandate vault provision` nor staging's apply makes a Kubernetes role, so every exchange's and every
managed executor's login is refused, and the staging connect fails at the exchange.

## Decision

1. **The role provisioner** is a workspace process (`provisioner`), with an AppRole of its own whose
   policy is `provisioner_policy` and nothing else, and egress to the vault alone. It follows the
   control stream. On a version-2 `ConnectionRequested` with `credential` `oauth` it makes the
   connection's two roles, the executor's and the token exchange's (`role_request`), and journals
   `ConnectionRolesMade`; if the vault refuses a role or cannot be reached in time, it journals
   `ConnectionRolesRefused` and raises the opaque operator alert `connection_roles_refused`, a fixed
   code with no subject and its runbook (`AGENTS.md` rule 6). It is restart-safe: it folds the
   stream and finishes any step a crash left half done, and making a role that exists, or removing
   one that does not, changes nothing.
2. **The exchange waits for the roles** (journal spec §9.21, v0.77). The deployment manager starts a
   request's token exchange only once it has read the `ConnectionRolesMade` that names the request.
   With a refusal, or no record by the deadline, nothing starts and the connect ends by its
   deadline; nothing waits in the API, since the code waits in the vault for the exchange.
3. **Cleanup.** When the request closes without an establishment (`ConnectionRefused` with occasion
   `connect`), the provisioner removes both roles. On a `ConnectionRevoked` it removes the
   exchange's role at once, and the executor's only once no generation of the request's account's
   executor that a `ProcessStartIssued` started is left without its `ProcessStopIssued`. An executor
   still running, a kill switch's to apply among them (DEC-2343, DEC-2344), so keeps its role and can
   always log in again; the revocation destroys its token instead. The provisioner reads the control
   stream alone: it is not given the account streams, whose `KillSwitchActivated` would say sooner
   that a switch was applied. A connect the API established keeps both roles for the connection's
   life. Each removal is journaled as `ConnectionRolesRemoved`.
4. **The records** (journal spec §9.33): `ConnectionRolesMade`, `ConnectionRolesRefused` and
   `ConnectionRolesRemoved` at schema version 1, rule 261 (a system actor and a cause), and stream
   rules 262 and 263 (each record tied to its request; a removal after the roles were made, each
   role once, on its occasion, and the executor's only once its executor is not running).
5. **The provisioner's added `delete`.** `provisioner_policy` already names
   `auth/<kubernetes mount>/role/+` with `create`, `update` and `read`, every parameter but the
   ServiceAccount's name pinned to its one value: that `+` is DEC-2630 item 7's and predates this
   decision. This decision adds only `delete` on those same two paths, for the cleanup of item 3. The
   provisioner's `+` stays on exactly the executor's and the token exchange's Kubernetes mounts; it
   never reaches another mount, a policy, a token, a secret, or `sys/`, and a test pins it.
6. **Where it lands.** This spec change first, alone (ES-22). Then the vault side, coordinated with
   lane V2: the provisioner identity and AppRole, `delete` in `provisioner_policy`, and making and
   removing a connection's roles. Then the journal crate's three records; the process; its chart
   (vault-only egress, to lane GO's network-policy pattern); and the manager's wait.

## Rationale

It builds the founder's design as decided, with the one missing piece: an order, so the exchange
never starts before its role exists, and a journaled outcome, so a refusal is seen and fails closed.
Keeping the executor's role while its executor runs keeps rule 13's kill switch reachable after a
revocation, at the cost of a role that may outlive its connection, which grants only a read of a
token the revocation destroyed.

## Alternatives considered

- **One templated role bound to every ServiceAccount in the namespace.** Rejected: it drops the
  per-role binding DEC-2060 and DEC-2630 item 7 chose.
- **The exchange retries its login until its role appears.** Rejected: it races, and a refusal would
  be indistinguishable from a slow provisioner.
- **The provisioner reads the account streams to remove the executor's role as soon as the switch is
  applied.** Rejected: it gives a process that should hold nothing read access to every account's
  positions and orders.

## Risks

- A held generation (DEC-2344) ends by its own exit, which the manager does not journal, so its
  executor's role may stay after the connection is revoked. A sweep of roles whose connection is
  revoked and whose executor is gone is a backlog row.
- Until the process lands, nothing makes the roles, so no OAuth connect can complete; that is today's
  state, recorded as a staging blocker.
