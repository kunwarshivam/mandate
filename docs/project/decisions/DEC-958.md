## Context

From DEC-957 on, a live run binds only its own live workspace's registrations. It then stops at
`a fee schedule for the mandate's environment`. The only schedule a registered fee artifact could
name was `conservative_v1`, the paper-only overestimate (`PAPER_ONLY`, "Proposed (founder)" as
values), which refuses every environment but `paper` (DEC-526 item 4). So no fee schedule existed
that a live run could be priced by.

## Decision

1. **The figures.** A live Robinhood US equity account is charged zero commission, the SEC Section
   31 fee and the FINRA TAF on sells, and the CAT fee on buys and sells, at these rates. Each was
   read from its source on 2026-10-16:

   | Fee | Rate | Source and effective date |
   |---|---|---|
   | Commission | 0 | Robinhood Financial Standard Pricing Fee Schedule, revised 2026-09-29: "Commission-free trading refers to $0 commissions" for U.S. listed equities and ETFs. <https://cdn.robinhood.com/assets/robinhood/legal/RHF+Fee+Schedule.pdf> |
   | SEC Section 31 (sells) | 0.0000206 × proceeds | SEC Fee Rate Advisory for fiscal 2026 (published 2026-02-27): "$20.60 per million dollars" for covered sales with charge dates on or after 2026-04-04, until 60 days after the fiscal 2027 appropriation is enacted. <https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/fee-rate-advisories/2026-2>. Robinhood posts the same rate, effective 2026-04-04 |
   | FINRA TAF (sells) | 0.000195 a share, capped at 9.79 per execution | FINRA By-Laws Schedule A, Section 1, as restored from 2027-01-01 by SR-FINRA-2026-021 (Exhibit 5): "$0.000195 per share for each sale of a covered equity security, with a maximum charge of $9.79 per trade". <https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/SR-FINRA-2026-021.pdf>. Robinhood posts the same rate, effective 2026-01-01 |
   | CAT (buys and sells) | 0.000003 a share | Robinhood's schedule above: "$0.000003 per share of listed equities", with no side named |

   **Two readings are the conservative ones.** (a) SR-FINRA-2026-021 sets TAF to $0.00 for
   transactions from 2026-10-01 to 2026-12-31. Robinhood still posts $0.000195, so the schedule
   keeps the posted rate through the pause as an upper bound. (b) CAT is booked on buys as well
   as sells, because Robinhood names no side. A buy therefore books CAT and nothing else, not
   zero, which is a departure from the coordinator's "a buy books zero". Booking zero would
   understate a buy of 1,667 shares or more, where Robinhood's nearest-cent rounding charges at
   least a cent. For R0's one share at about 85 it books 0.000003, which the day charge rounds
   up to 0.01, a cent more than Robinhood's own figure (zero, since it rounds a sub-cent CAT
   down to 0).

   The cap basis is `per_execution`. Robinhood: "The limit on this fee is based on execution of
   your order, which can occur in parts." It is also §6.2's default, and it charges at least as
   much as `per_order`. Every rate is the published figure exactly, and each is representable
   exactly in its fee type, so no figure is rounded at all, down or up.

2. **Where the figures live.** `config/fees/robinhood-live-2026-10-16.toml` is the dated
   transcription, with each source quoted beside its figure. `mandate-executor`'s
   `ROBINHOOD_LIVE` holds the same figures, and `the_live_schedule_is_the_transcription_figure_for_figure`
   pins the two together. `robinhood_live_fee_config(environment, broker, calendar, first_day)`
   refuses any environment but `live` (`environment_mismatch`) and any broker but `robinhood`
   (`non_canonical_payload`, field `broker`). The crypto figures are never charged, because a
   live run's artifacts admit US equities only; they repeat the paper-only overestimate, 250 bps.

3. **The registered object.** It is `config/fees/robinhood-live-2026-10-16.json`, with exactly
   four members: `{"broker":"robinhood","effective_from":"2026-04-06","environment":"live","schedule":"robinhood_live_2026_10_16"}`.
   `effective_from` is the first trading day on which every figure is in force. The shell's fee
   reader takes the members from the `schedule`: the live schedule needs these four, and every
   other schedule needs the paper artifact's three, so every paper artifact reads and refuses
   exactly as before. DEC-957 item 3's check still follows: the schedule's `environment` must be
   the mandate's. As a result, a paper run refuses the live object in two ways. As registered, it
   names `live`, so it fails the environment check. Renamed to `paper`, `robinhood_live_fee_config`
   itself refuses it. A live run refuses the paper object, as before.

4. **Rounding.** The fold books each fee at full precision and charges the day's total as
   `round(total, 2, ceiling)` (§6.2, unchanged). A property test checks that a live fill's day
   charge is exactly the exact regulatory cost rounded up to the cent, against an integer oracle
   of the published rates. It is therefore never below the true cost, and for a buy (R0's one
   order) never below Robinhood's own rounded figure.

   **Not closed here:** Robinhood rounds each fee of a trade separately: SEC up to the cent, and
   TAF and CAT to the nearest cent. For a sell, its total can exceed the platform's one rounded
   day total by under two cents a fill. For example, 30 shares sold at 85: Robinhood charges
   0.06 + 0.01 = 0.07, and the platform charges ceil(0.05847) = 0.06. A test pins the bound.
   Closing the gap needs a per-fill, per-fee ceiling in the accounting fold, which changes §6.2,
   a safety-critical accounting rule with reference cases. Under ES-22 that is a spec PR first,
   so it is the follow-up R10b in the rehearsal's blocker table. R0's one order is a buy and is
   not affected.

5. **Revisions.** When a source changes (TAF resumes on 2027-01-01, a fiscal 2027 SEC rate, a new
   Robinhood schedule), a new dated file, constant and schedule name are added. This one is never
   edited, so a journaled `config_refs` hash keeps naming the figures it was charged by.

## Rationale

The fee schedule is what a live account's cost, reservation and reconciliation rest on.
Transcribing the brokers' posted figures with sources keeps the platform's cost honest. Taking
the higher reading where sources disagree keeps it from looking cheaper than the account is
(rule 3), and binding the schedule to one environment and one broker keeps paper runs and live
runs apart, as DEC-952 and DEC-957 do for every other object.

## Alternatives considered

- **Price a live run from the paper-only overestimate:** refused by DEC-526 item 4. The figures
  are ten times the real ones, and reconciliation compares against the broker's posted fees.
- **TAF at $0.00 during FINRA's pause:** cheaper on paper, but Robinhood still posts the rate, so
  the platform could understate what the account is charged.
- **No CAT on buys ("a buy books zero"):** understates large buys. R0's one share is charged a
  cent above Robinhood's figure instead.
- **A per-fill, per-fee ceiling now:** this is the right closure of item 4's gap, but it changes
  accounting spec §6.2 and its reference cases, so it needs its own spec PR (ES-22). It does not
  block R0's buy.
- **Reference cases for the live rates:** the trading-domain reference cases use test values
  only ("Fee rates in `configs` are TEST VALUES ONLY"), so the live figures are pinned by the
  executor's tests against the dated file instead.

Spec §6.2 still says rates are "transcribed from Alpaca's fee schedule". Under this decision, a
live Robinhood account's rates are transcribed from Robinhood's. The one-line wording change goes
with R10b's spec PR.
