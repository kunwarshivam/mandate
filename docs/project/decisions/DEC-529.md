## Context

The founder decided on 2026-10-08, in the coordinator's session, that paper trading stays on
Alpaca paper ([DEC-502](DEC-502.md), [DEC-509](DEC-509.md)), and that before 2026-11-02 they want
**one real-money order on their own Robinhood account**, to prove the product thesis privately:
not public and not for customers. It goes through the same production path as the paper order:
a confirmed mandate version, the risk gate, journal before acting, and the Robinhood connector
(E7-6) in place of Alpaca's. Speed matters, and every safety gate stays: tests first (DEC-77), an
independent review on a different model, and zero missed mutants.

Several accepted rules stand in the way as written. Each item below names the rule, what it
narrows, the recommendation, and the risk the founder accepts with it. Every narrowing is for
**this one founder-run order** only; each rule stays in force for everything else.

## Decision

1. **One supervised live order, on the product path.** One opening order on the founder's own
   Robinhood agentic account (the account Robinhood marks `agentic_allowed`), through the same
   deployment path customers will use, with nothing built only for this order:
   - the runner is the paper path's deployment runner, built generic so that the environment
     and the broker come from the confirmed mandate and its connection record, not a second
     binary;
   - the **cap is the mandate's own envelope**: allocation and `max_order_usd`, set and
     confirmed by the founder (`AGENTS.md` rule 11; item 2);
   - the **confirmation is the product's approval flow**: the mandate's autonomy rules say
     `ask` for every opening, so the cycle builds the order, the gate passes it, and it waits
     for the founder's grant in the CLI inbox (mandate spec §6.1, `mandate-approval`), which
     shows exactly the order that will be sent;
   - the order is a limit buy in the regular session, long only, on the confirmed mandate's one
     instrument, through every gate: the confirmed version, the pinned model, the order builder,
     the risk gate with the US account rules, the account ledger, journal before acting, and
     the executor's idempotency.

   **What "one order" means** (the coordinator's recommended reading, under the founder's
   acceptance of "one supervised order"): one `place_equity_order` call that fills, wholly or
   partly. A further attempt is allowed only if every earlier attempt was refused before
   sending, or was cancelled with zero filled quantity, and the founder grants each attempt
   separately. An attempt whose answer was lost (`Unknown`, item 4) counts as filled until the
   founder reconciles it.

   This narrows `AGENTS.md` rule 8 ("Never place real orders") for this one order, granted by
   the founder. **Agents still never call Robinhood**, never hold or read the founder's
   credentials, and never run the live deployment; every agent test runs against fixtures and
   the simulated broker (DEC-441 item 11 stands).

   *Risk:* real money moves, bounded by the cap. A defect in the path could send an order the
   founder did not mean; the grant shows the exact order and is bound to its hash, and the cap
   is checked by the builder and the gate independently.

2. **Whole shares follow from data, not code.** Robinhood's profile ([DEC-531](DEC-531.md))
   allows fractional quantities only for market orders; our policy allows only limit openings.
   The builder intersects the two and sizes in whole shares. One share of the instrument must
   pass every limit at once, or the order rounds to zero shares and nothing is sent:
   - the eligibility floor's price floor (5 USD) and liquidity floors;
   - `max_order_usd`, `max_position_usd`, and `max_position_fraction` × the account equity E;
   - the limit price after the collar, plus the fee reservation, within all of those;
   - at least one whole share after rounding down.

   With 100 USD the order cannot be SPY. No rule is relaxed to fit any instrument.

   **The founder's answer (2026-10-08, relayed by the coordinator):** `max_order_usd` is
   100 USD, and the instrument is a cheap broad ETF priced under about 90 USD a share. The
   founder chose the cap and the class of instrument; the ticker is the founder's choice in the
   confirmed mandate, and this decision names none.

   *Risk:* at most one share near 90 USD is bought; the instrument class keeps single-name risk
   out. If the price drifts above what the cap covers, nothing is sent.

3. **The rules this order narrows, and only for it.** Each stays in force everywhere else, and
   each narrowing is a setting the product later tightens without rework.
   - **ES-23** (ADR-0001): "Only the paper trading and data hosts are compiled in; a `live` cargo
     feature is forbidden in CI and release checks. Live credentials will come only from the
     vault ... with a signed `live` build, a step-up-approved `AgentDeployed`, and the M13
     penetration test." Narrowing: the runner's `live` cargo feature adds Robinhood's published
     MCP host to the connector's allowlist. No check enforces ES-23's ban on a `live` feature
     today, so the brief's slice X1 adds `cargo xtask live-feature`: only the runner may declare
     a `live` feature, and no CI or release build may enable it except one compile-only job, so
     the feature cannot rot. A build test proves the default build holds no live host. The
     founder builds it locally from a commit on `main`. The OAuth token is session-only, in the
     connector process's memory (CN-1 already allows that), so no vault is needed for a
     supervised session. No signed build and no M13 test for this order.
   - **DEC-155 item 4 and mandate spec §6.1**: `cli_confirm` is allowed only in `paper`.
     Narrowing: `cli_confirm` is accepted as the step-up method for this live deployment's
     confirmation, deployment, and each grant, on the founder's own connection only. The method
     is already a field of the approval record, so E9-4's signed assertions replace it later
     without rework.
   - **V-001 and the paper brief's FT-10**: the default build stays paper only. A `live`
     mandate is accepted only on a Robinhood connection the founder recorded through the CLI
     (the first slice of E7-11's connection record, the component the product grows).
   - **The tracker's counsel line** ("Nothing trades live until this is answered"): narrowed
     for this order only, by the founder's call in item 9.
   - **FR-2.6** (1× buying power verified at connect and daily): the contract shows no margin
     field (U-R7), so for this order 1× rests on the founder's attestation (item 11) and on the
     gate's buying power, the lower of cash and the broker's figure.
   - **Trading spec §4.2** (`sip` profile required for live equities): narrowed by item 12's
     quote source for this order.
   - **DEC-124** (the simulated broker is Robinhood's required paper stage) **is kept, not
     narrowed**: the founder first runs the same live build against the simulated Robinhood
     server, and only then against Robinhood.

   **How the narrowings lapse.** The live build refuses to start unless the account is flat
   (DEC-470 item 1) and the connection record is unspent: the first `place_equity_order` that
   fills spends it (item 1's definition), so the same build cannot place a second filled order.
   Removing the `live` feature, or replacing it with the M8 path, is a follow-up story the
   coordinator adds to the backlog.

   *Risk:* a locally built, unsigned binary holds a live host and a live session. Mitigated by
   the founder building from `main`, the token living only in memory, and the spent connection
   record; not mitigated by a penetration test.

4. **DEC-441 item 10, idempotency.** `place_equity_order` takes `ref_id`, a client UUID by which
   Robinhood deduplicates; what a retry with the same `ref_id` returns is unknown. And
   `get_equity_orders` filters by `order_id`, `state`, `symbol`, `created_at_gte` and
   `placed_agent`, not by `ref_id`. Item 10 asked for both, so it is half met and returns to
   the founder. **Recommendation:** build the order path with `ref_id` as the idempotency key,
   derived deterministically from the journaled intent's idempotency key, so a restart
   recomputes the same value, and accept this recovery rule. It is written once, in shared
   reconciliation, for any profile that says "no query by client id" (DEC-531 item 2), and is
   allowed only when the account ledger shows the account dedicated to one agent:
   - After a lost or unreadable answer the order is `Unknown`. The connector **never re-sends**
     on its own, even with the same `ref_id`.
   - It lists the account's orders with `symbol` the intent's, `placed_agent` `agentic`, and
     `created_at_gte` the intent's `OrderSubmitted` time less a fixed margin, and adopts a
     record only if exactly one matches the intent on side, type, quantity, limit price and
     time in force (and on `ref_id`, if the records turn out to carry it).
   - Zero matches or several leave the order `Unknown` (DEC-176: when unsure, the reading that
     adds no risk). An `Unknown` order blocks every new order in that instrument (trading spec
     §5.3 rule 9) until the founder reconciles it by hand, and the next run is refused anyway
     while the account holds an order or a position (DEC-470 item 1).

   **Accepted, this resolves DEC-441 item 10 for this order only.** The customer path still
   needs Robinhood's written answer on U-R1 and U-R2, and item 10 stands for it. The brief's
   slices B2b (the list request and adoption in reconciliation) and C3 (its Robinhood mapping)
   implement this rule; B2b's and C1's implementations wait until this decision is Accepted.

   *Risk:* after a lost answer, an order Robinhood holds may stay `Unknown` until the founder
   looks in the app, and the instrument is blocked meanwhile. No duplicate is possible, since
   nothing re-sends.

5. **DEC-441 item 15** (whether one platform may act for many customers) **does not block an
   order on the founder's own account.** **Accepted, this resolves item 15 for this order
   only.** It still blocks every customer connection: no customer connects Robinhood in any
   environment until Robinhood's written answer is recorded.

   *Risk:* Robinhood's platform terms may not contemplate this use; the founder uses their own
   account under their own agreement with Robinhood.

6. **DEC-441 item 16, fixtures:** synthesized from the published tool contract only
   ([robinhood-contract.md](../tasks/robinhood-contract.md)). Nothing is recorded from a real
   account, the founder's included, for this path. The live run's own journal is evidence of
   the run, not a fixture.

   *Risk:* the response shapes are assumed from the contract (its output fields are not
   published), so the first real check of them is the founder's run with no place call (the
   brief's R0 step 4). An unexpected shape refuses or is `Unknown`, never a re-send.

7. **DEC-441 item 17, protection.** The contract has `stop_limit` with `time_in_force` `gtc`, and
   a `get_advanced_orders` listing that includes OCO groups, but no tool that places an OCO or a
   bracket. **Recommendation:** protection takes the strongest form the connection's profile
   offers (DEC-531 item 2); for Robinhood equities that is one resting GTC stop-limit sell for
   the whole position after the entry fills completely, as crypto does
   ([DEC-36](../04-decision-log.md#decisions)), with the take-profit left to the runtime. A
   mandate with protection off is refused for this run.

   *Risk:* a stop-limit may not fill if the price gaps through its limit. Robinhood's GTC
   expiry is not published, so the stop could lapse with no process running to re-place it;
   the founder checks the resting stop in the Robinhood app after the run and periodically
   while the position is held, and the runbook says so.

8. **DEC-441 item 20** (customer disclosures) **does not apply to the founder's own account.**
   It still blocks any customer connection.

   *Risk:* none to customers, since none connects.

9. **Counsel.** The tracker records that nothing trades live until counsel answers the adviser
   question (compliance questions 31 to 41). Whether counsel must review an order the founder
   places on their own account, with their own money, before it is placed is the **founder's
   call**. This decision states no legal conclusion and contains no legal text.

   **The founder's answer (2026-10-08, relayed by the coordinator):** counsel review is not
   required before this order on the founder's own account. Counsel still gates any customer
   use. This records the founder's call; it is not a legal conclusion.

   *Risk:* the founder's, as their own call.

10. **Credentials** (`AGENTS.md` rule 7). The founder's Robinhood OAuth session lives only on the
    founder's machine and in the memory of the one connector process for the run. It is never
    written to the repository, a log, the journal, an artifact, a fixture, or any agent's
    context, and the runner writes no token to disk: the founder logs in through the browser at
    each run. The account number is typed by the founder (the contract says it must come from
    the user, never defaulted from `get_accounts`), is held by reference, and is never logged or
    journaled in clear (journal spec §6.4).

    *Risk:* a token in process memory can be read by anything with access to the founder's
    machine while the run lasts; the runner disables core dumps.

11. **The account.** The founder's agentic account must be flat (no position, no open order)
    before the run (DEC-470 item 1), must not be shared with another agent (connections spec
    §6.3), and must hold no margin the platform cannot show is capped at 1× (FR-2.6). The
    contract shows no margin field (U-R7), so the founder attests, with `cli_confirm` when
    recording the connection, that the agentic account is a cash account or has margin
    disabled; the gate's buying power is the lower of cash and the broker's figure, as for
    Alpaca.

    *Risk:* a wrong attestation leaves margin available, but the gate never spends more than
    cash, so exposure stays at 1×.

12. **Market data.** Trading spec §4.2 requires the `sip` profile for live equity agents; the
    paper path uses Alpaca's free `iex` feed. **Recommendation:** for this order, the collar and
    the risk mark use Robinhood's own quote (`get_equity_quotes`, re-read by
    `review_equity_order`), which must agree with Alpaca's IEX quote within the collar or the
    run refuses; daily and minute bars stay Alpaca's, as on the paper path (IEX minute volume
    understates, so the participation caps only tighten). This needs a named spec change to
    §4.2. The alternative is Alpaca's paid SIP feed, which is spending and also the founder's.

    *Risk:* both quotes could be wrong in the same direction; the limit order and the collar
    bound the price paid.

13. **What does not change.** One order (item 1), then stop. A restart or a second run submits
    nothing. No customer connection, no crypto, no options, no extended hours, no market orders.
    No agent runs the live deployment, reads its output during the run, or receives the
    credential. Every safety-critical slice is a DEC-77 tests PR then implementation PR, reviewed
    on a different model, with zero missed mutants.

## Rationale

One real order on the founder's own money tests what paper cannot: Robinhood's real acceptance,
fills, fees, and settlement through the same cycle, gate and journal customers will use. Keeping
it to one capped limit order, sent only after the founder's grant in the approval flow, keeps the
loss bounded by the cap and the decision with the person whose money it is. Each narrowing is
the smallest that lets the order happen: no rule is relaxed for customers, and no agent gains
any route to Robinhood. Item 4 is the one real engineering risk: without a query by `ref_id`,
recovery after a lost answer depends on a match that is safe only on a dedicated, flat account,
so it refuses rather than guesses.

## Alternatives considered

- **A separate live binary with its own cap and typed prompt** (this decision's first draft):
  rejected by the founder on 2026-10-08 as code that needs rework; the mandate's envelope and
  the ASK approval flow already do both jobs.
- **Wait for the full M8 path** (connection manager, vault, signed build, OAuth, M13): the safe
  default. Rejected as the recommendation: it is months away and proves nothing more about the
  cycle.
- **Let an agent place the order through the Robinhood tools an agent session can see:**
  rejected. Rule 8 and DEC-441 item 11 forbid it, and it would bypass the gate and the journal.
- **Re-send with the same `ref_id` after a lost answer:** rejected for now; the contract does not
  say what a retry returns, so an answer could be misread. Revisit if Robinhood confirms the
  retry behaviour in writing.
- **A market order or a dollar-amount order** to buy a fraction of SPY under 100 USD: rejected;
  openings are limit orders (`AGENTS.md` rule 12).
- **No protection for one small order:** rejected; trading spec §5.4 protects every position,
  and a resting stop exists.

## Revisit when

The founder changes any item; Robinhood answers U-R1, U-R2 or U-R4 in writing; or the live order
is placed, after which the connection record is spent and every narrowing in item 3 lapses.
