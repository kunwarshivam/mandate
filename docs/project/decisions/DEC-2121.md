## Context

DEC-2108 item 2 makes a member's passkeys the fold of `CredentialEnrolled` and `CredentialRemoved`
over the control stream. Journal spec §9.31 leaves stream rule 251 to the writer: the API checks it
against its own fold before it appends, the verifier's fold fails closed on it, and "a member whose
records break it has no credential that counts". Three readers need the fold: the enrol route
(the writer's check, and the list), the step-up verifier, and DEC-2117's reduction-only opening,
which looks a passkey up by its id. §9.31 does not say where the fold lives, which members a
breaking record disables when it names another member's credential, or what the fold carries.

## Decision

1. **The fold is `mandate_passkey::credentials`.** It sits beside `stepup::consume`, which judges
   an assertion against an enrolled credential, and depends only on what `mandate-passkey` already
   does. It reads no clock and no store: `credential_record` maps one envelope, and `CredentialFold`
   folds the records in stream order on each read.
2. **A breaking record disables every member it touches.** A record breaks rule 251 unless it is
   its credential's first record and an enrolment, or its second and a removal by the member who
   enrolled it. A breaking record disables its own member and, when its credential was first
   enrolled by another member, that member too. From that record on, every read of theirs is
   `Broken` and none of their passkeys counts, so their step-up is `step_up_missing`. Disabling
   both sides of a collision only ever refuses a step-up; it never lets one through.
3. **What the fold answers.**
   - `passkeys(member)`: the member's enrolled, unremoved credentials, in enrolment order.
   - `held(credential)`: the enrolment that holds an id, and its member.
   - `admits(next)`: the writer's rule 251 check. An id ever enrolled on the stream, removed or
     not, is refused, which is DEC-2118 item 5's reused id.
4. **What the fold carries, and does not.** Each enrolment keeps its `seq`, member, credential id,
   the public key's artifact reference, algorithm, enrolment counter and `cool_off_ends`. The fold
   does not judge the cool-off. `consume` does that for step-up. DEC-2117 item 6 lets a passkey in
   its cool-off open a reduction-only session. The fold holds no public key; the verifier resolves
   the artifact. The counter stays the enrolment's until `StepUpAsserted` (journal spec §9.32,
   planned) is registered, and then the fold takes its greatest. Until then `credential_record`
   refuses a `StepUpAsserted` at `event_type` rather than passing over it. A stream that holds one
   fails closed, and the enrolment's counter is never taken for the last.

## Rationale

One pure fold serves all three readers, so the writer's check and the verifier's distrust cannot
drift apart. Item 2 reads "a member whose records break it" as every member a breaking record
touches. The narrower reading, the record's own member only, would keep counting the first
enroller's credential after another member's record named it. That ambiguity is exactly what the
rule exists to refuse.

## Alternatives considered

- **The fold in `mandate-identity`, beside the membership fold.** Rejected: it would have to name
  passkey types that `mandate-passkey` already owns, and `mandate-passkey` depends on
  `mandate-identity`, not the other way round.
- **Disable only the breaking record's own member.** Rejected, as the rationale says.
- **An `ever_enrolled` query of its own.** Rejected: `admits` already refuses a reused id, and the
  writer needs no other question.

## Follow-ups

- The verifier slice: resolve the key artifact and build `stepup::EnrolledCredential`. §9.31
  records no backup-eligible flag, which `consume` checks against the enrolment. That slice must
  settle where the flag comes from before it lands.
- The `StepUpAsserted` slice: the fold takes the greatest asserted counter.
