## Context

DEC-2108 item 2 makes a member's passkeys the fold of `CredentialEnrolled` and `CredentialRemoved`
over the control stream. Journal spec §9.31 leaves stream rule 251 to the writer: the API checks it
against its own fold before it appends, the verifier's fold fails closed on it, and "a member whose
records break it has no credential that counts". Three readers need the fold: the enrol route
(the writer's check, and the list), the step-up verifier, and DEC-2117's reduction-only opening,
which looks a passkey up by its id. §9.31 does not say where the fold lives, which members a
breaking record disables when it names another member's credential, or what the fold carries.

## Decision

1. **The fold is `mandate_passkey::credentials`.** It sits beside `stepup::consume`, which judges
   an assertion against an enrolled credential, and depends only on what `mandate-passkey` already
   does. It reads no clock and no store: `credential_record` maps one envelope, and `CredentialFold`
   folds the records in stream order on each read.
2. **A breaking record disables step-up for every member it touches.** A record breaks rule 251
   unless it is its credential's first record and an enrolment, or its second and a removal by the
   member who enrolled it. A breaking record disables its own member and, when its credential was
   first enrolled by another member, that member too. From that record on, `passkeys` answers
   `Broken` for them and none of their passkeys counts for step-up, so their step-up is
   `step_up_missing`. Disabling both sides of a collision only ever refuses a step-up; it never lets
   one through.
3. **Two views, one fold.** The step-up verifier and the reduction-only opening (route 2,
   DEC-2117) read the same records but must not fail alike. Step-up fails closed. Route 2 must
   never be denied, since pause and the kill switch are what it opens (`AGENTS.md` rule 13).
   - `passkeys(member)`, step-up's view: the member's enrolled, unremoved credentials, in
     enrolment order. It answers `AssertionUnread` while the stream holds a `StepUpAsserted` (item
     4), and `Broken` for a member item 2 disables.
   - `held(credential)`, route 2's view: the enrolment that holds an id, and its member, or none.
     It never refuses. It replays only the records that keep rule 251, so the first enrolment of
     an id wins: on a collision the first enroller still holds it and the colliding member does
     not. A breaking removal removes nothing. It skips a `StepUpAsserted`, since it reads no
     counter.
   - `admits(next)`: the writer's rule 251 check, on `next` alone. An id ever enrolled on the
     stream, removed or not, is refused, which is DEC-2118 item 5's reused id. A record that keeps
     the rule is admitted even from a member an earlier record broke; `passkeys` still discounts
     that member (§9.31). A `StepUpAsserted` is not a record the rule reads.
4. **What the fold carries, and does not.** Each enrolment keeps its `seq`, member, credential id,
   the public key's artifact reference, algorithm, enrolment counter and `cool_off_ends`. The fold
   does not judge the cool-off. `consume` does that for step-up. DEC-2117 item 6 lets a passkey in
   its cool-off open a reduction-only session. The fold holds no public key; the verifier resolves
   the artifact. The counter stays the enrolment's until `StepUpAsserted` (journal spec §9.32,
   planned) is read, and then the fold takes its greatest. Until then `credential_record` maps a
   `StepUpAsserted` to an unread `Asserted` record rather than passing over it. Step-up's view then
   fails closed, so the enrolment's counter is never taken for the last, and route 2's view is
   unchanged. The fold's `StepUpAsserted` support (journal spec v0.71, DEC-2122) ships before, or
   with, any writer that appends one.

## Rationale

One pure fold serves all three readers, so the writer's check and the verifier's distrust cannot
drift apart. Item 2 reads "a member whose records break it" as every member a breaking record
touches. The narrower reading, the record's own member only, would keep counting the first
enroller's credential after another member's record named it. That ambiguity is exactly what the
rule exists to refuse.

Item 3 keeps that strictness to step-up, which adds risk, and gives route 2 the reading that never
denies a reduction. A stream that breaks rule 251, or that holds a record the fold cannot read yet,
is a writer's fault the verifier refuses to build on. It is never a reason to deny a pause or a kill
switch. The first enrolment of an id is the one the writer admitted, so route 2 trusts that one.

## Alternatives considered

- **The fold in `mandate-identity`, beside the membership fold.** Rejected: it would have to name
  passkey types that `mandate-passkey` already owns, and `mandate-passkey` depends on
  `mandate-identity`, not the other way round.
- **Disable only the breaking record's own member.** Rejected, as the rationale says.
- **Refuse a `StepUpAsserted` row outright.** Rejected: route 2 maps the same rows, and a refusal
  there would deny the kill switch on a stream the writer had made valid.
- **Disable route 2 for a broken member too.** Rejected: it turns a writer's fault into a denied
  reduction, against rule 13.
- **An `ever_enrolled` query of its own.** Rejected: `admits` already refuses a reused id, and the
  writer needs no other question.

## Follow-ups

- The verifier slice: resolve the key artifact and build `stepup::EnrolledCredential`. §9.31
  records no backup-eligible flag, which `consume` checks against the enrolment. That slice must
  settle where the flag comes from before it lands.
- The `StepUpAsserted` slice: the fold takes the greatest asserted counter.
