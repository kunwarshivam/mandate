| **Story or claim** | E8-10 slice D1, claim [#753](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/issues/753); [notifications spec](../../specs/notifications.md) §3.4, §5.3, §5.4, §5.8, NT-6, NT-7 |

## Decision

1. **Home.** The dispatcher's pure policy is in `mandate-notify`, as [DEC-701](DEC-701.md) item 1
   already places "the quiet-hours rule by class, the retry schedule, the coalescing fold":
   `quiet_hours` in `src/quiet.rs` (slice D1a), and `coalesce`, `next_attempt`, and `notice_keys`
   in `src/delivery.rs` (slice D1b, two PRs to keep each under ES-13's 400 lines). No new crate
   is created; `mandate-dispatcher` stays DEC-701 item 2's process crate, created with the journal
   work. `mandate-notify` gains `mandate-time` (layer 0, already in the workspace) for `UtcNanos`
   and `NewYorkTime`, and no external dependency.
2. **Quiet hours take the zone's offsets as input.** The window is §5.8's `[start, end)` in
   America/New_York wall time, at minute precision, as `mandate_approval::deliver_now` reads it
   (DEC-165 item 7). The caller passes the UTC offsets in force (`Offset { from, minutes_east }`),
   and the offset at an instant is the entry with the latest `from` at or before it (the later in
   the list on a tie). The policy resolves no zone name and reads no tz database; DST is pinned in
   tests by typed 2026 offset tables. This amends DEC-701 item 1's sentence that the window
   arithmetic stays `deliver_now`'s: `deliver_now` cannot take caller offsets, and a held `info`
   push needs the window's end as an instant, which it does not compute. The two read the same
   rule.
3. **"Deferred to the window's end" is the first instant at or after the attempt whose wall
   minute is outside the window.** Across the spring change that can be the change itself (a
   window the skipped hour cuts short ends at 03:00 EDT), and a window in the skipped hour never
   holds anything. In the repeated autumn hour, a push held to 01:30 EDT and attempted at 01:10 EST
   is judged again at its own time (§7, "each attempt is judged at its own send time"), so it is
   held a second time.
4. **No offset, no guess.** An `action` or `info` attempt whose instant no offset covers is
   refused `Unrepresentable`, so the caller cannot get a verdict from a missing table. A `safety`
   push reads neither the window nor the offsets, so it is sent with any table or none (NT-7).
5. **Coalescing edges** (§5.4, DEC-700 item 5), per recipient and push channel:
   1. A `safety` cause is stale when its age at the pass (the pass's watermark less its commit time)
      is 60 seconds or more. It goes at once in its own message, and neither opens nor joins a
      window, so it is never held. This holds with no window open, the first pass included, and
      with fresh causes in the same pass, which open or join a window as if it were not there.
   2. A window ends at the earlier of its opening plus 60 seconds and its earliest joined commit
      plus 60 seconds. A pass at or after that instant finds it closed. Its opening causes went at
      once in the first message, so a window nothing joined ends with no second message: they are
      neither re-sent nor dropped.
   3. A window's message comes before a message of a pass at the same instant. One pass's own
      messages go in the read order of their first causes. A message lists its causes in read
      order, and its first cause's notice id is the message's.
   4. `action` and `info` causes are never coalesced: each goes alone at its pass. Their limits
      (the ask budget, one brief a day) are applied before the fold, not by it.
   5. A pass whose watermark is before the previous pass's is refused `Unrepresentable`.
6. **The retry schedule** (§5.3, DEC-438 item 9):
   1. The 15 s, 60 s, 5 min, then 15 min are gaps after the previous attempt. After attempt n, the
      next is due at the latest attempt's instant plus 15 s (n = 1), 60 s (n = 2), 300 s (n = 3),
      or 900 s (n ≥ 4).
   2. A window (`safety` 24 hours, `info` 6 hours) runs from the first attempt. An attempt due
      exactly at its end is made, and one due after it is `abandoned` `retry_window_ended`.
      `action` has no window: its `not_pending` stop is the caller's, from the approval.
   3. The provider's verdict decides, not its reason. §5.3 says "a `retryable` result, a timeout,
      and a provider 429 all retry" and "`permanent` stops that channel", and §5.2 returns
      `retryable { reason }` or `permanent { reason }` with the reason only as the record. So a
      `retryable` result retries within the class's window whatever its reason, and a `permanent`
      one stops that channel at once as `failed` with its reason. `too_large` is in none of §5.2's
      permanent lists, so it is not permanent by itself: a `retryable { too_large }` retries, and
      a `safety` channel is never stopped after one attempt on the reason alone. A timeout with no
      answer is the caller's `retryable { timeout }`, and a 429 is `retryable { rate_limited }`
      (§5.4). §5.2's permanent reasons arrive as `permanent` (the adapter contract, DEC-712), so
      the policy keeps no second list of them to drift from §5.2.
   4. A `permanent` result is checked before the window. A permanent failure is its own stop in
      §5.3's table, journaled `failed` with its reason (§5.5), and the reason is what §5.6 reads to
      mark the address or raise `channel_lost`; `retry_window_ended` is only for an `abandoned`
      attempt, one never made because no retry fits. With no retry to schedule, the window has
      nothing to judge, so a `permanent` result after the window's end is still `failed` with its
      reason.
   5. Zero attempts, or an `accepted` outcome (nothing failed), is refused `Unrepresentable`.
7. **One notice per cause** (§3.4, DEC-720's `owner_command`). The notice key of an
   `OwnerAlertSent` is the owner command it carries out when it names one, and its own event id
   otherwise. A key already issued is not issued again, so one user kill switch is one notice
   however many stream owners name its command.

## Rationale

- Item 1 follows an accepted decision instead of making a new crate. DEC-701 item 1 already put
  these rules in `mandate-notify` so they can be mutation-tested with no I/O nearby. Adding a crate
  would have meant a new layer entry and CODEOWNERS line for code that DEC-701 assigns elsewhere.
- Item 2 keeps the core zone-free, as the coordinator ruled. Whoever resolves the zone stays
  outside the safety-critical crate, and DST is tested by tables typed from the published 2026
  changes.
- Items 3 to 7 each fill a gap the spec leaves at a boundary. Each picks the reading that sends no
  later and drops nothing:
  - a stale cause goes at once;
  - a window never outlives its earliest cause's 60 seconds;
  - an attempt due at the window's end is still made;
  - a `retryable` result retries whatever its reason, so no `safety` channel stops on a reason
    the spec never made permanent;
  - the de-duplication key merges only records naming the same owner command.
  None of them touches trading state (NT-5).

## Alternatives considered

- **A new `mandate-dispatcher` crate for the pure policy.** Rejected for item 1's reason. The
  coordinator allowed `mandate-notify` where it is the better home.
- **Calling `mandate_approval::deliver_now`.** Rejected. `mandate-notify` and `mandate-approval`
  are both at layer 1, so neither can depend on the other. `deliver_now` also reads the zone
  through `mandate-time`'s bundled rules, which the coordinator's ruling keeps out of the policy.
- **Retry offsets from the first attempt (0, 15 s, 60 s, 5 min, 20 min, ...).** Rejected. "Then
  every 15 min" reads as a gap, and neither reading adds risk. If the founder or a review prefers
  offsets, only item 6.1 and the schedule test's expected column change.
- **A stale cause opens a window.** Rejected. A cause joining that window could then wait up to 60
  seconds more after the dispatcher has already lagged, and §5.4 says the bound wins over the
  message count.
