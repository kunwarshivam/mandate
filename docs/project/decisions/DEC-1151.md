On the Tuesday rehearsal clock, after R18's fix, the watch adopted the entry `filled` from its
order record. No fill was ever journaled, because the executor books a fill only from a fill
report, and Robinhood publishes none (connections spec U-R8: no activity feed, fill fields
unpublished). The reconciliation then read the broker's share against an empty ledger,
`BrokerPositionObserved` `mismatch`, and paused the agent (R19). Behind R19, the rehearsal met
three more stops, each recorded below: the tracer refused to send B3's protective stop (R20),
the reconciliation read that stop as the broker's own order (R21), and the rehearsal mandate set
no stop-limit offset (R22).

## Decision

1. **The broker's word on a fill, where it publishes no fill.** Robinhood's order record carries
   `filled_quantity` and no price; its position carries `average_buy_price`. U-R8 names the
   comparison DEC-529's order runs on a flat, dedicated account: the order's state and filled
   quantity, and the position. So the fill is read from the two, and only where they leave one
   reading (`mandate_executor::recorded_fills`):
   - the order is ours, a buy whose purpose adds risk, sent `day`, and its broker record
     reported a filled quantity above zero and no more than its own quantity, none of it booked;
   - the ledger holds the instrument flat, with no unattributed fill in it, and every other
     order in the instrument has booked all its record reported, so no other fill can be inside
     the broker's average;
   - the broker lists exactly one position in the instrument, long exactly the reported quantity.

   The fill is that quantity at that average price. Its trade date is the New York date of the
   order's `OrderSubmitted`, since a `day` order fills only in the session it was sent in.
2. **The idempotency key.** The fill's id is `record:<client order id>:<filled quantity>`. Every
   read of the same record derives the same id, so the journal books it once (journal spec
   §5.2), and a second derivation from the booked state derives nothing, because the order's
   booked quantity is no longer zero.
3. **Where it is booked.** Robinhood's reconciler hands these fills to the executor's
   reconciliation in its snapshot (`ExecutorReconciler::without_activities`). Step 2 books them
   through the executor's own fill path before step 3 compares positions (trading spec §11). An
   order already terminal books `LateFillApplied`, and one still working books `FillApplied` and
   its state (§5.7). So the reconciliation that follows the adoption finds the position explained,
   and the next start's own-fill pass (DEC-955) finds nothing owed.
4. **Never invented.** With no record of a fill, a ledger that is not flat, a second order with an
   unbooked fill in the instrument, a short position, two positions, or a position that is not
   exactly the reported quantity, nothing is derived. The position stays unexplained and the
   reconciliation pauses the agent, as before (`AGENTS.md` rule 3). Fees: the fill carries no
   broker fee, since Robinhood charges no commission (DEC-958 item 1). Its regulatory fees are the
   fee configuration's, which every fill record names (`config_refs`, DEC-958 and DEC-960).
5. **R20: the protective stop goes out beside the one opening.** The tracer sends one order per
   run (FT-1), and counted every submission. On a profile with no bracket, B3's stop-limit is a
   second submission after the fill, so the run stopped with `the tracer places one order per run,
   and the executor asked for another` before the stop reached the broker. DEC-940 item 2 already
   reads "An exit, a protective order or a flatten always passes", and `AGENTS.md` rule 13 exempts
   them from every such limit. The rule now counts only a submission whose purpose adds risk: a
   second opening is still refused (`one_order_only`), and a protective order or an exit always
   goes out. The watch watches the run's one opening, never a protective order sent before it.
6. **R21: the snapshot names our working orders.** A Robinhood record names our order only by
   `ref_id`, which the broker may not echo (U-R2, DEC-941 item 4). The account snapshot named
   nothing ours, so the reconciliation read the resting stop as an order of the broker's own. It
   drafted `ExternalActivityIngested`, which the journal refuses (DEC-956 item 5). The snapshot
   now names a record exactly as DEC-1150 item 7 names a listed one. It is our key when the record
   echoes that key's derived `ref_id`, or, echoing none, when its order id is the one this
   connector's place answered or its journal restored. A record with another `ref_id`, or with
   none and an id this connector never placed, names nothing. The executor's fold still reads no
   broker id (journal spec §9.16), so DEC-956 item 2's proposal stays the founder's.
7. **The simulator's cash.** `mandate-rh-sim` held cash and buying power fixed whatever filled.
   After item 3 booked the fill, the ledger's cash was the cost of the share below the broker's,
   so the reconciliation would have mismatched on cash. Real Robinhood spends a buy's cost from
   cash. The simulator now spends a buy's fill cost from both figures, and adds a sell's proceeds
   to neither. Under DEC-902 item 2, `get_portfolio`'s `cash` is settled cash, and adding unsettled
   proceeds would lift buying power. That is the reading that adds no risk, the simulator's own
   rule where the contract is silent.
8. **R22: the rehearsal mandate sets the stop-limit offset.** Robinhood protects US equities with a
   stop-limit only. With `stop_limit_offset` unset (version 1: `crypto_stop_limit_offset`), B3
   had no form to place and journaled `expiry_unreplaceable`. Mandate spec V-008 requires the
   offset where the profile protects with a stop-limit ("US equities on Robinhood"), and without
   the profile it counts every asset class as protected by one. The rehearsal mandate now sets
   `0.01`.

## Not closed here

- **V-008's code is looser than the spec (R22).** `mandate-spec`'s `document_rules` requires the
  offset only for crypto, so `version create` and `version confirm` accepted the rehearsal
  mandate. The spec's reading only refuses more. It is a `mandate-spec` change with reference
  cases, for its own PR. Until it lands, the founder's live mandate must set
  `crypto_stop_limit_offset`, or the position is unprotected and the run says so
  (`expiry_unreplaceable`, `protection_expiring`).
- **A sale's cash on Robinhood.** The platform's model credits a sell's proceeds at once. If
  Robinhood's `cash` adds them only at settlement, the first reconciliation after a live sell is
  out of the cash band. That alerts at once and pauses after DEC-146's second run. R0's order is a
  buy, and its stop's fill would be the first sell. The founder's check in the app after a sale
  answers it.
- **A restart before the fill is booked.** If a run ends after the adoption and before a
  reconciliation books the fill, the next start's own-fill pass asks the broker by client order
  id. Robinhood does not offer that request, so the start stops, fail closed: `the broker's
  answer for an owned order`.
- **Spec text.** Items 1 to 4 belong beside U-R8 and trading spec §11, and item 5 beside FT-1.
  ES-22 keeps spec text out of a code change, so these follow as a spec PR.

## Rationale

The broker holds the share, and the journal must say why before anything else reads the
account. On a flat, dedicated account, the position's average buy price is the fill's price, with
no other fill to blend in. That is the comparison U-R8 already names, so nothing beyond the
broker's own figures is believed. Every case with more than one reading derives nothing, and the
reconciliation then pauses the agent. Items 5 and 6 let the protection B3 places reach the broker
and be recognized there. Without them, a filled share would rest unprotected or stop the run.

## Alternatives considered

- **Book the fill at adoption, in the listing's path.** The listing's record carries no price, and
  the executor's step has no position to read. A price from the order's limit would be invented.
- **Price the fill at the order's limit.** It is a bound, not a price, and would misstate cash and
  P&L.
- **Leave R19 to a broker read of executions.** The contract offers none (U-R8). The founder's
  live order would then always pause the agent after its fill.
- **Count the protective stop as the one order, and send the entry in a second run.** That leaves
  a filled share unprotected across runs, which rule 13 forbids.
