## Decision

1. **The desktop stays; a page rises over it.** `/welcome` still opens on the desktop exactly as
   DEC-906 left it, filling the first screen. Below it, a long page (`LongPage` in
   `components/site/scroll/`) rises over the desktop as the visitor scrolls, as a sheet with its own
   bar (`scroll-bar.tsx`): the logo back to the desktop, links to the parts from 64rem, and Sign in
   and Sign up in words, which stick once the desktop's own bar is covered, so both stay in view
   (DEC-906 item 8). The desktop recedes behind the sheet where the browser has scroll timelines.
2. **Proper landing-page anatomy, and nothing the desktop already says.** In order: an opening
   ("Hire an agent. Keep the keys.") over a large picture of an agent's page and the brokers; four
   parts, each its words beside a picture of the app (the checks, asking you, threads, limits in
   dollars with the gap caveat beside them); "It won't", what no agent does in v1 (PRD §3, FR-5.12),
   on sun; the request for a place in the beta on one of the desktop's Met paintings, credited in
   its corner; and a footer with the site footer's existing disclaimer. The pictures are the
   example app's own screens in light and dark (`scripts/landing-shots.mjs`, `public/landing/`); the
   phone's lock screen shows only "An agent in your workspace needs your approval", the push the
   product sends. DEC-906 item 6 holds: no copy explains a picture.
3. **Faces.** Manrope for the page's text and a display serif for its headings, both self-hosted
   from `@fontsource` (`docs/dependencies.md`) and loaded only by the landing page (`site/faces.ts`).
   The founder asked for Gascogne Serial, which is sold under a paid licence; buying it is spending,
   reserved for the founder (DEC-79), so it stays Proposed. Until then Sorts Mill Goudy (OFL-1.1)
   stands in behind one token, `--font-page-display`, so the swap is one line.
4. **Tide, the third colour, spent sparingly.** Ink and sun leave the triad's third leg. Sun's
   complement is violet, which the palette bans; of the two remaining legs, teal (192, the existing
   teal ramp) was chosen over rose (345), which sits too near crimson and the colour-blind loss.
   `--tide` is teal-800 with paper-50 and teal-200 type (9.49:1 and 7.59:1), the same in both themes.
   It fills exactly one section, the part about asking you, and is the flying owl's trim, the lock
   screen's sea (item 8) and the thread (item 9); it is never used in the product and never text on
   paper (`long-page.test.tsx`, `COLOR.md`).
5. **A voxel owl flies down the page.** The brand owl's 16-pixel sprite read as a cat once extruded,
   since its ears and square head are all a cat has too, so the flying owl has its own 16 by 21
   sprite: a domed head, a pale facial disc ringed in tide, round sun eyes and a beak standing proud,
   folded wings edged in tide, chest bars and two feet. It is extruded into cubes (deepest at the
   belly, shallow at the crown and feet) and drawn on one canvas in four flat tones from one light,
   with only the exposed faces painted (`voxel-owl.ts`). Its feathers are the logo's ink, never an
   agent's colour (DEC-739). The head turns on the neck and the wings flap from the shoulders. On a
   desktop it peeks over the scroll cue, rides the sheet's top edge as it rises, then hops from
   perch to perch (`data-perch` on the pictures and parts) on damped springs: it leans into its
   travel, stretches and flaps harder when fast, turns its head to the pointer or glances about when
   left alone, bobs at rest and blinks (`owl-flight.tsx`). On tide it wears a pale coat, so its ink
   never sinks into the teal. It is decoration: hidden from assistive technology, never in the way of
   the pointer, and under reduced motion it stands still on the first perch, drawn once.
6. **Telling the visitor it scrolls.** A "Scroll" pill with an arrow sits on the desktop's first
   screen, centred on the browser's status bar where it shows (the one free place in both desktops'
   layouts) and otherwise near the bottom edge; it pops in, fades as the page rises, and is covered by
   the sheet. Clicking it brings the opening into view (`scroll-cue.tsx`).
7. **Motion on springs, beyond the craft floor's one moment.** The design system allows one
   longer motion moment; at the founder's request the long page has more. Its pieces settle on
   damped springs as they come into view (`reveals.tsx`): headings and words rise, pictures tilt
   back into place, the lock screen's notification drops in with a bounce, and each "It won't" verb
   is struck through in turn. The springs are the closed-form damped oscillator, as CSS `linear()`
   easings (`spring.ts`), and the owl steps the same spring numerically. Nothing is hidden unless
   motion is allowed and the browser can tell when a piece is in view, so with reduced motion or
   without a script every piece is simply there.
8. **The lock screen is a night in pixels.** Behind the notification, the phone shows a 44 by 95
   pixel night (`pixel-night.tsx`), the same in both themes as the desktop's paintings are
   moonlight: a dark sky that falls into tide by dithering, never a blend; stars, a few of which
   twinkle; a full moon in sun; and the brand owl awake on a branch over the hills, its face ringed
   in pale teal and its sun eyes the only warm thing in it. Every pixel is a flat token colour. The
   notification is paper on it and carries only the push the product sends, as before. Under
   reduced motion the stars are still.
9. **A pixel thread sews the page together.** A line of square tide pixels (`pixel-thread.tsx`)
   runs down the outer gutter and across the space above each part, knotted at every place the owl
   lands. It only goes down or across, so it never runs over itself, and it passes behind the words
   and under the pictures and the painting. It is sewn as far as the visitor has read, 55% down the
   window, catching up on a spring. Its colour is `--tide-line` (teal-800 in light, teal-400 in dark,
   9.03:1 or more on the page and cards), pale teal across the tide section and tide across sun, so
   it stays contrasting on each part. It is decoration on one canvas, hidden from assistive
   technology, and under reduced motion it is there whole.

## Rationale

The desktop already says what Owlhead is and lets the visitor use the app; a visitor who keeps
scrolling wants what it does not say, in the order a landing page answers it: what it is, how it
keeps you safe, what you are asked, what it will never do, and how to get in. Pictures of the app
show it without a line explaining them (DEC-906 item 6). A third colour only works if it stays rare,
so tide fills one section and is otherwise only trim, a sea under the stars and a thin thread. The owl is the brand's own sprite rather than an invented
mascot, and springs give the motion weight without a library.

## Alternatives considered

- **Rose (hue 345) as the third colour.** Rejected: near crimson, which means only the kill switch,
  and near the colour-blind loss.
- **A 3D library (three.js) for the owl.** Rejected: a new dependency and a WebGL context for one
  16-pixel sprite that a 2D canvas draws in four flat tones.
- **A motion library for the springs.** Rejected: CSS `linear()` carries a spring's curve natively,
  and the owl needs only a few lines of integration.
- **Buying Gascogne Serial now.** Not the agent's call (DEC-79); the stand-in keeps the page whole.
- **Extruding the brand sprite with longer ears.** Rejected: it still read as a cat; a disc, a beak
  and wings are what say owl, and they need a taller sprite.
- **A smooth night sky, or a thread that fades in.** Rejected: the design system bans gradients and
  fades, and dithering and whole stitches keep both on the pixel grid the owls and icons share.
