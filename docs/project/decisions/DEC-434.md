## Decision

### Accepted (agent)

1. **Environments.** Dev, CI, paper (Phase 1, the founder's own Alpaca paper account), staging
   (from M8), and production (after the Phase 2 gate). Only production may ever hold a live
   credential or reach a live trading host. Until the founder decides to go live, no production
   environment exists (design §1.1).
2. **Process topology.** One runtime process per agent deployment (DEC-08); one executor process
   per broker account, the single writer of the account stream (DEC-131's two writers); one
   scheduler per workspace; one model gateway per workspace deployment. Runtimes hold no broker
   credential and have no network route to a broker (rule 12) (design §3.1).
3. **Fencing is the only at-most-one guarantee.** The orchestrator may run two copies of a process;
   safety rests on the writer epoch (journal spec §5.1), and an executor appends `OrderSubmitted`
   before every broker request, so a fenced executor cannot send (design §3.4).
4. **Readiness.** A process is ready only when it holds its writer epoch, has replayed to the head,
   and (executor) its last reconciliation passed. A process that is not ready takes no new opening;
   kill-switch commands, risk exits, and protective re-placement still run once replay is done
   (design §3.4).
5. **Artifact before event.** An artifact is durable before the journal event that names it is
   appended, so a crash leaves an unreferenced artifact, never an `artifact_missing` reference
   (design §4.3).
6. **Synchronous-only failover.** A live journal fails over only to a synchronous standby. With no
   synchronous standby, commits wait and appends return `Unavailable`; the system never downgrades
   to asynchronous commit. The Phase 1 paper environment may run one Postgres with WAL archiving
   (design §4.1).
7. **Drain and hand-over.** No agent process is upgraded in place. The old process drains (no new
   decision cycle or opening; no order left between `OrderSubmitted` and its request; an exit
   sequence carried to its end within trading spec §5.4's bound), then the new one fences it by
   epoch and recovers. A deploy never cancels or replaces a resting protective order. A drain past
   its bound is abandoned, not forced: the new process recovers as after a crash (design §7.3).
8. **Rollback and migrations.** A rollback goes only to a release that reads every `schema_version`
   the newer one wrote. Migrations stay forward-only (ES-08) and are expand-only across one release,
   applied as a deploy step before processes roll (design §7.2, §7.4).
9. **Restores never auto-resume.** After a restore, every stream is verified (journal spec §11) and
   compared with anchors and cold exports held outside the restored database. Any `seq` they name
   beyond a restored head is an integrity incident. Every account is reconciled with its broker,
   which is the source of truth, and an order the restored journal does not know is treated as
   external activity, even if the platform sent it before the loss (trading spec §7.1, §11).
   Resuming takes the owner's acknowledgment with step-up (design §6.3).
10. **Opaque telemetry.** Metric labels, log fields, and operator alerts carry opaque IDs and
    enumerations only; a lint on the metrics registry enforces the allowed label set (design §8.1).
11. **Default-deny egress.** Each process type has an egress allow-list; non-production
    environments cannot reach a live trading host at the network layer (design §9).
12. **Epic E21, Operations and infrastructure.** E18 is the enterprise harness (DEC-149); E19 and
    E20 are left to the sibling specs drafted in parallel. E21's stories are proposed and not yet
    in the epic overview (design §14).

### Proposed for the founder

The most conservative option holds until each is decided: no new paid service, paper only, on the
founder's own host.

13. **Hosting provider and regions for managed cells.** Recommendation: decide at M8. Require
    compliance-mode object lock, a key management service, managed Kubernetes, and two US regions.
14. **Vault product.** Recommendation: OpenBao in every deployment mode, behind a `SecretSource`
    interface so the choice stays reversible; confirm its license at decision time. HashiCorp Vault
    is under the Business Source License, so bundling it needs counsel, as Restate does (ES-20).
15. **Managed Postgres service or self-run.** Recommendation: self-run with a failover manager,
    unless a managed service offers synchronous standbys with fencing of the old primary at the
    ES-08 version floor.
16. **Object storage on the customer's site.** Recommendation: require the customer's S3-compatible
    store with compliance-mode object lock, and bundle none. MinIO, which the HLD names for the
    edge, is AGPL-3.0, so bundling it needs counsel.
17. **Recovery point, recovery time, and SLO targets.** Recommendation: adopt design §6.1 and §8.3 as written.
18. **Budgets.** Recommendation: a monthly cap each for infrastructure and inference, with alerts at
    50%, 80%, and 100%.
19. **Staging.** Recommendation: a minimal staging cell at the start of M8.
20. **On-call.** Recommendation: the founder alone until design partners; a paging tool chosen then.

## Rationale

The HLD, ADR-0001, and the journal and trading specs already fix most operational rules, but
scattered: durability in journal spec §5.3, fencing in §5.1, retention in §6.2, protection in
trading spec §5.4, signing in ES-17. Items 1 to 11 collect them and close the gaps between them,
each time with the reading that adds no risk: wait rather than lose an acknowledged append, treat
an unexplained order as external activity, abandon a drain rather than force it, refuse rather than
guess. None loosens a spec, an invariant, or a non-negotiable, and each can be reversed by a later
decision.

Items 13 to 20 spend money or set what live operation commits to, which DEC-79 reserves for the
founder.

## Alternatives considered

- **In-place upgrades with a hot binary swap.** Rejected: harder to reason about than a fenced
  hand-over, and the journal already makes a restart safe.
- **Trusting the orchestrator for one instance per stream.** Rejected: partitions and slow
  terminations break it; the writer epoch already gives the guarantee.
- **Asynchronous failover for availability.** Rejected: it can lose an acknowledged
  `OrderSubmitted`, which can lead to a duplicate order after recovery.
- **Adopting orders that carry our ID prefix after a restore.** Deferred to the founder (design §15
  question 4): it loosens trading spec §11's treatment of unknown orders.
- **A cloud secrets manager.** Not recommended: it breaks the HLD's same-installer rule for on-prem.

## Founder decisions (2026-10-03)

The founder accepted items 13 to 20 as recommended:
- hosting chosen at M8, under the requirements item 13 lists;
- OpenBao, behind a `SecretSource` interface;
- self-run Postgres with a fenced synchronous standby, unless a managed service meets item 15;
- the customer's own S3-compatible store, with no bundled MinIO;
- the design's recovery and SLO targets;
- monthly caps for infrastructure and inference, with alerts at 50%, 80%, and 100%;
- a minimal staging cell at the start of M8;
- the founder alone on call until design partners.

The founder also decided the design's open question 4 (§15): an order that carries our
client-ID prefix but is unknown to a restored journal is treated as external activity (trading
spec §11), never adopted by its prefix.

## Added after the post-merge review (design v0.2, 2026-10-03)

The independent review of the design on #552 found one blocker and three majors. These items are
its fixes. Nothing above is rewritten.

### Proposed for the founder

21. **What an exit does while the journal is unavailable** (design §2.1). Rule 5 says every order
    intent is journaled before it is sent. Rule 13 says risk reduction is never denied, and lists
    the only permitted holds: agent mode `paused` or `stopped`, an `Unknown` order in the same
    instrument, or the broker. While the journal is unavailable the two meet: an executor cannot
    journal, so it can send no exit, and that hold is not on rule 13's list. Neither the trading
    spec nor the mandate spec says what an exit does then. The decision weakens or amends a
    non-negotiable either way, so DEC-79 reserves it for the founder. Options:
    - **(a) Name the hold** (recommended). Rule 13's list gains "the journal is unavailable", and
      the trading spec states the behaviour: nothing is sent, resting protective orders are
      untouched, the hold is alerted, the owner is told they can act at their broker, and owed
      exits are sent first when appends return. The exposure stays and is disclosed: a position
      can lose more than its limit intends if the market moves through a stop outside the regular
      session, or gaps through a crypto stop-limit, while the journal is down. Rule 5 keeps no
      exception, and the journal stays the single source of truth (DEC-16).
    - **(b) A fallback record for exits.** A risk exit may be sent once its intent is in a second
      durable record, copied into the journal later. Rule 5 gains an exception, recovery must
      merge two logs, and a duplicate order becomes possible if the two disagree.
    - **(c) Send exits unjournaled.** Rule 13 wins outright. This breaks rule 5 and the
      idempotency that "no order twice" rests on (OPS-3).

    **Until decided, the most conservative option holds: nothing is ever sent without being
    journaled first.** The design states the limit, its backstop (protection resting at the broker,
    with its own limits, and the owner acting directly at the broker), and restates OPS-4 with its
    precondition, "given an available journal", so the invariant and its test claim only what
    holds.

### Accepted (agent)

22. **The market-data channel is host-local.** The data-plane spec (§3.6) delivers quotes, statuses,
    and LULD bands to runtimes and executors over an in-process or same-host channel. The design
    places a workspace's data service, executors, and runtimes on one node in v1, treats the channel
    as a per-workspace local socket outside the network egress allow-list, and says so in the
    process table, so an allow-list built from that table cannot cut the executor off from marks.
    Spreading a workspace over nodes needs a data-plane spec change first (design §3.1, §3.2).
    This amends the reading in item 2 that an executor and its agents may sit on different nodes.
23. **The model gateway is a single writer.** The inference spec (§3.6) gives the gateway a
    `meter:{workspace_id}` stream it writes alone. So one gateway instance is active per workspace
    deployment and holds each workspace's meter epoch; a standby takes over by epoch; a fenced
    instance refuses every call and exits, and the caller records the output as missing (inference
    spec INF-4, INF-7). No load-sharing replicas until a per-replica meter stream is specified
    (design §3.1). This corrects the design's v0.1 row, which said the gateway was replicated and
    wrote nothing to the journal.
24. **Backup and drill records are journal events, defined before the drill is built.** The
    journal's catalogue is closed and has no backup or drill event, and telemetry may not hold the
    record (OPS-11). E21-25 changes the journal spec first; E21-5 is blocked on it (design §6.4).

Also noted in the design, without changing item 17: its region-loss targets assume the second
region that item 13 chooses at M8, so the two take effect together (design §6.1).
