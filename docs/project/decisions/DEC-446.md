## Decision

DEC-360 found that the executor's fold reads members the registered account-stream schemas do not
carry, and the founder ruled option (c): (a) for `risk_clock`, which journal spec §9.2 already says
the executor writes on every account-stream event, and (b) for the rest — the protective prices to
`ProtectionChanged`, and `OrderSubmitted`'s executor-only members to a companion record journaled
in the same batch. This decision settles what that ruling writes down, journal spec v0.13 §9.5.

1. **`risk_clock` joins `IntentReceived`, `GateDecided`, and `OrderSubmitted` at `schema_version`
   2, never by editing version 1.** Journal spec §8: a schema once closed is never edited in place,
   and the registered v1 schemas pin the version-3 vectors byte for byte (the 22 passing
   `journal::chain::*` and append cases read them). Neither is a version-1 record with the member
   ever conforming (the closed schemas refuse unlisted members as `schema`), so no conforming v1
   record is refused that was not refused before: the reading only refuses more (DEC-176). It also
   adds no risk: the fold already requires `risk_clock` on every account-stream event (DEC-302
   item 1) and rejects one that went backwards, so a record the new schemas refuse could never
   have been replayed. §9.2's sentence — "the executor writes it on every account-stream event and
   folds each at it" — is the authority; DEC-81's "every other account-stream risk input" gave the
   risk inputs of mandate spec §5.2 the member, and option (c) extends it to the three executor
   records. At version 2 each schema is version 1's members with `risk_clock` last, as
   `AccountSnapshotRecorded` already lists it (§9.2). Version 1 stays registered forever (§8): a
   stream that holds version-1 records keeps replaying them.
2. **The companion is `OrderRequestRecorded` on the account stream, `schema_version` 1, carrying
   exactly the executor-only members DEC-360 found:** `agent_id`, `intent_id?`, `purpose`,
   `extended_hours`, `stop_price?`, `order_class?`, `take_profit?`, `stop?`, `rung?`, `at_floor`,
   and `risk_clock` (item 1). The names are the registered schemas' canonical ones — `agent_id`,
   as `IntentReceived` spells it, never the fold's legacy `agent`. There is no `laddered` flag:
   `rung` non-null says a ladder's rung, and the first rung of a lone ladder writes `rung` 0, so a
   zero rung never means "not a ladder". `RequestRecord` rebuilds the exact request for a
   resubmission after a confirmed absence (trading spec §5.7), and the order's agent and purpose
   drive restrictions and the exit rules, so the companion carries only what the fold reads for
   them and nothing else (DEC-360 item 1's list: `agent`, `purpose`, `intent_id`,
   `extended_hours`, `stop_price`, `order_class`, `take_profit`, `stop`, and a ladder rung's
   `rung` and `at_floor`).
3. **Pairing, rule 45: the companion immediately precedes its `OrderSubmitted` in one `append`
   batch, and the `OrderSubmitted` names it as its `causation_id`.** The rule binds an
   `OrderSubmitted` at `schema_version` 2; a version-1 record keeps the version-3 vectors' form and
   never gains a companion. A batch is checked draft by draft first (§9.1's order), then rule 45:
   each `OrderSubmitted` in batch order — the record it must follow missing, duplicated, or out of
   order is `schema` at `event_type`, and one whose `causation_id` does not name that record is
   `schema` at `causation_id` — and only then an `OrderRequestRecorded` no `OrderSubmitted` in the
   batch names, `schema` at `event_type`. One batch, so the write of the request and the write of
   the order are one commit (§5.1, "refused whole"), and a crash cannot leave one without the
   other. There is no per-range §11 check: the pairing is fully inside the batch, and a
   verification range never starts inside a batch (§11 verifies from seq 1, or from a snapshot's
   head that a whole batch wrote).
4. **`ProtectionChanged` is closed at `schema_version` 1 with every member the fold reads, canonically
   named, plus `risk_clock` (item 1).** The actions are the fold's vocabulary — `placed`,
   `cancelled`, `unprotected_start`, `passive_start`, `watchdog`, `exit_unpriced`,
   `ladder_floor`, `expiry_unreplaceable`, `rung_short`, `interval_limit`, `unprotected_end` —
   and one new action, `intended`, the receipt-time protective prices a protected opening carries
   from its intent (DEC-360 option (c): the prices move here from `IntentReceived`'s fold reads).
   The record, written beside the `IntentReceived` in the same batch, is what the fold restores
   protection from at replay. The members are the fold's reads, traced in item 6:
   `instrument_id` (the fold's `instrument`, canonically renamed), `action`, `orders` (the orders
   the action names, a `[text]` list of client order ids — never the legacy space-joined text),
   `awaiting`, `qty?`, `stop?`, `take_profit?` (the protective prices, decimally), `intent_id?`,
   `bracket?`, `entry?`, `agent_id?` (the fold's `agent`), `replacing?`, `created_on?` (a `date`),
   `sent?` (a decimal), `uncovered?`, `acknowledged?`, and `risk_clock`. Rules 39 and 40 are the companion's; 41 to 44 the `ProtectionChanged` members'; 45 the pairing (item 3). Every member is present
   in every record (§4.2, §9.1's absent-member rule); rules 41 to 44 say per action which are
   null or empty. The fold's reads of legacy records stay exactly as tolerant as on `main`
   (item 7), so the closing changes no replay: a writer that journals the closed form adds no
   risk, and a member an old record left out reads as it always did.
5. **`IntentReceived` keeps §9's nine members exactly, and gains only `risk_clock` at version 2.**
   The protective prices are never members of it, at either version: option (c) moves them, it
   does not copy them. `received` journals the `IntentReceived` and its `intended`
   `ProtectionChanged` in one batch against one intent, so replay restores the protection the
   opening was handed in with (`AGENTS.md` rule 13: protection never lost to a restart).
6. **Member traces.** Every member comes from a writer that exists or a clause that names it, as
   §9.1 to §9.4 require. For the v2 records, the v1 members trace to the version-3 vectors and the
   registered schemas (DEC-174 item 5, DEC-307), and `risk_clock` to §9.2's sentence and item 1.
   `OrderRequestRecorded`: `journal_rung`'s pairs — `agent`, `intent_id`, `purpose`,
   `extended_hours`, `stop_price`, `order_class`, `take_profit`, `stop`, and `journal_rung`'s
   `extra`, the rung's `rung` and `at_floor` — renamed `agent_id`, minus the request members
   `OrderSubmitted` already closes (`client_order_id`, `attempt`, `instrument_id`, `side`, `qty`,
   `type`, `tif`, `limit_price`). `ProtectionChanged`: every member is a `fold::protection_changed`
   read — the per-action reads of item 4, with `intended`'s set (`orders` empty, `qty` null,
   `intent_id`, the prices) fixed by `received`'s record. No member is inferred.
7. **The fold and the writers.** The fold's legacy reads stay: `instrument` beside
   `instrument_id`, `agent` beside `agent_id`, `limit` beside `limit_price`, `order_type` beside
   `type`, `orders` as space- or comma-joined text beside the list, and a v1 record beside a v2.
   `FOLD_VERSION` stays 1: the folded state is unchanged (DEC-389 item 3's stub sites), and the
   dual reads are tolerance, not a state change. The writers move to the closed form:
   `received` journals `intent_received_fields` and the `intended` record,
   `journal_rung` the companion and the causation-naming `OrderSubmitted`, and
   `order_submitted_optional_fields` carries the request's nullable `limit_price`. The executor
   maps none of these records to a `JournaledFact`: none is a `ValidationContext` input, and the
   §9.2-style mapping is for the context's reads alone.
8. **Order of the changes (ES-22).** As DEC-174 item 4 and the coordinator's comment on #482
   sequence §9.x work: spec and vectors first (this story's spec PR), then the tests PR (the
   `mandate-journal` registration stubs, the draft-payload stubs, the fold and writer stubs, and
   the pending pins, each failing at its own stub, DEC-77), then the implementation PR. No
   protected fixture is hand-edited; `cargo xtask refcases --write` regenerates them. The journal
   suite's live cases never go pending, so version 1 can never gain the member (item 1).

## Rationale

**Why version 2 for `risk_clock`.** The alternatives all fail one pinned fact. Editing version 1
in place breaks §8 and the 22 live vector cases. Closing only new records carries `risk_clock` no
further than DEC-81 already does. Refusing to stamp it (dropping the fold's requirement) would let
a conforming record replay without the clock DEC-302 item 1 needs. Version 2 is the reader-
compatible step: old records stay conforming forever, new records carry what the fold already
requires, and `append`'s never-decrease check (§2) covers them as it covers the snapshot.

**Why a companion and not wider `OrderSubmitted`.** DEC-360 item 2: option (b) keeps
`OrderSubmitted` what the version-3 vector shows — the request as the broker sees it, its eight
members — and option (a) would have widened a closed schema toward nine executor-only members,
which is what the founder ruled against. The companion is a new record, so nothing closed moves,
and batch pairing (item 3) makes the two one commit, which is what "journaled beside it" must mean
for a resubmission to rebuild the request from the journal alone (§5.2: write before acting).

**Why `ProtectionChanged` closes whole rather than `intended` alone.** A schema is closed per
`(event_type, schema_version)`, not per action: closing only `intended` while `placed` carries
unlisted members is not a state `append` can express — the record would be refused at `orders`
before its action is read. The fold's reads are the member set, so closing refuses nothing a
conforming writer needs and everything DEC-360 item 1's drift (a payload the fold cannot replay
faithfully) would be.

**Why the dual reads stay.** Streams written before v0.13 hold v1 records and legacy
`ProtectionChanged` payloads (unclosed then, so the fold's tolerant reads accepted them). Replay
must keep reading them (§8): tightening the fold to the closed form would make a recorded stream
unreplayable. Tolerance at the fold and strictness at `append` are the two halves of every §9.x
closing (DEC-177, DEC-261).

## The cost, stated plainly

- Every account-stream batch that journals a submission is two events, not one, and every intent
  receipt is two when it carries protection. Hot-store rows and segment bytes grow by the
  companions; nothing else does.
- Legacy `ProtectionChanged` writers (the protective-exit stack's) are not touched here; they keep
  their unclosed members until the slice that owns them moves them to the closed form. Until then
  their drafts stay refused at `append` exactly as they are on `main` today (DEC-360 item 3), which
  holds no exit back.

## Alternatives considered

- **`intent_id` on the companion instead of `causation_id` pairing** (name the intent, let the
  `OrderSubmitted` keep a free `causation_id`). Rejected: two drafts of one submission would then
  pair only through the intent, which a ladder's later rung shares with its first, and the rung's
  own request would be ambiguous. The immediate-predecessor rule pairs one record with one record.
- **A §11 per-range pairing check.** Rejected (item 3): nothing spans a range boundary.
- **`OrderRequestRecorded` holding `limit_price` too, and `OrderSubmitted` slimming to the
  submission.** Rejected: it edits the version-3 form of a record whose eight members are closed
  and pinned, the exact move option (c) avoids.
- **`laddered` beside `rung`.** Rejected (item 2): a second name for `rung` non-null invites a
  record whose two disagree.
