## Context

The signal models and the liquidity check read daily bars from the workspace's store (DEC-846,
DEC-470 item 4). Only the local cell's data process has any: a recording of synthetic closes that
only dev and CI cells may list (DEC-2385 item 4). On a hosted cell the crossover agent therefore
has nothing to decide on. DEC-2385 item 7 left the real source to a later slice, read "over the
account's connection". The data process has no connection of its own, though, and a connection's
token is the executor's alone. The platform already holds one data-only paper key in the vault,
for news (DEC-2277).

## Decision

1. **A second source for the data process.** `mandate-datad` gets a source `alpaca-iex` beside the
   recording, on managed cells and for paper workspaces only: it writes nothing while a
   control-stream row it reads is not of the `paper` environment. A live workspace needs SIP
   (DEC-35), and a hybrid or on-prem site never holds the platform key. The recording becomes a
   fixture the chart refuses on any tier but `dev` and `ci`, by its own check, no longer only
   because no other tier lists instruments.
2. **One key for news and bars (founder, F2).** The data process reads the platform's data-only
   paper key, `platform/news-data-key`, as a new identity, `Identity::BarsReader` (role
   `bars-reader`), with read access to that path only. `policy::holders` changes from one reader to
   a list:

   | Secret | Read | Write | Delete |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | The platform data key | News reader, bars reader | Secret import | — |

   DEC-2277's guards carry over:
   - the key is read once at start into memory, its `Debug` redacted and its headers marked
     sensitive;
   - a key whose id does not begin `PK` is refused, with `NotPaper`;
   - a new transport sends it only to the data host's `/v2/stocks/bars` and
     `/v1/corporate-actions`, refusing any other path before a byte is sent. The corporate actions
     only qualify the bars: they are what lets the trust check refuse a split (item 4).

   The key never reaches the environment, a chart value, a log, the journal, or any agent or
   runtime. A `401` or `403` stops the process, and its restart rereads the key, which is how a
   rotation reaches it. The vault path keeps its name, so nothing already provisioned moves.
3. **The instruments.**
   - **The set.** The symbols of the latest `instrument_snapshot` registered on the control stream
     for each US-equity asset id, read as the runtime's composer reads them. The executor refuses
     any instrument without one, so this covers every pinned and working-universe instrument.
   - **The cap.** At most 50, in order of first registration, so one workspace's registrations
     cannot crowd the shared key's budget. The rest get no bars, which means no signal and no
     opening.
   - **Ambiguous symbols.** Two asset ids naming one symbol both get no bars.
   - **Access.** The process gets a read-only journal role over the control stream and the
     registrations' artifacts, and appends nothing.
4. **Generations: nothing appended, nothing guessed.** Each fetch writes a new, complete generation
   that is never rewritten (DP-2), and a reader picks its generation from its own clock.
   - **Daily generation `D`:** the 30 regular sessions ending at `D`, unadjusted, with that span's
     corporate actions, under `alpaca-iex/daily/<D>/`. It is read at any clock whose
     `last_completed_regular_session` is `D`. It is written once `D`'s last calendar span (extended
     hours included) has ended, plus 15 minutes (`SETTLE`).
   - **Minute generation `M`:** the trailing window's complete `1Min` bars, under
     `alpaca-iex/minutes/<M>/`. It is written at each whole minute of the regular session, and read
     at `M` or up to two minutes after it.
   - **Missing generation:** the reader refuses. That means no closes and no signal (MI-10), and no
     liquidity figures and no opening.
   - **Empty session:** when `D` itself has no bar for an instrument, that instrument's generation
     is asked again every 5 minutes for up to 2 hours (`CONFIRM`) before it is written with the day
     empty. The trust check then refuses the instrument while the day is in the span.
   - **Wrong answers:** each generation refetches its whole span, so a vendor's revised, late or
     late answer lasts one generation at most; a true no-bar day stays refused for its 30 sessions.
     An instrument registered after a generation was written waits for the next one. A split in the span is refused by the trust check
     on the stored actions (E2-4).
   - **Retention:** daily generations are kept two sessions and minute generations ten minutes.
     Decisions cite the closes object, not a generation (item 6).

   This answers the review of #2811:
   - a stored dataset that could only grow, poisoned for good by one empty day;
   - a span that grew without bound;
   - a re-added instrument with a gap that never closed;
   - raw bars with no corporate actions, so splits went unseen;
   - minute figures with nowhere to come from.
5. **Batched and paced.**
   - **Daily generation:** one bars request for the whole span and every instrument (`symbols=` with
     several symbols, paged by the vendor's token), and one corporate-actions request.
   - **Minute generation:** one bars request.
   - **Malformed pages:** a page with a symbol not asked for, a duplicate, or a record outside the
     asked span refuses the generation.
   - **Pacing:** the E2-1 client's: 200 a minute, `429` waits, server errors back off.
   - **Cost:** about 1 request a minute in session per workspace, plus 2 a session, plus at most 24
     while confirming an empty day, whatever its instrument count. Each workspace offsets its
     fetches by a fixed delay derived from its id, so workspaces do not ask at once. This answers E7-2zq's lesson for bars.
6. **Closes object.** At every tick that finds its daily generation present, written then or
   before, the process puts each instrument's trusted closes object from it (DEC-846 item 3, source
   `alpaca_iex_daily_bars`). That is byte for byte what the composer derives from the same
   generation, so the runtime's `ObservationRecorded` names an object that is there (DEC-2385
   item 5). A crash between a write and a put is repaired at the next tick. One test reads both
   sides.
7. **Readers.** `mandate-bars` resolves an `alpaca-iex` root to the generation its clock names, and
   applies the trust check unchanged; a `cell-recorded` root reads as before. This is a
   safety-critical change.
   - The composer wires the signal models for either root.
   - The executor's binding gate and the runtime's dry run read daily and minute figures as today
     (DEC-2274), from the resolved generations, with no new port.
   - Exits never read stored bars (DEC-2274 item 3).
8. **Order of work.** Each piece lands separately, tests first, with mutants on the
   safety-critical crates:
   1. This spec and decision.
   2. The multi-symbol bars (span and window) and corporate-actions fetches, and the narrowed
      transport, in `mandate-marketdata`.
   3. The generations and the readers' resolution in `mandate-datad` and `mandate-bars`, and the
      composer's root in `mandate-runtime-models`.
   4. With lane IAC:
      - the vault's reader list and `BarsReader`;
      - provisioning;
      - the data process's journal role and egress;
      - the chart's `data.source`, refused for the recording outside `dev` and `ci`.
9. **Customers (founder, F1, a compliance risk accepted).**
   1. **Accepted:** the bars may be served to customers' workspaces, not only the founder's.
      - **The risk, plainly:** IEX bars fetched with one platform key and served to many workspaces
        are likely redistribution under Alpaca's market-data terms. That holds until a data license
        is recorded, or until each workspace fetches with its own data key (backlog E2-20).
      - This is an exception to data-plane spec item 3, §1.3 and DP-11, for these bars and the
        corporate actions that qualify them. HLD §12 risk 3 and DEC-433 item 3 are marked with it.
      - Quotes, marks, news to other workspaces, and every other datum stay as they were: the shared
        plane holds no market data, and nothing else is widened on its strength.
   2. **Proposed, the founder's to confirm:** serving customers this way puts the platform key in
      every customer cell's data process. It means a `bars-reader` vault role bound to each
      workspace's namespace, instead of DEC-2277 item 6's binding to the founder's workspace alone.
      - **The blast radius:** a compromised customer cell exposes the key. The key reaches only an
        empty paper account's data and news, and a rotation restarts every data process.
      - **The alternative:** one platform-side fetcher writing each workspace's generations, which
        keeps the key in one namespace but writes into tenants' stores.
      - Until the founder confirms one, the role is bound to the founder's workspace only, the most
        conservative reading of F1, and customers' cells run without bars (no signal).
   3. **The compliance document is the founder's to amend.** The text of
      `docs/product/08-compliance-and-regulatory.md` (DEC-79) is the founder's, and the founder
      step below says so.
10. **Invariants.** Data-plane spec DP-15 (never guessed), DP-16 (a wrong answer does not outlive
    its generation) and DP-17 (the key goes nowhere else). Each has a test with its own oracle in
    the code PRs.

## Consequences

- A hosted cell's crossover agent decides on real IEX daily closes. They are IEX's prints only, so
  thin names may have days with no bar, and those hold the signal (item 4).
- Liquidity:
  - IEX volume understates consolidated volume, so the volume and ADV figures err toward the
    tighter limit (DEC-470 item 4).
  - The prior close is IEX's last print, which can differ from the official close in either
    direction. The price floor may therefore admit or refuse a name near the floor that the
    consolidated close would not.
- Every workspace shares one key's budget with the news reader. Past about 150 workspaces in
  session, `429`s leave minute generations missing, which refuses openings and adds no risk.
- One wrong daily close can drive one session's signal. The signal is an opinion, sized and
  admitted by deterministic code under the owner's caps (rule 4).
- Founder steps (relayed by lane IAC, DEC-79):

  2. Run the infra apply once the bars-reader AppRole lands.
  3. Set the staging cell's `images.data` and `data.source: alpaca-iex`.
  4. Decide item 9.2.
  5. Amend the compliance document for item 9.1, whose text is reserved to the founder.
