## Context

DEC-506 corrected the executor's property oracles so that #668, the agent-scoped kill switch, could
take them live. CI's `test` job does not pin the proptest seed, and on #668's head three of them
failed about one run in four. A sweep over seeds 101 to 120 found:
- `a_client_order_id_is_a_function_of_the_intent_id_alone` failing at 4 of the 20 seeds;
- `no_interval_exceeds_the_limit_without_an_alert` failing at 5;
- `no_order_is_submitted_while_an_unconfirmed_cancel_is_outstanding` failing at 5.

None of the shrunk scripts for the first two needs the kill switch. Two of the faults are in the
oracles and one is in the executor. This DEC-77 tests correction changes test files and adds two
`BEHAVIOUR_ONLY_TESTS` rows; no `src/` file changes.

## Decision

1. **A ladder rung is judged as its own order.** Trading spec §2.3 and DEC-160 (10): "each rung is
   a new order (§5.6), so each takes its own ID"; rung n ≥ 1 is the exit's id followed by `-l{n}`.
   A rung has no `IntentReceived` of its own: its `OrderRequestRecorded` companion carries the exit's
   `intent_id` and its `rung`. Every failing seed shrank to one intent with its base id and its `-l1`
   rung; at seed 116 that was the flatten's `md-k-…-1` and `md-k-…-1-l1`. The oracle now keys on the
   pair (intent, rung), reading the rung from the same companion, where null counts as 0. Planted
   bug 7, an attempt-2 id suffixed `-a2`, is still caught: the doubted lead is an opening, and both
   its attempts share the key (intent 3, rung 0).
2. **A protective submission does not wait on any buy's cancel.** DEC-346 item 6: "An entry that
   turns terminal partly filled, after that cancel or by any other path (§5.4's second case), gets
   one GTC OCO at the bracket's prices", sized to the filled quantity and capped by the position
   left after every live protective order and every exit still selling. DEC-506 item 7 read that as
   placing the OCO whatever else is pending, then held it back behind a bracket entry's cancel. The
   coordinator's ruling in this session keeps DEC-346 item 6's placement, since the OCO is
   protection, and the oracle now lets a protective submission skip any buy's outstanding cancel.

   The bound. Let:
   - B be the position outside the two entries, C the protection resting in the instrument, and X
     the exits still selling;
   - f1 be entry 01's filled quantity and Q1 its whole quantity, 01's cancel still unconfirmed and
     its legs held;
   - f2 be the filled quantity of entry 02, the entry the OCO protects.

   The OCO is at most f2, its own entry's fill; the cap only lowers it. If 01's remainder fills
   late, the broker activates 01's legs, which are held until the entry is complete and sized to Q1
   (§5.4). The sells are then C + X + OCO + Q1, at most the position B + Q1 + f2 whenever
   C + X ≤ B. If 01's cancel succeeds instead, the sells are C + X + OCO ≤ B + f2 ≤ B + f1 + f2. In
   #668's minimal script (seed 103), B = C = X = 0, f1 = f2 = 1 and Q1 = 2: the OCO is 1, and after a
   late fill the sells are 2 + 1 = 3 against a position of 3. The hand case
   `a_terminal_entry_oco_covers_its_own_fill_not_the_room` pins the OCO at its entry's fill, 5, where
   the room is 11; with `room` planted for `filled.min(room)` it fails (11 against 5), and before it
   the whole executor crate passed that plant.

   The premise. The bound needs C + X ≤ B: no live protective order or exit covers the filled shares
   of a bracket entry whose legs are held. That is stronger than rule 12's Σ protective sells ≤
   position, whose position includes f1 and f2, and the executor does not keep it today. `re_place`
   (`protection.rs`, reached from `settle` → `replace` after an exit sequence, and from `new_day`'s
   re-placement before expiry) sizes on `long`, which includes a working bracket's ingested partial
   fills, while `covered` counts no held legs. With C + X between B and B + f2, the OCO placed during
   01's unconfirmed cancel oversells by C + X − B once 01 completes. For example, B = 10, a
   re-placement over f1 = 1 gives C = 11, Q1 = 2 and f2 = 1: the OCO is 1, and the sells are
   11 + 1 + 2 = 14 against a position of 13, where waiting for 01's cancel would give an OCO of 0 and
   13 against 13. The root cause is E1's: held legs are counted by no cap, so the precondition is
   itself a rule-12 defect already armed on its own. It is recorded on backlog row E1, which the
   coordinator schedules as the next executor fix after #668.

   The oracle stays strict for every other sell. An exit still waits on every outstanding cancel,
   rule 5's overdue carve-out aside (DEC-160 (7), (13), (18)); only a protective submission may skip
   a buy's cancel.
3. **An `unprotected_end` that names a bracket ends that bracket's interval (defect E4).** Trading
   spec §5.4: every interval is journaled from start to end and is bounded by `max_unprotected_s`.

   Defect E4. With two partly filled brackets in one instrument, the executor's fold ends the
   *first* open interval in the instrument and ignores the `bracket` the end names. The second
   bracket's end therefore closes the first's interval, and the bound then measures from the second
   start. At seed 101:
   - bracket 01 partly fills at 34, bracket 02 at 42;
   - 02 completes at 46 and its legs are placed;
   - at 96, 01's share has been unprotected for 62 s and there has been no alert, because `bound`
     would first fire at 102.

   This is an executor defect outside #668's code, so it is fixed in its own PR.

   The same defect has a second path. When the second bracket ends partly filled, its OCO ends the
   interval only at the broker's acknowledgment (DEC-348 item 2). That `unprotected_end` carries
   `acknowledged` and names no bracket, and the fold's arm for it also ends the first open interval
   in the instrument. The fix keys it to the bracket whose OCO was awaited, read back from the OCO's
   id (§2.3).

   This change carries four things for it:
   - The property's oracle keys the end to the interval whose start named the same bracket, and an
     end that names none closes the latest open interval as before. Without this, a fixed executor
     would fail the oracle whenever two brackets' ends arrive first-in, first-out.
   - The hand case `a_second_brackets_end_leaves_the_first_brackets_interval_bounded` runs two
     brackets in one instrument. It checks that the second's end leaves the first interval open, that
     the alert comes at the first tick `max_unprotected_s` after the first partial fill (70, not 80),
     and that nothing alerts again at 85: the first interval is alerted once and the second has ended.
   - The hand case `an_acknowledged_oco_for_a_second_bracket_leaves_the_first_brackets_interval_bounded`
     does the same through the second path: the second bracket ends partly filled at 30, and its OCO
     is acknowledged at 35.
   - Both cases reach no stub, so each is pending under a `BEHAVIOUR_ONLY_TESTS` row citing backlog
     E4. The fix deletes the rows and the `#[ignore]` lines. Backlog row E4 names both paths.
4. **An overdue cancel of a bracket entry can let sells exceed the position (defect E5, recorded).**
   On #668's head, two bracket entries' cancels journaled `cancel_overdue` end a risk exit's wait.
   The exit is submitted, sized on those entries' fills, while both entries still rest at the
   broker. Then one entry's cancel is confirmed and its OCO placed. If the other entry fills late,
   its legs activate, which no cap counted: sells of 1 + 1 + 2 against a position of 3. This is
   arithmetic, not a harness run: the harness cannot fill that entry behind the newer orders. It is
   backlog row E5, and the coordinator rules on it with E1 and E2. The oracle is unchanged for it,
   since DEC-160's overdue carve-out is accepted.
5. **What this leaves.** On `main` the three corrected properties still fail at the kill switch's
   stub, and `xtask ci pending` reports no executor problem. The two E4 hand cases fail on E4's
   behaviour, as their rows say. The OCO-cap case passes on `main`, since the cap exists, so it is
   live rather than pending: the pending gate refuses a pending test that passes. On #668's head, with these oracles and E4's fix applied together,
   the whole `mandate-executor` crate passed at every seed from 101 to 120. With the oracles alone,
   the client-order-id and cancel-wait properties each passed 20 of 20.
