## Decision

1. **The sentence that is not true.** §5.4 said "Protection is never removed for a passive exit."
   Two rules break it:
   - The hand-on rule on `main` (#286 round 2, M1′) cancels a passive exit's own placement for an
     exit that arrives later and no longer fits beside it.
   - #508 (DEC-419) cancels it at the placement, and at every step an exit waits, for an exit the
     gate allowed earlier.
2. **What §5.4 now says.** It keeps what is true and adds the rule:
   - The rest of the position keeps its stop, so a passive exit never removes protection by itself.
   - The new OCO's stop and the other exits' sells must fit within the position together, by one
     fit (`fits`) read the same way at every check, per DEC-425: whether the placement holds the
     exits back (`protection_holds`), whether it is cancelled for them (`overtaken`, at every step
     an exit waits, the placement's own step included), and whether an exit the gate allows leaves
     it resting (`begin`). The other exits are those still selling and those received with no order
     yet.
   - Exits the gate holds are left out of the fit at every one of those checks — allow, wait, and
     cancel — because they cannot move; a held exit counts again once released, and then either
     fits beside the placement or has it cancelled before it goes (DEC-425 item 2).
   - Where they do not fit, the new OCO is cancelled for them, like any protection before a
     marketable exit.
   - The cancel falls inside a journaled interval bounded by `max_unprotected_s`. Protection is
     re-placed for what is left once those exits end, or once the bound ends them.
3. **Until the founder rules,** the code on `main` stands. Without it, an allowed risk exit would
   wait for ever behind a placement, which rule 13 forbids outright. The cancel is bounded,
   journaled, and alerted at the bound.
4. **The second question is answered.** This decision first left the two checks differing: at an
   allow, held exits counted, so the placement could be cancelled for one that cannot yet move,
   while at a waiting step they were left out, and making them agree was left as a possible later
   code change, in either direction. DEC-425 has since resolved that question in the
   leave-held-out direction (the coordinator's option (A) on #524): one fit, `fits`, read by
   `protection_holds`, `overtaken`, and `begin`, leaves the exits the gate holds out at every
   check — allow, wait, and cancel — the placement's own step included. Item 2 states what §5.4
   says as a result, and no agreement question is left open here.

## Rationale

The spec has to describe what the executor does. The alternative to the cancel is to strand a risk
exit, and rule 13 rules that out. Every gap the cancel opens is already inside §5.4's bounded,
journaled interval, so the change adds no unbounded or silent window.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the sentence and do not place a passive exit that would not fit.** The placement is the
  passive exit's own order, so not sending it needs a new answer for an exit the gate allowed: re-gate
  it, send it marketable, or hold it. This is DEC-419's rejected alternative, and it is open to the
  founder here.
- **Keep the sentence and let the waiting exit wait for the bound.** Rejected: the bound abandons it,
  which refuses risk reduction (rule 13).
