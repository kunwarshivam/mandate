# DEC-2804: The account risk fold runs in the cell's executor: its opening per deployment, the executor fold's reading of its records, the account stream's tick, and the gate's risk snapshot (E6-15 slice C, lane RK's R4)

## Context

`mandate_executor::risk_state::AccountRiskFold` now reads marks, ticks, the agent's own fills and
fees (DEC-2393), and replays its own records (DEC-2399). Nothing deployed calls it, which leaves
three gaps:
- The cell's executor process (`mandate-executord`) never builds the fold.
- The executor's own fold refuses the records the risk fold returns (`RiskDayStarted` as a later
  slice, the rest as `E6-4`), so the first one appended would stop the process.
- The binding gate (lane X, DEC-2380 item 2) has no risk snapshot to read, so it holds every opening
  of an agent that has ordered.

## Invariants first

- The binding gate never judges an opening on a risk state the journal does not support: an
  unread or unreadable view holds the opening (`AGENTS.md` rule 3).
- No reading here holds an exit, a protective order, a risk exit, or a kill switch (rule 13).
- Every risk record is journaled before the next input is handed (rule 5, DEC-2307).
- A replay of the account stream reaches the view the running process had (DEC-2399).

## Decision

1. **The executor fold reads the risk fold's records as records** (C0). `RiskDayStarted`,
   `RiskLimitTriggered`, `RiskLimitLifted`, `HighWaterMarkReset`, `InstrumentRestrictionChanged`
   and `GoalCompleted` fold as records that move nothing in `ExecutorState`; the risk fold is
   their reader. `PositionReleased`, the seventh risk record of DEC-2399 item 1, the executor fold
   already reads. An `AgentModeApplied` the risk fold returns is the executor's own originated mode
   record, which the executor fold already reads. `MandateVersionApplied` and `UniverseChanged`
   keep their stories (R2b, E17-3). Nothing in the executor acts on a rung's `flatten_and_pause` or
   `trim`, or on `GoalCompleted`'s `on_complete`, until R5: these records stop refusing the process,
   and R5 owns the actions.
2. **One fold per deployed agent, opened flat at its first deployment on the account, from inputs
   fixed by the journal as of that deployment.** The process follows each agent the control stream
   deploys on the account (`follow::agents_of`). For each one that has not stopped, the process
   opens an `AccountRiskFold` and folds the account stream into it from seq 1. Every input is fixed
   by rows the journal already holds, so a replay at any later head opens the same fold
   (DEC-2399):
   - **The deployment** is the agent's first `AgentDeployed` (version 2 or 3) naming one of the
     account's connections. An agent id is deployed once and its stop is final (journal spec v0.56,
     DEC-1173), so a later `AgentDeployed` of the same agent is a redeployment of a running agent.
     When it names the same `mandate_version` it changes nothing. When it names another, it is a
     version change, R2b's: until R2b lands, the fold is not opened, and redeploying never resets
     E, H, E₀, the latched limits or the rungs.
   - **The opening instant** (`Opening.at`) is that `AgentDeployed`'s `event_time`, floored to the
     whole second. The fold opens flat.
   - **The version** is the one that `AgentDeployed` names, confirmed and validated as
     `mandate_gate_inputs::control` does, over the control rows up to that `AgentDeployed`'s seq.
     `inherited_loss_usd` is the connection's loss carry in that same context (mandate spec §5.7).
   - **The equity** the version is validated on is chosen by journal order, never by the head at
     process start. The boundary row is the first account-stream row whose `event_time` is at or
     after the opening instant. The equity is the last `AccountSnapshotRecorded` journaled before
     that row, or, when there is none, the first one journaled after it. The fold is not opened
     until both the boundary row and that snapshot are journaled. From then on no later row can
     change the choice.
   - **The instrument** is the deployment's reviewed instrument (DEC-2380 item 5), with its asset
     class. `mark_max_age_s` is the reviewed quote staleness, in whole seconds.

   The process reads the control stream on every pass. When a row appears that means a fold would
   not be opened (a redeployment on another version, or item 8's second agent), the process drops
   the agent's open fold, as a replay that saw that row would never have opened it.

   An agent whose fold is not opened has an unreadable view, with the cause `not_opened` (the #2120
   review's follow-up). Its openings are held, and the owner sees the hold through the executor's
   `opening_held` alert. Its exits, protective orders, risk exits and kill switch are not held. No
   fold is opened for a stopped agent.
3. **Rows before the deployment are not the agent's.** The fold compares a row's own clock with
   the opening instant: a `ClockAdvanced`'s `clock`, and every other risk input's `risk_clock`
   (journal spec §2). A row whose clock is strictly before the opening instant steps nothing,
   whatever instrument it names. A row in the opening's own second is the agent's, as a later row
   is. Such a row names an instrument the agent may trade, the agent is flat, and a mark there moves
   no figure but the mark's age. An own fill or fee before the opening instant cannot be (an order
   names its agent only after the deployment). The fold refuses to decide on one, and the view
   fails closed.
4. **The executor meets DEC-2399 item 3's contract.** After every committed batch, and after the
   start's replay before any other write, the process folds the batch's stored rows into every
   agent's fold and appends each fold's `owed()` as the next batch. An owed batch in doubt is
   offered again first, with the same event ids, before any other append, the next `ClockAdvanced`
   copy included.
   - **Nothing the book cannot append stops the executor** (`AGENTS.md` rule 13). An owed batch
     the journal refuses, records that cannot be drafted, or folds still owing after a bounded
     number of batches make every fold unreadable (`append_failed`): openings are held, and the
     process goes on.
   - **An owed batch genuinely in doubt holds every append, the kill switch's included, only until
     the journal answers.** This is a hold rule 13 does not list, bounded to the doubt and cleared
     by the journal's answer, as [DEC-2805](DEC-2805.md) item 5 records for a refusal. The
     executor's own record of applying the switch cannot be journaled, and so no cancel or close
     sent, before the journal answers (rule 5); the executor already holds this way for its own
     batches and marks in doubt. A journal that stays in doubt holds the switch for as long as the
     doubt lasts, and the operator is alerted once per outage (`journal_unavailable`); the switch
     applies on the first pass the journal answers.
5. **The account stream's tick** (DEC-2307 item 5, DEC-2399 item 5). On each scheduler tick the
   process hands the tick to the executor core and then steps a copy of each opened fold with the
   `ClockAdvanced` copy it would journal. The process journals that copy on the account stream when
   either:
   - any fold's copy returns a record; or
   - the tick crosses New York midnight from the last copy journaled, whether or not any fold is
     readable (journal spec §2).

   The copy's `clock` is the tick's, and its `causation_id` is the scheduler's event. The records
   then owed follow in the next batch. A tick that returns nothing is not journaled. The live fold
   does not step it either, so a replay, which never sees it, reaches the same view. Its clock is
   not the fold's until a journaled row carries it. That changes no result (MI-13), because every
   risk input carries its own `risk_clock`.
6. **The calendar** is `mandate-time`'s US-equities exchange calendar. For an equity it counts
   regular-session seconds and answers each instant's session (`overnight` while the market is
   closed). For crypto it counts every second and answers `crypto` (mandate spec §5.2). A clock that
   goes back is refused for both.
7. **The binding gate's risk snapshot** (C1). `mandate_executor::risk_state::risk_snapshot(view)` is
   `Some((RiskSnapshot, AgentMode))` for a readable view, with E, H, E₀, C, L, the latched limits,
   the active rungs, the size factor and the mode copied from the risk state's snapshot. It is
   `None` for an unreadable view. The gate then holds the opening. Mapping the risk state's
   `LimitKey` to the gate's is exhaustive. Both have exactly `max_daily_loss`, a drawdown rung,
   `lifetime_floor` and `profit_stop`, so a variant added to one fails the build rather than drop
   a latch. A tripwire latches no `LimitKey`: its `exits_only` reaches the gate through the mode,
   which the risk state folds it into (mandate spec §5.9). The instrument restrictions (staleness
   and universe) are not in the gate's `RiskSnapshot`. R4b hands them to the gate beside it, by
   instrument. An exit, a protective order, a
   risk exit and a kill switch never read it (DEC-2380 item 2's `input` answers `None` for every
   purpose that does not add risk).
8. **A second agent or a second instrument on an account fails closed at the fold now, and is
   refused at deploy in R4c.**
   - DEC-2308 item 4 stands: the fold binds one instrument, and a row naming another makes the view
     unreadable.
   - Executord marks every instrument its deployments trade on the account, so a second deployed
     agent there makes every fold on it unreadable. So does an agent whose universe may hold a
     second instrument (DEC-2310 item 3).
   - That must not happen silently or late. So the process opens no fold at all, with the cause
     `not_opened`, for any agent on an account where more than one agent that has not stopped is
     followed, or whose confirmed version's `universe.max_instruments` is above 1. Openings are held
     and exits go on.
   - Refusing such a deployment before it runs, with a typed and journaled reason, is R4c. It gets
     its own decision, because what it refuses touches DEC-117's proposed default and the local
     cell's two demo agents, which the coordinator rules on.
9. **Proposed (founder): another instrument's mark steps nothing.** A mark for an instrument other
   than the bound one would step nothing, instead of making the view unreadable. It moves no figure
   the risk state holds, because the agent's sub-ledger holds none of that instrument: E, H, E₀,
   the limits and the mode are the same with or without it. A property test (views equal with and
   without the other instrument's marks) would come with it. An own fill or fee on another
   instrument stays a refusal to decide, so the single-position risk state can never price a
   position on the wrong instrument's quote, which is all DEC-2308 item 4 guards. If the founder
   accepts it, the item-8 rule for a second agent can be withdrawn, and two single-instrument
   agents can share an account. The rule for a second instrument stays until the multi-instrument
   risk state lands. Until he decides, DEC-2308 item 4 and item 8 bind.

## Consequences

- R4a, in `mandate-executor`: item 1, the opening and pre-deployment readings (items 2 and 3) on
  `AccountRiskFold`, the calendar (item 6), and `risk_snapshot` (item 7), tests first.
- R4b, in `mandate-executord`: the `RiskBook` (items 2, 4, 5 and 8's refusal to open), with lane X
  wiring `risk_snapshot` into `AgentRisk`. It is tested on the executor process's fixture journal:
  the replay, a crash between a batch and its records, a crash between a `ClockAdvanced` copy and
  its records, midnight, a fill tripping a rung, a later snapshot and a redeployment that open
  nothing new, and a second agent.
- Until R4c or the multi-instrument risk state, an agent whose mandate keeps DEC-117's proposed
  `max_instruments` of 5 opens nothing: its fold is never opened (item 8).
- R4c: refusing a second agent or instrument at deploy (item 8), in its own decision and its own
  spec and code pair. Its scope includes a stopped agent's leftover position, orders and marks on
  the account. These make the next agent's fold unreadable (safe, with openings held), and R4c
  says whether a deployment waits for them.
- Rung actions (the flatten a `flatten_and_pause` asks for, the trim, cancelling openings on
  `exits_only`) are R5. Owner acknowledgments and versions are R2b; until they land, either
  makes the view unreadable, holding openings only.

## Alternatives considered

- **Open the fold at the account's head when the process first sees the deployment.** Refused: the
  head depends on when the process ran, so a replay would open it elsewhere. The deployment's
  instant is in the journal.
- **Journal every scheduler tick on the account stream.** Refused: one row a second per account
  adds nothing a replay needs (MI-13).
- **Open the fold on the latest snapshot, version and loss carry when the process starts.**
  Refused: a restart after a later snapshot or carry would open the fold on other inputs, and a
  replay would not reach the live view (the spec review's finding).
- **Open the fold at the latest deployment.** Refused: redeploying a running agent would reset its
  limits and rungs.
- **Refuse `max_instruments` above 1 as a V-rule on every mandate.** Refused here: it would make
  DEC-117's proposed default unconfirmable everywhere, not only at deploy (DEC-2310 item 3). R4c
  decides the deploy-time refusal.
