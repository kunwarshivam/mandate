## Context

[DEC-2126](DEC-2126.md) item 7 scoped the passkey wire and verifier to a connect's start. A
version's confirmation (`POST /mandate-versions/{v}/confirm`) and a deployment (`POST /agents`)
also need step-up, of kinds `confirm_version` and `deploy` (DEC-2101), and the server's port
admits neither through `cli_confirm` (DEC-2104 admits paper `approve` alone, and DEC-2125 adds a
paper connect). So in the web app both are refused today: `step_up_method` for `cli_confirm`, and
`step_up_missing` for `passkey`, since neither body can carry the assertion the passkey verifier
judges. DEC-2108 items 7 and 8 already decide the wire and the verifier for every kind the
challenge route issues; this decision applies them to these two routes.

## Decision

1. **The wire, as DEC-2126 item 1.** `commands/confirm-request` and the deploy body (§3.11 item 11,
   decoded strictly by the server until its schema lands) each gain an optional
   `step_up_assertion`: DEC-2108 item 7's object (`envelope.schema.json#/$defs/StepUpAssertion`)
   or `null`, an absent member read as `null`.
   - `step_up` of method `passkey` with no assertion is `step_up_missing`.
   - An assertion beside `step_up` of method `cli_confirm`, or beside a `null` `step_up`, is
     `invalid` at `/step_up_assertion`, one violation, among the body checks, before
     `step_up_required`.
2. **The verifier is DEC-2126 item 2's**, through the port's `asserted` method (DEC-2126 item 4),
   bound once and dispatched on `step_up.method`. Each route judges the assertion against the
   action it already builds: kind `confirm_version` or `deploy`, with the route's digest.
3. **The commit, as DEC-2126 item 3.** A confirmation or deployment under passkey step-up stores
   the assertion's canonical bytes as an artifact, then appends its one control-stream event
   (`MandateConfirmed`, or `AgentDeployed`) and the `StepUpAsserted` in one batch, in that
   order, the assertion's `causation_id` the event (journal spec §9.32 rules 253 and 254). A
   refused call appends nothing. A repeat of a committed call (§3.4 step 1) answers as today and
   judges no assertion, since the challenge it named is spent.
4. **No route changes what it requires.** A call that needs no step-up today still needs none, and
   one carrying an assertion it does not rest on is refused by item 1. `live` stays
   `live_unavailable`, before any step-up is judged, as today.
5. **Order of the changes, as DEC-2126 item 5.** `mandate-api`'s `ConfirmRequest` and the server's
   deploy body gain the member in the first route's tests change, before the schema text names it;
   this change carries the schema and the spec. Each route then lands as its own tests and
   implementation pair: confirmation first, then deployment.
6. **Scope.** An approval's grant is not in this decision: the API does not yet serve a grant
   under any method (`POST /approvals/{id}/responses` records a Skip and refuses every grant for
   its step-up), and serving one is its own slice, with the runtime judging the evidence as
   mandate spec §6.1 says. Stop, resume and lifting a hold carry the same member when their routes
   serve passkey step-up, in their own slices. DEC-2126 item 6, the end of DEC-2125's interim, is
   unchanged.

## Rationale

- Items 1 to 3 add no rule: they are DEC-2126's, applied to two more routes that commit one
  control-stream event, the shape §9.32's batch rule names.
- Item 3's repeat answer keeps §3.4's idempotency: the key, not the assertion, names the call, and
  a spent challenge cannot be judged twice (rule 254).
- Item 6 keeps a grant out because the gap there is the grant itself, not its method.

## Alternatives considered

- **One decision per route.** Rejected: the readings are the same, and one decision keeps them
  from drifting.
- **Including the approval grant.** Rejected for item 6's reason.
