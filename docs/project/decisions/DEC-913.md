## Context

`ConnectionAnchor::from_verified` (DEC-889 item 1) folds the rows a `VerifiedPrefix` bound and
anchors the last row's stream. `VerifiedPrefix::bind` verifies §11's checks 1 to 6, which walk rows
by position in the order given, so a prefix that chains two streams' rows into one hash chain —
`seq` 1 to n contiguous and each `prev_hash` the row before it — binds exactly when the trusted
start commits to its tail. `from_verified` then folds each stream on its own, and nothing breaks a
rule in a clean pair of streams, so it gives an anchor for the last row's stream: the range judges
that one stream, and every other stream fails closed at its first judged record (DEC-889 item 2).

That reading passes nothing the full chain passes on: #1242's "Not done" already noted no false
pass exists, because a rewritten row fails `bind`. But the hold anchor folded from the same prefix
reads a wider rule: DEC-892 item 6 gives `HeldAnchor::Unknown` for "a prefix that is not one agent
stream's", covering "rows of more than one stream", because "such a prefix is not a chain the fold
can read". Two anchors of one prefix should not disagree on whether that prefix is a chain at all.

## Decision

1. **A mixed-stream prefix anchors nothing.** `ConnectionAnchor::from_verified` gives
   `ConnectionAnchorError::Broken` when the prefix's rows are not all on one stream. This is
   DEC-892 item 6's reading for the connection anchor: rows of more than one stream are not one
   stream's chain, which is what DEC-889 item 1's anchor is. An empty prefix keeps its meaning
   (DEC-889 item 2): no rows, no stream, the anchor of the range's first judged record's stream.
2. **The caller runs the range unanchored**, as it does for any refused anchor (DEC-892 item 3):
   the range fails closed with `ConnectionCheck::Unanchored` at its first judged record, of any
   stream — the stream the looser reading anchored included, whose records then pass.
3. **`VerifiedPrefix::bind` is unchanged.** It is the shared check, built by lane L5 and accepted
   by DEC-892 item 1 for every anchor fold; the approved scope of this change is the connection
   anchor only, so the refusal is `from_verified`'s, the connection anchor's own constructor.
4. **Nothing else moves.** A prefix of one stream still binds and anchors its range's stream
   exactly as DEC-889 accepted, and `bind` verifying a mixed chain is not itself refused here.

## Rationale

A prefix whose rows span two streams reaches `from_verified` only through a writer that numbered
one range's rows of several streams as one chain, or a caller that handed two streams' rows to the
anchor as one prefix: §2 gives a stream its own numbering, and §11's checks verify the chain they
are given, not the streams it names. Today's reading refuses everything the tightened reading
refuses at every stream but the last row's; the only difference is that one stream, judged from a
fold whose rows another stream owns a part of. Refusing it closes that gap while binding no
caller that the looser reading bound: DEC-890's callers read one stream at a time. And it gives
the two anchors of one prefix one answer to "is this prefix a chain", so the next reader cannot
copy the looser one where the tighter belongs. Refusing more and admitting nothing, it is a
DEC-176 tightening.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep today's looser reading**, leaving the follow-up proposed by #1242 open. Rejected: the
  founder approved the tightening, and DEC-892 item 6 already read a mixed prefix as no chain for
  the hold anchor of the same prefix.
- **Refuse in `VerifiedPrefix::bind`.** Rejected: `bind` is the shared check of every anchor fold,
  and changing it moves L5's hold anchor and DEC-892 item 1's accepted semantics; the approved
  scope is the connection anchor only.
- **Anchor each stream from its own rows.** Rejected already by DEC-889: no caller needs it, and
  DEC-890's callers read one stream at a time.
