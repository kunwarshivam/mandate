## Decision

1. **What DEC-189 fixes.** The figure is "the order value the agent could still submit today without
   an ask, bounded by the `auto` paths, each live delegation's remaining caps, `max_orders_per_day`,
   `max_order_usd`, and gross-exposure headroom, computed from the mandate and the journal", display
   only. It names the bounds but not how they combine, what "today" and "live" mean against the risk
   clock, how much of a rule's condition counts, what an unknown input shows, or the rounding. DEC-189
   put the formula in the product brief; the backlog moved it to mandate spec §4.2 beside the
   confirmation screen's other dollar figures, with a reference-model function the web figure is
   tested against. This decision states it there.
2. **The formula** (mandate spec §4.2, `unasked_usd` in `reference/mandate/ref.py`). An upper bound
   on the order value of opening and increasing orders the agent could have decided `auto` from the
   risk clock *t* to the end of its risk day. Within the day only a new version or a policy change can
   raise it. Each `auto` path is a set of slices:
   - An `auto` default or `auto` rules give one unlimited slice. Its size is the largest per-order
     amount they allow: `max_order_usd`, narrowed by an `order_usd` bound in the rule's condition.
   - Each delegation whose window meets the rest of the risk day gives slices of its per-order cap,
     until its remaining orders or remaining total run out.

   The `max_orders_per_day` still left picks the largest slices. Their sum is the figure, rounded up
   to the cent, with exact decimals throughout. `max_orders_per_day` is required by the schema (1 to
   10,000), so the figure is always finite.
3. **Unknown is never 0.** A missing journal input makes the figure unknown, shown as "not known"
   (`AGENTS.md` rule 3). The journal inputs are the risk clock, today's order count, and delegation
   usage. It is a known 0 only when the review date has passed (§6.2 step 5b), the version is
   `policy_nonconforming`, or the effective `auto_allowed` is false (step 5c). A policy that is not
   known reads as allowing `auto`, the larger figure.
4. **Conservative readings taken in the interim.** Each gives the larger figure, so the owner is
   never shown less than can run without them:
   1. "Today" runs from *t* to the end of the risk day. A delegation starting later today counts now;
      DEC-189's "right now" would leave it out.
   2. The agent's mode and delegation suspension (§6.5 condition 5) do not lower the figure; the
      state is shown beside it. A suspension can lift within the day without the owner.
   3. Everything the figure leaves out can only stop an order from running unasked, never let one
      run, so leaving it out can only make the figure larger. It leaves out:
      - every condition field but an `order_usd` bound (`bought_today_usd`, `gross_usd_after`,
        `purpose`, instrument, session);
      - rule order, and `ask` and `deny` rules;
      - the admission and client ceilings;
      - the position cap, cooldowns, and sessions;
      - the gross exposure limit.
   4. **No gross-headroom cap.** DEC-189 lists gross-exposure headroom as a bound. But headroom
      measured at *t* can grow within the day: marks fall, working opening orders are cancelled or
      expire, and exits fill. A cap taken at *t* can therefore understate. The PR's first revision
      had one, and the reviewer's counterexample showed a figure of 0 while a 1,000 USD order could
      later run `auto`. The reference fuzz now frees headroom within the day, and a seeded mutant
      restoring that cap is caught.
5. **Workspace API §5.3's `unasked_usd_after`** is the same figure for the preview's version at the
   agent's current state: a decimal string, or `null` when unknown.
6. **The loss answer** (DEC-182: it maps only to `capital.max_loss_from_allocation`,
   `risk.max_drawdown`, and `risk.max_daily_loss`; mandate spec §7, `loss_answer_fields`).
   - A fraction, or a dollar amount divided by the allocation, is rounded down to whole basis points
     to give F.
   - `max_loss_from_allocation` is F (`user_stated`).
   - `max_drawdown` D is 0.8 × F and `max_daily_loss` is 0.2 × F. Both are `platform_proposed` and
     inactive until confirmed (V-020, MI-12).
   - These are the base mandates' ratios (0.1, 0.08, 0.02), so V-014 holds.
   - An F of 0, or of 1 or more, is asked again, and so is an answer too small for item 7's ladder.
7. **The ladder the drafter proposes beneath D** (`proposed_ladder`). This is a separate
   `platform_proposed` proposal, not part of the loss answer's mapping, and the owner confirms it like
   any other field. It is needed because V-011 puts the flatten rung at exactly `max_drawdown`; a
   drafted D with the template's ladder fails V-011. The first revision did exactly that: F = 0.05,
   0.12, and 0.2 each gave V-011.
   - The rungs sit at the base mandate's fractions of D (0.03, 0.06, 0.08 of 0.08):
     - `scale_sizes` with factor 0.5 at 0.375 × D;
     - `exits_only` at 0.75 × D;
     - `flatten_and_pause` at exactly D.
   - `risk.hysteresis` is 0.125 × D (0.01 of 0.08).
   - All but the flatten rung are rounded down to whole basis points.
   - If rounding leaves any of them at 0, or breaks hysteresis < the two lower rungs < D (V-010,
     V-012), the answer is refused and asked again. No rung is dropped: dropping one would propose a
     ladder that de-risks later than the template's, and that is not the safe outcome. In practice
     this refuses an F below about 0.1 percent.
   - The reference fuzz runs `semantic()` on every drafted result.
8. **Questions for the founder**, each with the interim this change takes:
   1. Should the figure be a snapshot of "right now"? That would drop to 0 while delegations are
      suspended or the agent is not in `normal` mode, and would leave out delegations not yet
      started. The alternative is the rest of the risk day, as written. Interim: the rest of the day,
      which gives the larger figure.
   2. Should more of a rule's condition narrow the figure, such as `bought_today_usd` as a day total
      or the position cap per instrument? Interim: `order_usd` bounds only.
   3. Should a tighter cap be defined, one aware of exits and marks? For example, the gross limit
      less the exposure that cannot be freed today. Interim: no cap (item 4.4).
   4. Are 0.8 and 0.2 of the loss answer the right proposed drawdown and daily loss? Or should the
      drafted drawdown leave a wider gap below the floor for gaps and exit pricing? Interim: 0.8 and
      0.2, both shown as proposed and confirmed by the owner.
   5. Does deriving the ladder and hysteresis from the proposed drawdown conflict with DEC-182's "the
      loss answer maps only to" three fields? Interim: derive them as a separate `platform_proposed`
      proposal the owner confirms (item 7). The loss answer itself still sets only the three fields.
9. **Interim while Proposed.** The formula, the mapping, and the ladder live in the spec text and
   the reference model only. No reference case, fixture, Rust code, or web figure depends on them,
   and nothing in the gate or the autonomy decision reads them.

## Rationale

DEC-189 makes autonomy a dollar amount. A figure that the web and the reference disagree on, that
shows 0 when it does not know, or that understates would mislead the owner exactly where they decide
how much to hand over. An upper bound computed from the same inputs §6.2 decides on can be checked,
and the reference fuzz checks it three ways:

- It runs the real autonomy decision over adversarial days, with headroom freed by marks, cancels,
  and exits, and asserts that nothing runs unasked beyond the figure.
- It asserts that a greedy day reaches the figure in a family of mandates with only `lte` bounds and
  no `ask` or `deny` rule ahead of an `auto` one.
- `reference/mandate/mutants.py` seeds 23 bugs, and each one is caught.

The loss mapping keeps the owner's one essential answer as the floor. It proposes the two tighter
limits, and a ladder that validates, beneath it.

## Alternatives considered

- **A snapshot "right now"** (question 1): smaller, but it would read 0 during a short suspension
  and understate a delegation that starts in an hour.
- **A gross-headroom cap at *t*** (question 3): rejected because it understates (item 4.4).
- **The exact maximum over every condition field:** needs a solver over the §6.3 language, and a
  model that cannot be checked independently.
- **Showing only the dial's label:** DEC-189 rejected it.
- **Mapping the loss answer to `max_loss_from_allocation` only,** with the other two left for the
  owner to enter: the three questions would become five.
- **Dropping a collapsed rung instead of refusing** (item 7): it would propose a ladder that
  de-risks later than the template's.
