# DEC-905: The landing page shows the app itself in a second browser tab, opens each visit on a painting picked at random, and moves: it powers on, its wallpaper drifts and leans, its windows lean and glide when thrown, and its icons spring

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-10 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | The founder, who asked to "dont write pc/mac", to "remove pattern desktop wallpaper", to "randomly select wallpaper", to "add a tab in the browser to actual show the screen of inside app", and to "learn from posthog" and make the page "extremely pretty", adding "modern motion design" so it will "stand out like crazy", saying "you can break the design"; the agent chose the details under DEC-79, since none touches money, legal text or a safety rule |
| **Story or claim** | Landing page (DEC-212, DEC-218, DEC-903, DEC-904) |

## Decision

1. **The switch writes no name.** The other desktop's computer on the desktop is a picture alone:
   no "Mac" or "PC" under it. Its accessible name is still the computer's, and its description
   still says what it does. It leaves the Start menu and the owl's menu, which would have to write
   it; the keyboard reaches it in Display Properties, under "Desktop", as the two computers'
   pictures, named "Windows 98" and "System 7" for assistive technology only. This amends DEC-904
   item 2.
2. **No grey pattern; a painting per visit.** The Mac's desktop is a painting too, so the pattern
   and its "Desktop pattern" choice go (amends DEC-904 item 4). The `(site)` layout picks one of
   the paintings at random on each request (`randomWallpaper` in `art.ts`) and hands it to the
   pages, so the server and the browser draw the same one and the sign-in pages share it. A
   painting picked in Display lasts until the page is reloaded, as before; "Day and night" is still
   offered.
3. **The app in a second tab.** The landing page's browser has two tabs, "Owlhead Home Page" and
   "Inside the app" (`browser.tsx`). The second shows the app's own screens, Home with requests
   waiting, a request, an agent, its record and its mandate, captured from the example workspace in
   both themes by `scripts/app-screens.mjs` into `public/app/` (`app-tab.tsx`). They play in turn,
   each for its progress bar's seven seconds, while a pixel cursor labelled "You" springs to the
   part each screen is about; pointing at the tab or focusing in it holds the screen on show, and
   picking one stops the play. The address follows the screen (`https://app.owlhead.ai/…`), and a
   caption says what each shows. The hero gains "Look inside the app", which opens the tab; a link
   to a part of the home page brings its tab back. The screens are images, not a live frame,
   because the app needs a session and the site sends `X-Frame-Options: DENY`.
4. **Motion that makes the desktop physical.** Following PostHog's desktop site and the motion of
   the galleries the founder shared (Godly, Saaspo, the Liveblocks hero's cursors):
   - **Power on.** Once per page load the painting warms up out of the dark, the taskbar or menu
     bar slides in, the icons spring up one after another, and the home window settles, in about
     1.3 seconds (`letter.module.css`, `boot*`).
   - **A living wallpaper.** The painting drifts across its canvas over two minutes, as a museum
     film pans, and leans a few pixels away from a fine pointer (`wallpaper.tsx`).
   - **Windows with weight.** A dragged window leans up to 2.5 degrees into its travel about the
     point it is held by, levels when the pointer rests, and when let go mid-throw glides on a
     bounded distance and springs level (`window-motion.ts`: `leanFor`, `glideFor`, `settle`).
   - **Icons that answer.** A desktop icon springs up under the pointer and dips when pressed.
   Reduced motion drops all of it, and none of it runs from the keyboard.
5. **Which rules this breaks, on the landing page only.** `web/DESIGN.md` says nothing in the
   frame moves on its own (DEC-215) and that interactions stay under 300ms; the drift, the screens'
   play, the power-on and the springs break both, on the signed-out pages alone, as the founder
   allowed. The product's own screens keep every rule. No gradient, glow or weight above 600 was
   needed, so those guards stand.

## Rationale

The landing page said what Owlhead does but never showed it; the app's own screens, played with a
cursor that points at what matters, show the product in the first minute, which is what PostHog's
site does inside its desktop. Pictures rather than mock-ups keep it honest: they are the app as it
renders the example workspace, and they say so. The motion is what makes a desktop of windows feel
like an object rather than a drawing of one, and each piece starts from where things are and stays
interruptible, as DEC-903's window motion does.

## Alternatives considered

- **A live frame of the app.** The app needs a session and refuses to be framed.
- **A recorded video in the tab.** It would go stale silently and could not be paused per screen
  or follow the theme; the stills are re-captured by one script.
- **Keeping the Start menu entry.** Any menu line has to write the computer's name, which the
  founder asked not to.
- **A boot screen before the desktop.** It would hold back the page; the power-on plays over the
  page as it paints.
