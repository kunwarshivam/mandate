# DEC-654: How `change_roles` reads identity spec §4.5 and §5.2: the step-up it yields, who counts as an active owner or admin, and roles of the other scope

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-09 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | The lane L1 builder agent, under DEC-79 and DEC-176: each item refuses at least as much as the spec's plain reading, or carries a requirement that reading would drop, and grants nothing new |
| **Story or claim** | E9-2 (claim #757), part c: the pending tests for `change_roles` and `PrincipalContext::into_tenant`; [identity spec](../../specs/identity.md) §4.2, §4.5, §5.1, §5.2, §8.3; ID-4, ID-8, ID-13; items 7 to 9 from the tests correction after the part c implementation's review |

## Decision

1. **`change_roles` yields the step-up the whole change needs.** Its stub returned `Result<(),
   Refusal>`, which drops what §4.5 says an authorization carries ("an authorization carries the
   row's step-up requirement"), and ID-4 requires step-up for granting a role. It now returns
   `Result<StepUp, Refusal>`, resolved for the change: `StepUp::Required` when any part needs it (a
   grant at a workspace's scope, the "S for grant" cell; any change through "Org memberships and
   org roles", whose cell is `S`), otherwise `StepUp::NotRequired` (removing a workspace role,
   deactivating a workspace member, and the leave row). It never yields `ForInvite` or `ForGrant`.
   §7 still verifies the evidence.
2. **An active owner or admin** (§5.2's `last_owner` and `last_admin`) is a member whose membership
   in that organization or workspace is in the state `active`, and whose org owner or workspace
   admin role is effective at `now` (its effective-from instant at or before it, §8.3). A
   `cooling_off`, `invited`, `deactivated` (suspended), `removed`, `expired`, or `revoked`
   membership counts zero, as does a membership in another organization or workspace. A successor
   granted the role in the same change counts when their membership is `active`: §8.3 gives an org
   owner or workspace admin grant no cool-off, so the role is effective at once.
3. **A role of the other scope's kind is `forbidden`.** A change at a workspace's scope that grants
   or removes an org role, or one at an organization's scope that names a workspace role, is
   refused `forbidden`: no row of §4.2 grants it (§4.1, no inheritance either way).
4. **The order of refusals** is §4.5's: first the rows the change uses, each through `authorize`
   (the roles row for a grant or removal, the members row for deactivating another member, the
   leave row for the author's own deactivation), then item 3, then `own_roles`, then
   `owner_role_reserved`, then `last_owner` or `last_admin`. One entry naming the author refuses
   the whole change.
5. **Lowering one's own roles** is a removal of a role of its own author, so it is refused
   `own_roles` like raising them, with or without step-up (ID-13, DEC-643). The way down is the
   leave row, which needs no step-up and is still refused `last_owner` or `last_admin`.
6. **An entry that changes nothing** (granting a role the member already holds, or removing one
   they do not hold) is judged like any other entry: it passes or is refused by the same rows and
   checks, changes nothing, and counts toward the change's step-up as its kind does, so a grant
   that changes nothing still needs step-up. It is never a refusal of its own: §4.2 and §4.5 name
   no such refusal, and treating it as one would only make retries fail (the reading that adds no
   risk, DEC-176; raised by the #905 review).
7. **A grant to a non-member, or a change naming no member, is `forbidden`.** A grant to a
   principal whose membership does not reach the scope (none there, one only in another
   organization or workspace, or one `invited`, `deactivated`, `removed`, `expired`, or `revoked`;
   §5.1) is refused `forbidden`, never `Ok`: granting is what must never reach a non-member, and an
   `Ok` grant applied by a caller would give a role to a principal no one invited (§5.2, "Invite"),
   outside the lifecycle of §5.1. A removal from, or a deactivation of, a principal with no
   membership in the scope at all is refused `forbidden` too: there is nothing to act on, and an
   `Ok` would be an oracle for who is a member. A removal from, or a deactivation of, a member
   whose membership is in the scope but `invited` or `deactivated` is judged like any other entry
   (as item 6): it only reduces access, so refusing it adds no safety and would block offboarding;
   an admin must be able to strip roles from a suspended member. The last-owner and last-admin
   rules still apply; they count only `active` holders, so that member never makes them refuse. A
   `cooling_off` member is reached and passes. `no_membership` is not used, since §4.5 gives it to
   an author that does not reach the scope, and API-9 would answer the author 404 for a scope it
   can see. The refusal is judged with item 3, after the rows and before `own_roles`,
   `owner_role_reserved`, and `last_owner` or `last_admin`, and one such entry refuses the whole
   change (found while preparing the E9-2 part c implementation; narrowed on the lead's ruling).
8. **An empty change goes through the roles row.** A `RoleChange` with no grant, removal, or
   deactivation is judged by the roles row ("Grant or remove a workspace role" at a workspace, "Org
   memberships and org roles" at an organization), so an author that row refuses gets its
   refusal (`no_membership`, `forbidden`, or `reduction_only`, as `authorize` gives it), never
   `Ok`, `last_owner`, or `last_admin`. An empty change is then no oracle for whether a scope the
   author does not reach has an active owner or admin (ID-8). An author the row grants gets its
   step-up (`S` at an organization, none at a workspace), and §5.2 on the unchanged state.
9. **What the role checks read.**
   - (a) **A failed read refuses.** None of the rows a role change uses (the roles row, the
     members row, "Org memberships and org roles", the leave row) is among §4.5's risk-reducing
     rows (workspace API-7's operations and ID-5's never-gated reductions), so DEC-642 item 10's
     snapshot route never applies to them: when the author's membership read or `change_roles`'
     read of the scope's members fails, the change is refused `membership_unavailable`, and the
     session's roles snapshot, which holds only the author's roles, never stands in.
   - (b) **A cooling-off owner role is held.** For `owner_role_reserved`, a member holds the org
     owner role once it is recorded, even while it is still cooling off (§8.3) or the membership
     is `cooling_off`, so deactivating them is reserved to an org owner; and an author is an org
     owner only when that role is effective at `now` in a membership reaching the organization.
     Under the other reading a non-owner could deactivate an owner who is about to become active.

## Rationale

The tests must assert one outcome per case, so each of these readings is pinned. Items 2 to 5 and
7 to 9 only refuse: none lets a change through that the plain reading refuses. Item 1 surfaces a requirement
the old signature lost, so a caller cannot commit a grant without asking for step-up.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep `Result<(), Refusal>` and let the caller derive step-up from the change.** Every caller
  would re-derive §4.2's cells, which is the second authority ID-2 rules out.
- **Count a `cooling_off` owner or admin as active.** It reaches its scope (§5.1), but the spec's
  text says `active`; counting it would let the last active owner leave behind an owner who is not
  yet active.
- **Ignore a role of the other scope's kind.** Silently drops part of the request the caller made.
