## Decision

1. **The app, live, in front.** The landing page's browser opens on "Inside the app", now its first
   tab, and the tab holds the app itself in a frame (`app-tab.tsx`), not screenshots. Every screen,
   link and button works as in the app, on the example workspace (`buildWorkspace("approvals")`)
   and the fixture runtime. This amends DEC-905 item 3: the screens' play, the "You" cursor, the
   captions and `public/app/` with `scripts/app-screens.mjs` go.
2. **The browser drives it.** Back, Forward, Home and Reload, in the toolbar and the Go and View
   menus, act on the app: Back and Forward step through its own history and grey out at either
   end, Home opens its dashboard, Reload starts it over. The address shows where it is
   (`https://app.owlhead.ai/…`) and takes a typed one, `app.owlhead.ai/agents`, `https://…` or
   `/agents`; anything else goes nowhere (`addressPath`). The page's own address never changes.
   The status bar says it is contacting the host until the app has drawn. On the home page's tab
   the chrome does what it did.
3. **How it is framed.** The app runs at `/demo` (`app/demo/page.tsx`, `components/demo/`), which
   stands in for Next's router and pathname and keeps its history in memory, so links and
   `router.push` move between screens; `demoScreen` routes exactly as the `(app)` pages do, which
   `demo.test.tsx` checks path by path. A link to a sign-in page opens it in the whole window. The
   page and the frame talk by `postMessage`, each accepting only the other window on this origin
   (`demo-messages.ts`). The frame wears the page's theme and colour-blind palette and follows them
   as they change. Its clock stands still, so the requests waiting in it never run out
   and it never redraws once a second on the main thread it shares with the desktop's motion.
4. **What `/demo` may reach.** It is public, so it reads nothing from a request: no session
   (`session={null}`), no cookie, no account, no fetch; `demo.test.tsx` fails if the demo's files
   import the Supabase client, the workspace loader or `next/headers`. Only the exact path is
   public (`auth-routes.ts`); `/demo/…` still needs sign-in. Only `/demo` may be framed, and only by
   this origin (`X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, `frame-ancestors 'self'`); every other route still
   sends `DENY`.
5. **Owls with minds of their own.** On the public pages (`(site)/layout.tsx`, `OwnMinds` in
   `owl.tsx`) every owl, even the many logo owls of one seed, blinks on its own rhythm, turns to
   the pointer on its own spring after its own pause of up to 0.4 seconds, and every few seconds
   glances somewhere of its own before finding the pointer again. The temperament comes from the
   seed and React's `useId`, so the server and the browser draw the same one. In the app an
   agent's owl keeps the rhythm its ID gives it (DEC-217). Reduced motion stills them all.
6. **The landing copy explains nothing.** No line on the landing page describes what a screen
   shows, vouches that it is real, or narrates what the visitor is looking at; the thing shows
   itself. The status bar's "Example workspace, paper money" becomes "Paper", the app's own badge
   word, and the demo's unknown-address page keeps its heading and its link only.
7. **The wallpapers are moonlight and snow.** The product is an owl, awake while you sleep, so the
   desktop's pictures are moonlit nights and snow from the Met's public-domain collection (`art.ts`): Hiroshige's
   *Autumn Moon at Ishiyama on Lake Biwa*, *Full Moon at Takanawa*, *Kanasawa under a Full Moon*
   and *Kiso Mountains in Snow*, Friedrich's *Two Men Contemplating the Moon*, Dahl's *Copenhagen
   Harbor by Moonlight* and Homer's *Moonlight, Wood Island Light*. Hokusai's *Great Wave* and
   Van Gogh's *Wheat Field with Cypresses* go. Each visit still opens on one at random (DEC-905
   item 2). "Day and night" shows the Ishiyama print, the palest of them, in the light theme and
   Dahl's harbour in the dark; the tour video's desktop wears the Ishiyama print too. The owl
   scroll stays in the Picture Viewer. `art.test.ts` fails if a picture's file is missing, of
   another size than recorded, or left in `public/art` unused.

## Rationale

Screenshots showed the product; the product itself, inside a browser from 1996 whose buttons
really drive it, is something a visitor plays with, and nothing else looks like it. A frame keeps
the app's fixed layers, shortcuts, styles and routing from leaking into the desktop around it. The
owls moving in lockstep read as one animation copied, not as a page of creatures; small differences
in timing are enough to make each one its own. A wave and a wheat field said nothing about an owl;
moonlight and snow are where one keeps watch.

## Alternatives considered

- **Mounting the app in the page.** Its portals, fixed dock, global shortcuts and media queries
  would act on the whole desktop, not the window.
- **Framing the real `(app)` routes.** They need a session and an account; the example has to read
  neither.
- **A random temperament per load.** It would differ between the server's markup and the browser's
  and break hydration; the seed and `useId` give the same owl both times.
