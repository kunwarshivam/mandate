## Decision

1. **A positive cap below one step of the grid slices at the on-grid minimum** (trading-domain spec
   §9.6, `conduct::slice`). A participation cap is tested for zero **before** it is truncated onto
   the instrument's grid. A cap above zero is truncated onto the grid and floored at the
   instrument's `min_order_size` rounded **up** onto the grid, so the slice is never zero and never
   off the grid. This supersedes **only** [DEC-163](../04-decision-log.md#decisions) item 4's
   clause that a positive cap truncated to zero slices nothing. A cap that is zero before
   truncation still slices nothing, exactly as a missing volume does: no volume, a volume the
   executor could not supply, and a daily cap used up or overdrawn today.
   - The row it changes: an order cap of `0.05 × 19` = 0.95 on a grid of 1 slices every paced
     exit of 10 at 1 share under `OrderSizeParticipation`. On `main` it released the exit of 10
     whole.
   - The daily cap in the same corner: a positive remainder below one step slices at the on-grid
     minimum, and a remainder of zero still slices nothing.
   - Risk exits, protective orders and kill switches stay exempt from every participation cap, and
     no exit is denied or held by one (`AGENTS.md` rule 13).
   - The floor is the larger of `min_order_size` rounded up onto the grid and one step of the grid
     ([#578](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/578) review, round 1, B1): with a
     `min_order_size` of zero, a positive cap below one step would otherwise slice at zero, an order
     of nothing. The floor never raises a slice past the proposal, since a cap binds only where its
     slice is below the proposed quantity, so an exit smaller than one step still goes whole as a
     full close (rule 12).
2. **An off-grid remainder beside a resting sell is truncated onto the grid** (mandate spec §5.5,
   `trim::proposals`). Where the trim rounded up on the grid would sell more than the agent's own
   non-protective resting sells leave unsold ([DEC-399](DEC-399.md) item 7), and that unsold
   remainder is off the grid and is not the whole position, the trim is the largest quantity on
   the grid at or below it. If that is zero, or below the minimum, the trim is withheld as
   [DEC-423](DEC-423.md) item 2 already withholds it. DEC-423 item 2's reading (the remainder is
   not a full close) and its promise (the exit path never emits an order the broker would refuse)
   both stand; this item makes the trim keep that promise.
   - The reference model (`reference/mandate/ref.py`) and the family-B driver do not yet carry this
     truncation, nor the gate's order of operations (the gate subtracts the resting sells before it
     rounds up on the grid; `ref.py` rounds up first). They agree with the gate today only because
     every family-B trim case is on a whole-share grid with whole quantities. The harness PR that
     states each case's own grid must bring both, the spec and the reference first
     ([#578](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/578) review, round 1, minor 2).
   - The row it changes: 2.5 shares held at factor 0, 1 share resting on the agent's own sell, on a
     grid of 1. On `main` the trim was the 1.5 unsold, off the grid and not a full close, an order
     §5.3 rule 2 refuses. It is now 1.
3. **Where it is pinned.** The two rows above move out of their live tests on `main`
   (`gate::tests::a_zero_cap_slices_no_exit_of_any_kind` keeps its three zero-cap rows, and
   `trim::tests::a_resting_partial_trim_proposes_only_the_remainder` keeps its 2-share row) into
   pending tests that stop at the grid stub until DEC-427's implementation PR:
   `gate::tests::a_positive_cap_below_one_step_slices_at_the_minimum` and
   `trim::tests::an_off_grid_remainder_beside_a_resting_sell_is_truncated_onto_the_grid`. Each
   marks its instrument fractionable with a grid of 1, so the stub's own report stops it (DEC-77);
   the implementation reads only the grid. The venue-grid rows of `a_slice_truncates_to_the_venue_s_quantity_grid`
   and `a_trim_rounds_up_on_the_venue_s_quantity_grid`, and the property
   `the_trim_and_the_slice_stay_on_the_venue_s_grid`, pin both items on other grids.

## Rationale

**Item 1.** On `main` a fractionable instrument with an order cap of 0.2 already slices at its
minimum of 1, because `cap.max(minimum)` floors a positive cap; a whole-share instrument with a cap
of 0.95 released the exit of 10 whole, only because truncation reached zero first. Both caps are
positive and below the minimum, and the control gave opposite answers. Testing the untruncated cap
gives one answer on every grid. It paces more and never denies, and `AGENTS.md` rule 13 names
participation caps as the pacing owner and discretionary exits may carry, so it tightens a §9.6
control (DEC-176).

**Item 2.** The clamp's 1.5 is an order §5.3 rule 2 refuses, so on `main` that trim sold nothing.
Rounding up to 2 would sell more than is unsold (rule 12). Reading the clamp as a full close of what
can be sold would widen rule 2's full-close exception past DEC-423 item 2's reading, which is the
founder's (DEC-79), so it is not taken. Truncating sells what the broker accepts.

## The cost, stated plainly

- **Item 1:** an exit in an instrument whose cap is below one step now leaves over several intervals
  instead of at once. In the row above, the owner's exit of 10 leaves at 1 share an interval, over
  ten intervals, where `main` sent all 10 at once.
- **Item 2:** until the resting share fills or is cancelled, half a share stays above target, less
  than one step of the grid. The trim is evaluated again when that sell ends.

## Observation for the founder

Item 1 leaves one discontinuity in place, unchanged here. A cap of exactly zero slices nothing and
releases the exit whole, while a cap a hair above zero slices at the minimum. That edge is
pre-existing, from DEC-163 item 4, which treats a zero cap like a missing volume.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep DEC-163 item 4 on every grid** (option (b) on #571). Refused: it carries the whole-share
  inconsistency onto every venue grid, and on a 0.25 grid a cap of 0.2 releases a tenfold larger
  order than the control allows.
- **Split by grid** (option (c)). Refused: two readings of one control, with no principled
  boundary.
- **For item 2, propose the off-grid remainder anyway.** Refused: the broker refuses it, so it sells
  nothing.
- **Gate the moved rows behind their own conditional stubs.** Refused: the live property
  `an_allowed_exit_is_never_below_its_minimum_or_zero` draws whole-share caps between 0.05 and 0.95,
  so a stub there refuses draws a live test needs, and the only way back is to narrow that test.
