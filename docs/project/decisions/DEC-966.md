## Context

The R0 coordinator's dispatch asked that the live Robinhood opening carry its protection, a stop
and, when the mandate sets one, a take-profit, as the paper path's bracket does: native linked
orders if Robinhood's contract has them, otherwise the stop right after the fill and the
take-profit as a separate order that never sells more than the position.

On `main` the executor's `bracket_of` ignored the profile: an equity opening with a take-profit was
sent as a bracket, which the Robinhood connector refuses before any call (its profile offers none),
so the live opening could not be sent at all, and one with a stop and no take-profit was refused
`Unimplemented`. Nothing placed a stop after a plain entry filled. The runner's executor context
also gave the stop-limit no offset, and the simulator filled a stop order whatever the market did.

## Decision

1. **The contract has no native linked order.** Robinhood's published tool contract
   ([robinhood-contract.md](../tasks/robinhood-contract.md)) places one equity order per
   `place_equity_order` call; `get_advanced_orders` lists OCO groups but no tool places one or a
   bracket (U-R4). Trading spec §5.2's Robinhood profile records it: "one resting stop-limit
   (gtc); no OCO, no bracket". So the sequencing is the separate-order one, and
   `mandate-rh-sim` models what the contract offers: `stop_market` and `stop_limit` orders that
   rest until a quote reaches the stop, then fill in part or whole (a stop-limit never through its
   limit), a broker reject, a cancel, and the refusal of a sell larger than the position less every
   working sell.
2. **The entry goes plain, and the stop follows the fill.** `bracket_of` sends a bracket only when
   the profile's regular-session, whole-share `limit` cell lists the bracket form with `gtc`
   (Alpaca's does, so nothing changes there, LT-14). Otherwise a protected equity opening goes as a
   plain day limit, whatever its prices. Once it is terminal, the executor journals one protective
   order and then sends it (`OrderSubmitted` before the request, `AGENTS.md` rule 5), named
   `{entry}-p{record}` (§2.3), for the filled quantity capped at what the position leaves after
   every live protective order and every exit still selling (rule 12). Terminal means filled
   completely, or ended part filled; a part fill still working at `bracket_partial_fill_timeout_s`
   (or `max_unprotected_s`, if shorter), or outside the regular session, has its remainder
   cancelled first, as a bracket's is (§5.4, DEC-620 item 3). The order is the strongest form
   `protective_shape` finds in the profile: on Robinhood's, one GTC stop-limit at the intent's stop
   and stop × (1 − `stop_limit_offset`) (DEC-539). The interval starts at the first fill, names the
   entry in `ProtectionChanged`'s `bracket` member, and ends when the broker acknowledges the stop
   (DEC-348 item 2). Crypto entries keep slice 5's path, unchanged.
3. **No resting take-profit (a narrowing of the dispatch, the reading that adds no risk).** With no
   OCO, a take-profit sell beside a stop for the same shares would sell them twice if both
   triggered. Robinhood refuses the second sell, as the simulator does (rule 12, §5.3 rule 4). The
   only way to rest both is to split the shares, which leaves part of the position without its
   stop. So the take-profit is never placed as an order; the placement records the stop and a null
   take-profit, and §5.4's runtime-managed take-profit stays as the spec and DEC-529 item 7
   already say. With a single sell resting, "cancel and replace one when the other fills" has
   nothing to do.
4. **The bound flattens.** §5.4 for this profile: "if it is not resting by then, the executor
   exits the held quantity as a `risk_exit`". When an interval naming such an entry reaches
   `max_unprotected_s`, the owner is alerted (`unprotected_interval_limit`, opaque ids only,
   `AGENTS.md` rule 6). If the entry is terminal, the executor hands itself a `risk_exit` intent,
   `w-<record>` from the `interval_limit` record, as the watchdog's (§2.3). The intent sells the
   long position less what exits already sell, its limit the lowest sane mark or bid, else the
   stop, and its agent the entry's. It takes §5.4's marketable sequence, which cancels any stop
   still awaited before it sells and is never refused (rule 13). An `Unknown` order in the
   instrument holds it, which rule 13 allows. If the entry still works, its cancel is asked first;
   when it ends, everything it filled is exited the same way and no stop is placed, since the bound
   has passed for all of it. This keeps an exit from being sized before a late fill.
5. **The runner hands the executor the mandate's offset.** The paper and live context answers
   `crypto_stop_limit_offset` with the mandate's offset (`protection.crypto_stop_limit_offset` as
   `mandate-spec` reads it) for the reviewed agent when protection is on, and refuses the run if it
   does not read as a fraction. Alpaca equities are unaffected: their profile offers an OCO first.
6. **Tests.** The executor's `plain_entry_protection` pins items 2 to 4. Its property runs random
   fill, part-fill, end, cancel, accept, reject and wait sequences against an oracle that sums the
   fills itself. It asserts that the protective and exit sells never exceed the position, and that
   past the bound the position is covered by accepted stops, or flattened whole with the owner
   alerted. The simulator's `stops` pins item 1. The shell harness drives the stop through the
   Robinhood connector to the simulator and back.

## Not settled (backlog)

- **The runtime-managed take-profit** (§5.4, for crypto and this profile) has no implementation.
  Until it lands, a Robinhood position is protected by its stop alone. A mandate's take-profit
  distance is carried in the intent and unused by the executor.
- **An entry whose cancel is never confirmed** keeps its filled shares waiting on that cancel, as
  every exit already waits on an opening's cancel (§5.3 rule 5). The order's own lifecycle bounds
  it (§5.7's lookups make it `Unknown`, which holds exits under rule 13).
- **The transitional Alpaca profile** (DEC-838 item 5) is not deleted here. Every production start
  sets the connector's profile (the test DEC-838 item 5 asked for). Deleting the default touches
  about seventy constructors and stays the backlog's row.
- **A stop rejected after the bound** is §5.4's existing re-placement sequence (DEC-348 item 2),
  unchanged here.

## Alternatives considered

- **A resting take-profit beside the stop, for the same shares:** it oversells if both trigger,
  and Robinhood refuses it anyway. Refused.
- **Split the shares between the stop and the take-profit:** it leaves part of the position without
  a stop. Refused.
- **Flatten at the bound even while the entry still works:** a fill after it would be left with
  only a late stop, and the bound fires once per interval. The property found this; refused.
- **Change the journal schema to name the entry in a new member:** `bracket` already carries "the
  entry a bracket action names", and §5.4 calls this sequence "as for a bracket above". No schema
  change is needed.
