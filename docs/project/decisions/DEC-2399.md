## Context

The executor restarts. Its account risk fold must then be rebuilt from seq 1, and the stream it
reads back already holds the records the fold returned before the restart. DEC-2301 item 2 says a
journaled record the fold would not have produced makes the view unreadable. DEC-2304 item 4 says a
failed append leaves the view unreadable on restart too. Neither says how the fold matches a record
to the input that produced it. Nor do they say how the executor avoids appending a record twice, or
what it does when a crash fell between a cause and its records.

## Invariants first

- The executor appends each record the fold returns exactly once.
- A replay of the journal from seq 1 ends at the view the live fold had.
- Any record on the stream the fold would not have produced, in that place, makes the view
  unreadable: openings wait, and exits, protective orders, risk exits and the kill switch go on
  (DEC-2301 item 3).
- The fold stays pure: it returns records and never appends them (DEC-2304).

## Decision

1. **The fold owes what it returns.** Every record the fold returns for a row joins a queue it
   owes. The next risk records on the stream must be exactly these, in order: the same type, schema
   version, `causation_id` and payload, in order. The risk records are `RiskDayStarted`, `RiskLimitTriggered`,
   `RiskLimitLifted`, `HighWaterMarkReset`, `PositionReleased`, `InstrumentRestrictionChanged`,
   `GoalCompleted` and `AgentModeApplied`. A matching record is consumed and steps nothing. Any
   mismatch is `not_reproduced`.
2. **The records follow their cause's batch.** The executor is the account stream's only writer
   under its epoch. It journals a whole batch (a fill with its `FeesCharged`, its order's state and
   its protection; a tick with its `TradingDayStarted`) before the fold reads it. It then appends
   the records the fold owes for that batch as the next batch, before any other. So rows that are
   not risk records may come between a cause and its records, and they are folded as any row is.
   The risk records themselves must come exactly in the order owed. A risk record that is not the
   front of the queue, or that comes when nothing is owed, is `not_reproduced`, except as item 4
   says. A replay folds the same rows in the same order as the live fold did, so it reaches the
   same state and owes the same records at the same rows.
3. **The tail append.** When the fold has read the stream to its head and still owes records, a
   crash fell after their causes' batch and before, or partway through, their own. By item 2, the
   only rows that can follow those causes on the stream are the rest of their batch and the
   records already appended in order. Anything else would be a batch the executor wrote without
   appending the owed records first, which it never does. The executor then appends exactly the
   records still owed (the fold's `owed()`), and nothing else, in their order. Each is what the
   fold derives from the journal alone, in the place a running executor would have put it, so it
   adds no risk. An unreadable view owes nothing, after a failed append or any other cause, so the
   executor never appends a record that is no longer the journal's. This refines DEC-2304 item 4,
   which left the view unreadable for good after such a crash. A failed append while running is
   still `append_failed`.

   **The executor's contract** (lane RK's R4 meets it, with a test for each clause):
   - after every committed batch (each input, each step of an input's chain, each mark, each tick
     copy, each flushed request), it folds the batch's stored rows and appends `owed()` as the next
     batch, before any other append;
   - after a restart it replays to the head and appends `owed()` before any other write: before
     the startup reconciliation, `Input::Started`, and the first tick;
   - an owed batch whose append is in doubt is offered again first, with the same event ids from
     the same head, and nothing else is appended until it commits;
   - a batch the risk fold cannot read makes its view unreadable, and an unreadable view owes
     nothing, so nothing is appended for it.
4. **Another writer's mode record.** Every record the fold returns names its cause, and the owed
   queue holds those causes in order. An `AgentModeApplied` is the fold's only when it names the
   cause of the record owed next, and then it must be that record. One naming no cause (the
   executor's own for a kill switch, an unmapped broker status or external activity) or another
   cause (the copy of an agent stream's `AgentModeChanged`, journal spec §9.6, whose cause is the
   agent stream's event) is another writer's, whether or not records are owed. It moves nothing
   in the fold, whose mode is the risk state's.
5. **The account stream's tick** (DEC-2307 item 5, built with the wiring). The executor folds each
   scheduler tick on a copy of the fold. It journals the `ClockAdvanced` copy, and then the records
   owed, only when that step returns a record or crosses New York midnight. A tick that returns
   nothing changes no result (mandate spec §5.2, MI-13), so leaving it unjournaled loses nothing a
   replay needs.

## Consequences

- `AccountRiskFold::owed()` names the records owed. The executor appends `owed()` at the head, both
  while running and after a replay, and never the return of a replayed row.
- The tests replay journals the executor writes, batch by batch, and compare against the live view.
  A property test cuts each journal at every point, as a crash would. Its oracle is the records the
  live fold returned, journaled apart from the fold's own queue. A repaired journal, with the owed
  records appended at the head after a cut inside or before the records, replays again from seq 1
  to the live view. A tampered type, version, cause, payload or order, a caused mode record that is
  not the one owed, and a forged record are each `not_reproduced`.
- The account stream's tick (item 5) gets its test with the wiring: a live fold fed every scheduler
  tick against a replay of only the journaled ones.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave the view unreadable after any crash between a cause and its records** (DEC-2304 item 4 as
  written). Refused: it holds every opening for good after a crash the journal itself can repair,
  with no gain in safety. The records appended are the ones the fold derives from the journal.
- **Match records by `causation_id` alone, anywhere later on the stream.** Refused: a record could
  then sit after inputs it should have preceded, and a replay would read a state the live fold never
  had.
