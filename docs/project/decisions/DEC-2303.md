## Context

The mandate-version backtest has to apply splits and dividends (DEC-2245). The account fold
already books them (trading spec §8.5). The risk state, though, keeps its own quantity, cost basis,
realized P&L and mark (mandate spec §5), and neither `ref.py` nor `mandate-spec`'s `risk::Input`
folds a corporate action. If only the account applied a 2:1 split, the risk state would read the
next mark against the old quantity, halve E, and trip the drawdown ladder on nothing. Mandate spec
§5.2 already names the inputs; what it does not say is what folding them does.

## Invariants first

- A split moves E only by the residual's cash in lieu less the residual's value at the adjusted
  mark, and by the adjusted mark's rounding, which never raises E.
- With no residual a split never raises E, so it lifts nothing, clears no breach and restarts no
  wait; it triggers everything a clock tick at the same instant would.
- A cash dividend moves nothing at application. Its receivable, round(Q × d, 2), moves E with the
  first counted sane mark after application, so E never holds the dividend and the cum-dividend
  mark together.
- No action moves E₀, C, N (dollars contributed) or L.
- Posting a receivable moves no figure.

## Decision

1. **A split** folds as §8.5 books it, at application (§8.5's instant): Q', the residual basis R
   removed, and cash in lieu round(f × p, 2) added to realized less R. Cash in lieu is a receivable,
   so it is in E from application. The risk mark becomes mark × old ÷ new **rounded toward zero**
   at 12 places, not half to even as trading spec I3 rounds the account's mark: a rounding up can
   raise E past a limit's boundary and clear the breach its confirmation is counting (MC-R31). The
   account's books keep I3's rounding; only the risk state's mark rounds down, which only tightens.
2. **A cash dividend** is due round(Q × d, 2), Q being the position at application, which is the
   position after every fill traded before the ex-date. Nothing moves at application: the last mark
   is still the cum-dividend close, and counting the dividend against it would overstate E by the
   dividend until the ex-date's mark, long enough to lift a rung or restart a wait (MC-R30). The
   receivable joins realized with the first counted sane mark after application, in that mark's
   step. That only tightens: E is lower in the window, never higher.
3. **`CashInLieuPosted` and `DividendPaid`** move a receivable to cash, so they change nothing the
   risk state holds, whatever amount the broker posts. Cash the risk state never booked, such as
   cash in lieu after a split that posted no price (MC-R32), stays out of E; the difference is the
   reconciliation's.
4. **An action is not a quote.** §5.6's hard trigger reads only quotes, so an action never starts
   or completes a hard-trigger wait.
5. **H follows E**, as after every input (§5.2's evaluation order). Because a dividend joins E
   with the ex-date's mark, in the same step, H never rises by a dividend the mark has not yet
   shed (MC-R29).
6. **Everything else stays out of scope** (§8.5: stock dividends, spin-offs, mergers, symbol
   changes, delistings). It is never folded.
7. **What it adds:** mandate spec v0.8 (§5.2's bullet, MI-33), `ref.py`'s three kinds, MC-R27 to
   MC-R32 with their title assertions, the fuzz's splits (with and without a posted price, and
   with no whole share left), dividends and postings with an independent oracle that carries the
   dividends due itself, a boundary fuzz that lands a split on a rung's, the daily loss's or the
   floor's exact boundary while its confirmation counts, and seven seeded bugs in `mutants.py`. `mandate-spec` follows in a DEC-77
   pair (DEC-2245 item 6, slice 2b).

## Rationale

The risk state is a second ledger of the same position. Folding the action the way the account
does keeps the two in step, which is what §5.2's list of inputs already implies. Defining the change
in E from the action alone gives each limit a precise, testable reason to move: real cash only.

## Alternatives considered

- **Hold E, H and every mark still across an action, and re-base the position silently.** This
  hides real cash: a residual sold for less than it was worth would vanish from every limit.
- **Exclude corporate-action inputs from H.** It would loosen the drawdown by the dividend in the
  window between the receivable and the ex-date's mark. That is a loosening, so it is the founder's.
- **Count the dividend at application and forbid it to lift a rung or reset a wait.** That is a
  special case in every limit's lift and confirmation logic for one input, where holding the
  receivable until the ex-date's mark removes the window itself.
- **Hold every limit's breach state across a split.** It works for a split with no residual, but
  needs its own rule for which limits are held and for how long; rounding the mark down makes a
  split unable to raise E at all, with no new state.
- **Round the split's mark half to even, as I3 does, and weaken MI-33's claim to "within I3's
  bound".** That loosens the rule (a split could clear a breach), so it is not an agent's reading.
- **Leave the risk state unfolded and keep refusing actions in a backtest.** This is today's
  behavior; it leaves E4-4 slice 2 unbuildable.
