# DEC-936: A 250 ms clock-skew tolerance on the paper preflight's IEX quote check

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted (founder) |
| **Date** | 2026-10-16 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | the founder (DEC-79: it loosens a bound DEC-935 kept) |
| **Story or claim** | E2, [first paper trade brief](../tasks/first-paper-trade.md); claim #1362; evidence #1355; amends DEC-935 item 2 and its "Not covered" |

## Decision

1. The paper preflight's IEX quote check (`DataClient::latest_quote`, judged by
   `judge_quote_age` in `mandate-alpaca`) accepts a quote whose own stamp `t` is up to
   **250 ms** (`IEX_QUOTE_CLOCK_SKEW`) ahead of the host clock read at the quote's read. More than
   250 ms ahead, by even a nanosecond, is still refused `AheadOfClock`. A crypto quote keeps no
   tolerance: the founder's approval names the IEX quote only.
2. The 10 s maximum age (`iex_quote_max_age_s`) is unchanged. It is measured from the later of
   the quote's `t` and the host clock, so a quote stamped ahead of the clock is aged zero, never
   negative, and a quote 10 s and a nanosecond older than the clock is `Stale`.
3. The observed skew is recorded on the quote the check returns, as `LatestQuote::clock_skew`:
   how far `t` was ahead of the host clock at the read, zero when it was not ahead. A quote parsed
   without a clock read carries `None`.
4. Nothing else changes. The asset record's age check (`AssetSnapshot::current`), the shell
   judge's quote check at the run clock and the binding gate's recheck immediately before
   submission (both DEC-935 item 2), and the risk gate's freshness rule (DEC-163 item 1) still
   refuse a quote stamped after their own clock. A quote within the tolerance that is still ahead
   of the run clock refuses at "Validate: a current, uncrossed IEX quote"; one the clock has
   passed by then is judged as any other.

## Rationale

#1355's E2 runs refused "Reconcile: a current IEX quote" with `AheadOfClock`: the IEX quote's
`t` was 2 to 24 ms ahead of the host clock, and the host could not reach NTP to correct it. A
stamp a few milliseconds ahead says the two clocks disagree, not that the quote is old; 250 ms is
an order of magnitude above the skew observed and far below the 10 s age bound, so a quote
accepted under it is at most 10.25 s old by the exchange's clock. Measuring the age from the later
instant keeps the bound from ever reading a future stamp as extra freshness. Recording the skew
makes a drifting host visible in the facts before it reaches the tolerance.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep refusing (DEC-935).** Every E2 run on a host whose clock trails the data host's refuses,
  and fixing the host clock is not in the run's reach where NTP is blocked.
- **Widen every quote check, including the shell judge and the binding gate.** Not approved: the
  founder approved the preflight check only.
- **Shift the host clock by the observed skew.** Rejected: one clock adjusted by a fact it judges
  would let the quote set its own age.
