## Context

The founder asked for a demoable v1 by about 2026-10-26: a user signs in, connects Alpaca paper,
confirms a mandate, deploys an agent, answers approval asks from a phone, and sees the dashboard
and audit trail. Four founder-reserved choices stood in the way, and the lane leads collected them
into one ask.

## Decision

1. **Domain.** The demo runs on `owlhead.ai`, a domain the founder already owns:
   - the web app on `app.owlhead.ai`;
   - the workspace API on `api.owlhead.ai`;
   - the notification sender on `notify.owlhead.ai`.

   Agents never touch DNS or registrar settings; the founder sets each record when asked. The
   domain is the base of three things:
   - the passkey relying-party ID (identity spec);
   - the Alpaca OAuth redirect URI (DEC-441 item 18);
   - the sending domain (DEC-438 item 23), with SPF, DKIM and a DMARC `p=reject` policy.
2. **Sign-in: DEC-437 item 15 is accepted as recommended.** Supabase Auth is the managed OIDC
   issuer, verified with asymmetric signing algorithms only. Workspace services are the WebAuthn
   relying party for step-up and for the risk-reduction path, so no hosted service sits in a core
   path. The founder creates the Supabase project with asymmetric JWT signing keys and supplies
   its issuer URL, which is public. The free tier is used, with no spending. A paid plan would be a
   new decision.
3. **Email sender: DEC-438 item 21 is accepted as recommended for the demo.** The sender is the
   founder's own SMTP submission account, for paper only, as item 21 says. Its credential lives
   only in the vault: never in the repository, a log, an environment file, or an agent's context.
   A transactional vendor comes before any customer mail and is a new decision.
4. **Footer: DEC-438 item 24, for owner-only mail.** The real SMTP transport may send only to one
   address: the founder's own, held in the deployment's configuration as a single allowlisted
   recipient. Every message has exactly that one recipient, in `To`, with no `Cc` and no `Bcc`. Any
   other recipient, or more than one, is refused before anything is sent. For that mail,
   `[[EMAIL-FOOTER]]` resolves to exactly:
   `Sent by your Mandate workspace to its owner.`
   All other mail stays refused until counsel supplies the general footer and unsubscribe wording.
   The notifications follow-up states the rule as "no transport sends outside the allowlist". That
   replaces the planned guard in the backlog, under which only the fixture transport may be built
   while the footer is unresolved. The notifications lane pins each refusal with a test: a second
   recipient, a `Cc`, a `Bcc`, and a non-allowlisted address.
5. **Non-negotiable 6 is unchanged.** Every notice still carries only opaque IDs and generic text.

## Rationale

Each choice is the recommended, no-spend option. Together they let the demo run end to end on the
founder's own accounts, with no customer exposed to an unreviewed legal text.

## Alternatives considered

The coordinator offered a platform subdomain, passkeys only, a transactional vendor now, web push
only, and keeping email blocked. The founder chose the options above.
