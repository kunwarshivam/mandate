# DEC-502: DESIGN.md holds principles; the rendering and the history move to web/design/

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-07 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | the founder, in conversation with the agent, on the agent's proposal |
| **Story or claim** | a UI review on fixture data (`web/design/review-2026-10-07.md`); no story yet |

## Decision

1. `web/DESIGN.md` holds only what must hold on every screen: the five rules (Control, No-Nudge,
   Meaning, no gamification, records are pages), the tokens with their values in both themes, the
   type scale, the layout tokens, one line per component, the motion rules, and the do's and
   don'ts. It stays under about 200 lines.
2. The description of how each surface, component and page is drawn today moves verbatim to
   `web/design/reference.md`. It is a record and a reference, not a rule: a sentence there may
   change without a decision, and where it states a safety behaviour, the rule lives in DESIGN.md.
3. The dated record of founder feedback moves to `web/design/history.md`. New entries are one line:
   the date, what was asked, and the DEC. Detail belongs in the decision file.
4. A rule enters DESIGN.md only when the product relies on it: safety, compliance, or a founder
   decision recorded as a DEC. Founder feedback on the look does not by itself add a rule or a test.
5. A test pins what DESIGN.md states and nothing more. It checks the invariant ("the owner's request
   comes before the chart on a phone", "Approve and Skip have the same class"), not the current
   rendering (a height at four viewports, an exact sentence of copy, a byte-exact asset). Tests that
   exist only to notice that the look changed are to be loosened to the invariant they protect or
   removed, in a change of their own, with the safety and compliance tests (Stop present, reduced
   motion, no gradients, contrast pairs, no example amounts in set-up, the disclosure, notification
   payloads, role gating) kept exactly as they are.
6. Reviews of the UI from screenshots are written to `web/design/review-<date>.md` as numbered
   findings. A finding becomes a story in the backlog; a review file is never a spec.

## Rationale

DESIGN.md had grown to 409 lines, about a third of it a dated changelog and most of the rest
paragraphs of pixel measurements per component, each paired with a test. The web app has 11,600
lines of tests for 27,000 of code, and a large share of them hold the current look in place rather
than protect a behaviour. While the look is still moving, every design change paid for the test
that pinned the last one, and every agent read 400 lines of rendering detail to find the twenty
lines of rules. The founder agreed ("the rules file is outrunning the product").

Splitting the file keeps everything written (nothing is deleted; the reference is the old body
verbatim) and makes the binding part short enough to read before every change. Rule 5 is the
process change: it stops the growth at the source, where feedback became a measurement became a
test. The sort of existing tests is deliberately a separate change, because an over-eager pass
there is how a safety test goes missing.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave the file and add a summary at the top.** Agents would still read and copy the detail,
  and the test rule would not change.
- **Delete the component detail.** It is the only written record of several founder decisions'
  consequences and of why measurements are what they are; moving it loses nothing.
- **Sort the tests in the same change.** Rejected for the reason in the rationale.
