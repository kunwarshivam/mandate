## Decision

[Journal spec](../../specs/journal.md) v0.20 adds §9.8, the connection records the
[connections spec](../../specs/connections.md) CN-10 asks for.

1. **`ConnectionEstablished` version 2**: version 1's four members, then `account_ref` (`ulid`),
   `user`, `step_up` (never `null`), and `margin_attestation`. Version 1 stays registered and
   closed. A writer that has an `account_ref` writes version 2. No version-1 connection exists
   outside the paper fixtures (CN-12): E7-17's writer is the first, and it writes version 2.
2. **The binding** (closes DEC-261 item 10). An account stream `acct:{workspace_id}:{account_ref}`
   belongs to the connection whose version-2 establishment names that `account_ref`. One
   `account_ref` belongs to one `connection_id` (CN-5). §9.2's mapping still takes the account
   stream's connection as an argument; its owner now reads it from the binding.
3. **The connecting user, step-up, and attestation** that DEC-261 item 8 assigned to E7-1 ride in
   the same version:
   - A second version within one story would only add an upcaster.
   - Identity spec ID-4 requires step-up to connect, so `step_up` can never be `null`.
   - `margin_attestation` (`cash_account` or `margin_disabled`) is the owner's no-margin
     attestation, confirmed with that step-up. DEC-529 item 11 asks for it on the founder's live
     account, where the contract shows no margin field. Rule 64 requires it exactly for a `live`
     connection, so the deployment that accepts `live` reads it from the journal, never from a
     flag.
4. **Who writes what.**
   - The control services write `ConnectionRefused` and `ConnectionCredentialRotated` on the
     control stream.
   - The executor writes `ConnectionChecked`, `ConnectionStateChanged`, and
     `ConnectionCredentialRefreshed` on the account stream, each with `risk_clock` (DEC-447) and
     the `connection_id` they act for.
   - The executor also copies the version-2 establishment and each rotation onto its account
     stream, as §2 copies owner input, with the original as `causation_id` (rule 63).

4a. **The connect sequence** (#769's blocker 1, agreed with the L2 S4 builder).
   1. The connection manager assigns `connection_id` and `account_ref` when the connect starts.
   2. The connecting executor opens the account stream and journals `ConnectionChecked` (occasion
      `connect`) there.
   3. The control services run check 4, then append `ConnectionEstablished` version 2 with that
      check as its `causation_id` (rule 63). Before appending, they confirm the check:
      - it is on the bound stream, for the same connection;
      - it passed every check it records (1, 2, 3, and 7);
      - it lists `contract` for an MCP connection.
   4. The executor copies the establishment. The stream is `connecting` until that copy (rule 68).

   The broker account id goes to the personal-data vault, and the record carries only its
   reference (`pii_` and a ULID). From that reference the control services have the vault compute
   the fingerprint, for check 3's comparison and check 4.

   A control-stream `causation_id` may name the account-stream check.

   **A refused connect** is `ConnectionRefused`:
   - its cause is the failed check when the executor's check failed;
   - it has no cause when the control services refused, or when the teardown of connections spec
     §5.2 step 6 ended it with no check failed: `check` `null` and reason `timeout`,
     `restart_past_deadline`, `executor_stopped`, or `start_failed` (the executor failed to start
     after the vault write), the words agreed with the L2 S4 builder for #769 (rules 54 and 65).

   It leaves an orphan account stream, kept under §6.2's retention and never written again. Its
   executor has exited and its writer epoch is never reused.

   A reconnect (`reconnect`) and a credential replacement (`reauthorize`,
   `ConnectionCredentialRotated`) run the same sequence.
5. **A suspended connection recovers by re-authorization, not by reconnect** (Accepted: the
   founder's, under DEC-79, accepted on 2026-10-08 in [DEC-824](DEC-824.md) item 6).
   Connections spec §9.1 says a `suspended` connection ends when "the owner reconnects the same
   account". That reading would trap positions:
   - §3's reconnect needs a `ConnectionRevoked` first;
   - the workspace API refuses a revocation while an agent holds positions (§4.5);
   - so a suspended account with positions would have no way back but the compromise path.

   The reading taken: replacing the credential of a connection that is not revoked is
   `ConnectionCredentialRotated`, and a reconnect (second `ConnectionEstablished`) is only for a
   revoked connection. The replacement is held to what a reconnect is held to, and the review of
   #786 tightened it so that a stale or refused check can never lift a suspension. On the account
   stream, in order (rule 68):
   - the executor journals a `ConnectionChecked` with occasion `reauthorize`, after the
     `ConnectionStateChanged` that entered `suspended`, in which checks 1, 2, 3, and 7 passed;
   - the control services confirm it, compare the fingerprint (the same account), and append
     `ConnectionCredentialRotated` naming it. The rotation keeps the connection's `connection_id`,
     and with it its `broker`, `environment`, and `account_ref`, and its scopes stay the same or
     narrow (rule 67);
   - the executor copies the rotation;
   - a `condition_cleared` names that copy as its `causation_id`;
   - the owner's acknowledgment follows.

   A check the control services refused is named by no rotation, so it never clears. Neither does
   a check or a rotation from before the suspension.

   Re-authorization is the only way out of `suspended`. A revoke and reconnect (the connections
   spec's literal reading) does not by itself leave it: the reconnect continues the account stream
   and its state, and produces a `ConnectionEstablished` copy and a `reconnect` check, never a
   rotation, so nothing it journals can be the cause rule 68 asks of a `condition_cleared`. After
   reconnecting, the owner still re-authorizes as above. This replaces the earlier wording of this
   item, which called revoke and reconnect "a second way out"; the review of #786 found rule 68
   never allowed it. It is a DEC-176 tightening: the reading that only narrows the ways out of a
   halt and adds no risk, and it is the one the founder accepted in DEC-824 item 6 ("a suspended
   connection recovers by re-authorization").
6. **The state machine on the account stream.**
   - The states are `active`, `degraded`, and `suspended`, with the reasons of connections spec
     §8.2 and §9.1 (and §9.2's vault outage as `lease_expired`).
   - `condition_cleared` records that the cause cleared while the state stays.
   - `acknowledged` returns to `active`, names the copied `OwnerAcknowledged` as its cause (rule
     61), and only follows a `condition_cleared` (rule 68).
   - A reconnect continues the account stream and its state, and one account stream names one
     `connection_id` for life (rule 68, CN-5).
7. **The halt is never behind these records.** Into `degraded` or `suspended`, the executor
   commits `AccountRestrictionChanged` and `AgentModeApplied` first (CN-6, trading spec §7.3), and
   the check and state records in a later batch. Out of it, the `acknowledged` record is committed
   no later than the batch that lifts the restriction. A refused append of a §9.8 record therefore
   keeps openings halted (`AGENTS.md` rule 3).
8. **Checks 5 and 6 are not in `ConnectionChecked`.** `AccountStateObserved` records the
   multiplier and the account status they read; neither refuses a connection or changes its
   state. `ConnectionChecked`'s `account_pii_ref` (rule 62) is how the control services reach the
   fingerprint without the executor writing the connection record (#769's review, M2). An
   executor never reports `account_mismatch`: the fingerprint comparison is the control services',
   refused on the control stream (rule 58).
9. **Where the stream rules are checked.**
   - **Stream rules 66 to 68 are the owner's and §11's, not `append`'s.** `append` folds no
     stream. The connection manager and the executor check them against their own fold before
     appending, and the new per-range check `connection_lifecycle_mismatch` verifies every range.
   - **The causes that cross streams** are verified by the full-chain run's
     `connection_cause_mismatch`:
     - a version-2 establishment's or a rotation's check is on the bound stream, for the same
       connection, with the right occasion, passing, and with `contract` for an MCP connection;
     - each copy carries its original's payload.
   - **Robinhood is the only MCP broker today.** An absent `contract` result passes only for a
     connection that is not MCP.
   - A version-1 first establishment has no `account_ref`, so it is never re-established.
   - No rule refuses a `ConnectionRevoked`, so the compromised revocation of workspace API §5.6,
     whose kill switch shares its batch, is never held by one.
10. **What the journal cannot see.** The fingerprint is never journaled (connections spec §3.1), so
    that a new connect to an account whose connection was revoked must be a reconnect of that
    connection (CN-12) is the connection manager's to enforce (E7-11, E7-14). Rule 66 makes the
    binding unable to drift once it does.
11. **Vectors.** A generated, additive `connections` section of `journal.yaml`, built by
    `reference/journal/connections.py`: 8 base drafts, 159 invalid and 16 valid drafts, 39 stream
    `sequences`, and 9 cross-stream `chains`. 50 validator, 21 stream, 7 chain, and 9 vector
    mutants are each caught. The vectors stay version 3.
12. **Version and numbers.** The coordinator moved this change up the journal queue to right after
    J3 (#736), which adds no section. It is v0.20 and §9.8, with rules 54 to 68, the next after
    `main`'s v0.19 (#808, which adds rule 21b and no §9.x section), §9.7, and rule 53. The changes
    behind it (α's E10-15 three, L3) renumber after it. It adds nothing to `ConnectionRevoked`; the stream rules count a revocation of either
    version. #792's `ConnectionRevoked` version 2 lands after this change, at a later spec version,
    and changes none of these rules.
13. **Vault credential events** for the minimal local vault (the L2 lane's V1) are a separate,
    following spec change, so that this one stays reviewable.
14. **The OAuth token's breadth event is a separate, following journal spec change.**
    [DEC-821](DEC-821.md) item 4 (with DEC-441 item 22, and connections spec §5.2 steps 1 and 5
    and §12 item 22) requires an Alpaca OAuth token's possible breadth to be journaled with the
    connection and disclosed to the owner, with "the journal spec change that adds that event"
    first. §9.8 does not add it. That change (backlog E7-27) must land before any Alpaca OAuth connection completes
    (E7-1, E10-13); until it does, no Alpaca OAuth connect appends `ConnectionEstablished`.

## Rationale

CN-10 requires every connection change journaled before it takes effect, and E7-12 and E7-14 wait
on these schemas. Every member traces to:
- the connections spec: §3's fields, §8.1's checks, §8.2's signals, §9.1's states;
- the identity spec: ID-4's step-up;
- DEC-529: the attestation.

Every rule only refuses drafts, and the ordering in item 7 keeps a refusal from ever lifting a
halt. The review of #786 found two ways a stale or refused check could clear a suspension, and a
teardown with no shape; item 5's tightening and item 4a's teardown reasons close them.

## Alternatives considered

- **`account_ref` alone in version 2**, the user and step-up in a version 3 with E7-1: two
  upcasters for one story's records, and a version-2 record E7-1 would supersede at once.
- **The attestation as its own event**: a second record to keep consistent with the
  establishment, for one member the establishment's step-up already confirms.
- **Suspended ends by revoke and reconnect**, as §9.1 reads literally: traps positions, since a
  revocation is refused while an agent holds them. Nor is it a way out at all: a reconnect
  continues the stream's `suspended` state, and rule 68 clears it only on a rotation (item 5).
- **Any passing check clears a suspension** (#786 as first pushed): a stale check, a daily one, or
  the executor's half of a reauthorize the control services refused would lift it.
- **Connection state on the control stream**: the executor detects health and owns the account
  stream; a control-stream copy would add a cross-stream writer for a fact only the executor has.
- **Stream rules enforced at `append`**: the journal would have to fold control-stream records it
  otherwise treats as opaque; §11 already verifies cross-record rules (`mode_event_mismatch`).
