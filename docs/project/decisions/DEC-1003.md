# DEC-1003: A backtest or evaluation report carries its disclosures as named placeholders only, until counsel writes them

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Proposed (founder-reserved, DEC-79: legal and compliance text). The conservative reading below is in force until the founder decides |
| **Date** | 2026-10-17 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | Proposed by the lane I4 builder (Claude Code); the founder decides |
| **Story or claim** | E4-5, E17-8; product brief B2; compliance questions 10 and 35; claim [#1463](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/issues/1463) |

## Context

The product brief's B2 shows a backtest report "under the hypothetical-performance legend", names
the placeholder `[[LEGEND-HYPOTHETICAL]]`, and says that for a research agent the report states
that a backtest tests mechanics, not thesis quality (DEC-99). The brief's rule 9 row keeps all
compliance text as named placeholders. Compliance questions 10 and 35 ask how the Marketing Rule
treats backtests and scorecards. None of this wording exists, and an agent may not write it.

## Decision (proposed)

1. **Named placeholders.** A report's `disclosures` is a list of names, never text:
   - `LEGEND-HYPOTHETICAL` on every backtest report (the brief's existing name);
   - `NOTE-BACKTEST-MECHANICS` on a research-agent version's backtest: the statement that it tests
     sizing, the gate and accounting, not thesis quality (DEC-99);
   - `NOTE-BACKTEST-LIMITS` on every backtest report: what the run cannot know, rendered from its
     `limitations` codes;
   - `NOTE-FORWARD-PAPER-INTERNAL` on every evaluation report: that it scores the team's own
     internal paper theses (DEC-103) and is not an owner's result.
2. **Neutral by construction.** A report holds figures, codes and these names. It holds no
   adjective, no projection, no annualized "expected" figure, and no statement about the
   platform's performance. The report is the owner's own strategy's history, labelled as
   simulated.
3. **Until decided:** the web client shows each name as a visible placeholder (the brief's form),
   and nothing renders a report to anyone outside the workspace that ran it.

## What the founder decides

Whether these four names are the right set, and, with counsel, the text behind each. Adding,
renaming or wording one is a new decision.

## Rationale

Names keep the record stable while the text is unwritten, and they let counsel's text arrive
without a schema change. Placeholders that render visibly cannot be mistaken for finished copy.

## Alternatives considered

- **Draft provisional wording.** Rejected: legal text is founder-reserved (DEC-79).
- **Ship no disclosure member.** Rejected: the brief requires the legend on every report, and a
  member added later would change the schema.
