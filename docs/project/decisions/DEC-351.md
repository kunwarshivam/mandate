# DEC-351: A fired tripwire is a latched limit that only the owner's acknowledgment lifts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-02 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | The stream H builder (agent), under DEC-79 and DEC-176: each item only tightens |
| **Story or claim** | E6-13, claim [#439](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/issues/439); mandate spec §5.2, §5.8 to §5.10, §6.5, §6.7, MI-31 |

## Decision

1. **Arming.** A tripwire counts over the inputs after the one that armed it: the
   `MandateVersionApplied` that began its unbroken run under its `id` with the same metric, or the
   acknowledgment that last lifted it. Changing only the threshold or action keeps the count.
2. **Firing.** The executor evaluates tripwires in `id` order after the lifetime floor at every input,
   version inputs included, so adding or tightening one applies at once. A firing journals
   `RiskLimitTriggered` (limit `tripwire:<id>`, reason `tripwire_condition`, metric, threshold, value)
   and an `OwnerAlertSent` carrying only that event's opaque id and generic text; no new event type.
3. **While fired** it is a latched limit: every delegation is suspended (§6.5 condition 5, MI-28) and
   allocation increases are rejected (MI-7). It holds the stricter of the action it fired with and
   its action in the version in effect; holding `exits_only` adds restriction `tripwire` (mode
   `exits_only`).
4. **Lifting.** Only an `OwnerAcknowledged` naming it, with step-up valid when the executor processes
   it (§6.1), lifts it (`RiskLimitLifted`, reason `owner_acknowledged`) and re-arms it with nothing
   counted. No version, risk day, restart, or time lifts it, and a version that removes or loosens it
   leaves it holding what it held.
5. **Independence** (the round 1 ruling on #443, the stricter reading): with
   `independent_approval_required`, the acknowledging user must not be the user who requested the
   lift, as for the drawdown ladder's acknowledgment (§5.8). The acknowledgment names both; one from
   the requester is refused with `not_independent` and the tripwire stays fired.
6. **Independence fails closed, at the stricter moment** (the round 2 ruling on #443, DEC-176, rule 3):
   the requirement is the stricter of the policy when the lift was requested and the overlay when the
   executor processes it, as §4.3 and §6.4 check 7 read it; under it an acknowledgment that omits the
   requester or the acknowledging user is refused `not_independent`. `not_independent` joins §6.1's
   refusal reasons for an acknowledgment only, and journal spec rule 28 keeps it to the account
   stream. With the requirement on and one user, a fired tripwire cannot be lifted until a second user
   exists; whether such a mandate should be refused at validation is a founder question in the backlog.

## Rationale

Reusing the latched-limit path means the delegation suspension, the gate's `exits_only`, approval
cancellation on entering `exits_only`, and the journal events already exist and are already tested.
Re-arming at the acknowledgment keeps the owner from being asked again at once for what they just saw.

## Alternatives considered

- Let a version that removes a fired tripwire lift it: a loosening version would then clear a latch,
  which MI-3 forbids for every other limit.
- Re-arm on every tightening: a lower threshold could then fire later than the old one.
- Step-up alone even under `independent_approval_required` (DEC-187's words): it would make a
  tripwire the one latched limit a single credential can lift where policy asks for two people.
