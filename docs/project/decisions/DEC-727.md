# DEC-727: A relayed send's VAPID `sub` is a role or platform contact address, never a person's

## Decision

1. **A relayed send never signs as a person.** The VAPID `sub` claim of a push a deployment sends
   through the relay names a role or platform contact address, never a person's address. DEC-790
   item 4 still makes `sub` one contact URI per deployment, from its configuration; this narrows
   which URI a deployment that sends through the relay may configure. A direct send is unchanged.
2. **The form.** A relayed send's subject must be exactly `mailto:<role>@<domain>`, where `<role>`
   is one of `push`, `notifications`, `postmaster`, `abuse`, or `security`, compared exactly
   (lowercase, no `+` tag, no other local part), and `<domain>` is a non-empty run of lowercase
   ASCII letters, digits, dots, and hyphens with nothing after it. Anything else is refused as
   `invalid_subject`, an `https:` subject included. The founder's value,
   `mailto:push@notify.owlhead.ai` (DEC-790 item 4), passes. `noreply` is not a role here: `sub` is
   the contact a push service writes to (RFC 8292 §2.1). A longer list is a new decision.
3. **Where it is checked.** The deployment checks it, never the relay: DEC-726 item 2 has the relay
   read no claim. `mandate_webpush::VapidSubject::check_relayed` holds the rule; the deployment's
   relayed sender calls it on its configured subject before it builds any relayed header, and
   refuses to send through the relay with a subject that fails it. The rule is pinned by
   `a_relayed_subject_is_a_role_mailbox_never_a_person` in `crates/mandate-webpush/src/tests.rs`.

## Rationale

DEC-726 item 8 states that the relay could read the header's claims, which are encoded, not
encrypted, and so could read `sub`. NT-2 keeps a person's address away from every party that does
not need it, and DEC-724 kept the endpoint, the one address the relay must see, out of its log.
A role mailbox gives the push service its contact without handing the relay, or the push service,
a member's address. Every reading refuses more: a deployment with a person's address configured
loses relayed push until it sets a role address, which is only a missed interruption (NT-5) and
touches no trade path (NT-9).

## Alternatives considered

- **Any contact URI, as DEC-790 item 4 allows for a direct send.** Rejected by the coordinator: a
  deployment could put a member's address in every relayed header.
- **Accept `https:` subjects.** Rejected for relayed sends: a URL can carry a person's name or
  handle in its path as easily as a mailbox can, and one closed form is enough for the founder's
  value.
- **A refusal at the relay.** Rejected: the relay would have to decode the claims, which DEC-726
  item 2 forbids.
- **A role list taken from configuration.** Rejected: the deployment would choose what counts as
  a role, which is the judgement this rule removes.
