# DEC-498: ninety-six mutation shards, one mutant at a time, and a per-mutant timeout the shard budget is derived from

| **Story or claim** | [#652](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/652) review rounds 3 to 6; supersedes DEC-473's shard count; DEC-464's ten-minute budget, DEC-497; measured by [#657](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/657) |

## Decision

1. Supersede DEC-473's twenty-four mutation shards with **ninety-six** deterministic, zero-based
   slice shards.
2. **Run one mutant at a time** (`MUTANT_JOBS` in `xtask/src/main.rs`, `cargo mutants --jobs 1`),
   not two. A shard's critical path is then its mutants in a row, with no waves to bound.
3. **Set cargo-mutants' per-mutant test timeout explicitly, to 180 seconds**
   (`MUTANT_TEST_TIMEOUT`), instead of taking the value cargo-mutants derives.
4. **Price a mutant's test phase at that timeout, not at how fast mutants were observed to run.**
   A shard spends its time on setup through the unmutated baseline, then a build and a test phase
   per mutant. Only the mutant's test phase is a term a mutation can stretch, and
   `MUTANT_TEST_TIMEOUT` is the cap that stops it; the other two are bounded by measurement with
   headroom, which is sound because no mutation can reach them. A shard of `k` mutants is
   budgeted at `k × (build + timeout) + setup`. Items 1, 3 and the shard count are chosen
   together so the sum fits the ten-minute limit; none may be changed without redoing it.
5. Keep everything DEC-473 kept: the ten-minute job limit, the complete source diff, the selected
   packages, the live-test preflight, the unmutated baseline, the test command, and the
   zero-survivor rule. `cargo xtask check` stays unsharded as the complete local oracle.
6. **When a shard runs out of time, expand the matrix; never the job's ten-minute limit, the diff,
   or the tests.**
7. **A shard budget is measured on `ubuntu-24.04`, over repeated runs, and bounded by what the
   configuration permits rather than by what a sample did.** Every number here comes from such
   runs, because the three drafts before it did none of that and were wrong three different ways:
   the first scaled from a four-core developer box and was out by a factor of two and a half; the
   second divided a nine-mutant total by nine and priced a shard at its average mutant instead of
   its critical path; the third priced a mutation-sensitive phase at what the harness does when
   nothing goes wrong.

## Rationale

DEC-497 runs the whole reference harness for any mutation of a crate it is built on, so a mutant
of a core crate costs its own package's tests plus the whole of `mandate-refcases`. #652's review
asked for the arithmetic, then for it to be measured, then for it to be measured properly, then
for the part a mutation controls to be bounded rather than extrapolated. This is the fourth
answer and the first that does not depend on mutants behaving.

### What the runner measured

[#657](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/657) is a throwaway branch built to produce
these numbers: three untested `pub fn`s in `mandate-canon`, nine mutants, none caught, all in a
single shard. An uncaught mutant runs the whole harness to completion, so it measures the
harness's natural pace. The same nine ran four times, twice at one worker and twice at two, with
the timeout lifted so nothing was cut off. Ranges cover all nine mutants in each run; the ninth
is the tail, which runs alone once the others are done, and at two workers it is visibly cheaper
for it.

| Workers | Run | Test phase | Build | Wall clock per mutant | Nine mutants |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a | 77–83s | 16–27s | 99s | 14m49s |
| 1 | b | 63–66s | 16–25s | 82s | 12m17s |
| 2 | a | 61–139s | 21–55s | 83s | 12m29s |
| 2 | b | 90–181s | 24–71s | 108s | 16m15s |

**Why one worker (item 2).** These samples show no throughput difference between the two
settings: 82 and 99 seconds a mutant at one worker, 83 and 108 at two, where the gap between the
two settings is smaller than the gap between repeated runs of either. Two runs each cannot
support a claim about throughput in either direction, and none is needed. What the samples do
show, well outside that spread, is that two workers contend: the per-mutant test phase runs to
181 seconds at two workers and to 83 at one. One worker is chosen for that — it keeps a single
mutant short and predictable, and it makes a shard's duration the sum of its mutants rather than
a sequence of waves.

**Why 180 seconds (item 3).** The timeout has to sit above every legitimate harness run, or it
turns a judged mutant into a `TIMEOUT` and fails a green PR. The slowest legitimate test phase
measured at one worker is 83 seconds, and repeated runs of the same configuration differ by about
a quarter, so 180 is 2.2 times the slowest observation and roughly nine times the observed
run-to-run spread above it. It is not a claim that only hangs reach the cap: a mutation that
makes the code slow without hanging can reach it too, and item 4 is what makes that affordable
rather than a surprise.

### The budget, and which of its terms a mutation can stretch

A shard spends its time on three things: setup through the unmutated baseline, then, for each
mutant, a build and a test phase. **Exactly one of the three is something a mutation can make
slow**, and that distinction is what the budget turns on. The point is not that every term has a
cap — one does and two do not. It is that the term no measurement can safely bound is the one
that has a cap, and the terms bounded by measurement are the ones a mutation cannot reach.

**A mutant's test phase can be stretched, so it is priced at its cap.** That is the term the
earlier drafts got wrong: a mutant can loop longer, retry more, or be caught only after the suite
has ground through it, and nothing about the measured pace bounds that. `--timeout` does, because
cargo-mutants kills the test run at `MUTANT_TEST_TIMEOUT` and records a `TIMEOUT`. So **180**
seconds, as a bound rather than an extrapolation.

**A mutant's build cannot be stretched, so measurement bounds it.** cargo-mutants applies the
explicit `--timeout` to test commands; its build timeout is a separate `--build-timeout` option
which is off by default and which this gate does not set, so no cap of ours covers compilation.
None is needed, because of what a mutant is. Every genre cargo-mutants generates edits an
expression inside a function: a body replaced by a value of its return type, a binary or unary
operator swapped, a match arm or a struct-literal field deleted, a match guard forced to a
constant. None of them touches a signature, a generic parameter, a trait bound, or any item at
all, so none gives the compiler more to infer, resolve, or monomorphise — each substitutes or
removes code within one body, and the body-replacement genre, the common case here, deletes the
whole of it. The one way a mutant could hang a build is long-running `const` evaluation, and
rustc interrupts that itself under the default `long_running_const_eval` lint, which this
workspace leaves on: nothing passes `--cap-lints`, and `.cargo/mutants.toml` holds only an empty
`exclude_re`. What remains is ordinary variation in how fast the runner compiles. Mutant builds
at one worker took 16 to 27 seconds, so **60** is 2.2 times the slowest of those and above the
slowest build measured at any worker count (71 seconds, at two).

**Setup and the baseline cannot be stretched either, for a simpler reason: the baseline is the
unmutated tree.** It builds and tests the source as committed, before any mutant is applied, so
no mutation can affect either of its phases whatever the run goes on to find — and the same holds
for checkout, toolchain, and the live-test preflight ahead of it. This term is runner variation
too, measured at 50 to 56 seconds across the four runs; take **60**.

```
k × (60 + 180) + 60 ≤ 600   →   k ≤ 2
```

`--sharding slice` cuts the list into contiguous slices whose sizes differ by at most one, so `S`
shards hold `2 × S` mutants. The largest safety-critical diff so far is E6-13's 169, which
DEC-473 measured, so `S ≥ 85`; **ninety-six** shards is the next round number above it and holds
**192**. The worst case is then `2 × 240 + 60 = 540` seconds against the ten-minute limit.

So this is not a closed arithmetic guarantee, and it is not claimed as one. Two of its three
terms rest on measurement with better than twofold headroom; what makes that sound is that
neither is a term a mutation can reach, so a pathological mutant cannot walk them past their
allowance. The three earlier drafts all priced the one term a mutant *can* reach at something a
sample happened to do. This one prices that term at what the configuration permits and spends the
headroom on the two it cannot.

In normal operation it is far cheaper than its bound: at the measured pace two mutants and a
baseline come to `2 × 107 + 60 = 274` seconds, and a shard whose mutants are *caught* is cheaper
still, because a caught mutant stops at nextest's first failure. #652's own shards, one caught
mutant each, finished in 30 to 58 seconds including setup.

Ninety-six jobs is twice the previous matrix and four times DEC-473's. On a public repository
GitHub's standard runners cost nothing, so the price is concurrency and not money, and the jobs
are short: almost all of them have no mutant to test and exit after setup.

### What the measurement found on the way

The first #657 run reported nine `TIMEOUT`s, every one at a twenty-second cap, and no verdicts at
all. cargo-mutants sets its per-mutant test timeout to five times the unmutated baseline's test
phase, floored at twenty seconds, and it runs that baseline over **the mutated package alone**:
the `--test-package=mandate-refcases` that DEC-497 adds applies to the mutants and not to the
baseline. The baseline measured a second of `mandate-canon` tests, the floor gave twenty seconds,
and every mutant that reached the reference harness blew through it.

That failure is closed, not silent — `cargo mutants` exits 3, which `mutants_outcome` refuses
outright rather than reading as a verdict — but it would have failed every honest change whose
mutants the harness catches slowly. Hence item 3.

### When the bound is wrong

A diff past 192 mutants fails loudly and never quietly: the shard hits GitHub's hard timeout, the
job fails, the `full` aggregate fails, and the PR cannot merge. No mutant is ever reported caught
because its shard ran out of time. The same holds for the two terms measurement bounds rather
than a cap: a runner slow enough to spend more than 60 seconds on a mutant's build, or more than
60 on setup and the baseline, overruns the shard and turns it red. So the cost of this bound
being wrong is a red check and item 6, not a gap in the gate.

## Alternatives considered

- **Raise the ten-minute job limit.** Refused for the reason DEC-473 refused it: it weakens the
  latency gate DEC-464 made enforceable, and a limit tuned to today's suite is a number that rots.
- **Size the matrix on the measured pace rather than on the timeout** (the draft #652's round 6
  refused). `k ≤ 4` and 48 shards follow from pricing a mutant at the 107 seconds the harness took
  when nothing went wrong. But the timeout permits far more than that, so the 48-shard matrix held
  192 mutants only while every one of them behaved. A capacity that is conditional on the thing
  being measured is not a capacity.
- **Set `--build-timeout` as well, so both terms carry an explicit cap.** It would make the budget
  a closed arithmetic guarantee rather than one measured term and one capped one. Refused because
  it buys nothing against the risk this budget exists for and adds a new one: a mutation cannot
  make a build slow, so the cap would only ever fire on a slow runner, and cargo-mutants leaves
  the option off by default for exactly that reason. Trading a real flake for a hypothetical one
  is the wrong direction. Revisit if a mutant is ever seen to lengthen a build.
- **Keep two workers and bound the critical path by waves.** `ceil(k / 2)` waves, each as long as
  its slowest mutant, is the correct arithmetic for two workers, and round 5 was right that an
  earlier draft did not use it. But two workers double a mutant's latency for no throughput these
  measurements can see, so the right fix is to remove the waves rather than bound them.
- **Lower the timeout instead, and keep a smaller matrix.** `k = 3` at 57 shards needs
  `60 + T ≤ 180`, so `T ≤ 120`, which is 1.4 times the slowest legitimate test phase against a
  run-to-run spread of about a quarter. The margin that would buy a third of the jobs is the
  margin that protects green PRs from a red check nobody can reproduce, and on a public
  repository the jobs are free.
- **Leave the per-mutant timeout derived, and run the reference package in the baseline too.** It
  would make the derived number honest, but it charges every run a full harness pass before the
  first mutant, and it would leave the budget depending on a measurement again.
- **Narrow the external oracles so a core mutation does not run every suite.** Tried in three
  shapes and withdrawn in DEC-497: it saves little and buys a failure mode that reports a mutant
  caught when no oracle ran.
- **Make the expensive suites cheaper.** The one change that would shrink the worst case for every
  PR rather than redistribute it, and worth doing on its own merits. It belongs in the backlog,
  not in a PR about configuration references.
- **Larger runners or an external CI service.** Refused for DEC-473's reason: it introduces
  spending or a new service, which DEC-79 reserves to the founder, when the native matrix holds.
