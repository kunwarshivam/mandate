# DEC-498: one mutant per shard, a hundred and ninety-two shards, a cap on every phase cargo-mutants will cap, and a named remainder under them

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-10-07 (UTC) |
| **Decided by** | The merge coordinator (agent), under DEC-79: a reversible scheduling decision of the same kind DEC-464 and DEC-473 made, which the founder may overturn |
| **Story or claim** | [#652](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/652) review rounds 3 to 14; supersedes DEC-473's shard count; DEC-464's ten-minute budget, DEC-497; measured by [#657](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/657) |

## Decision

1. Supersede DEC-473's twenty-four mutation shards with **a hundred and ninety-two** deterministic,
   zero-based slice shards, sized so that the widest safety-critical diff measured so far
   (E6-13's 169 mutants) puts **at most one mutant in a shard**.
2. **Run one mutant at a time** (`MUTANT_JOBS` in `xtask/src/main.rs`, `cargo mutants --jobs 1`).
   Two workers contend, and with item 1 a CI shard has nothing to parallelise anyway; the setting
   still governs the unsharded local run.
3. **Set cargo-mutants' per-mutant test timeout explicitly, to 180 seconds**
   (`MUTANT_TEST_TIMEOUT`), instead of taking the value cargo-mutants derives. `--timeout` also
   caps the unmutated baseline's test phase, so this one number bounds both.
4. **Cap a mutant's build too, at 60 seconds** (`MUTANT_BUILD_TIMEOUT`, `--build-timeout`), which
   cargo-mutants leaves off by default. A mutated operator in a top-level `const` can size an
   array, so a mutant can lengthen a build and not only a test run. Unlike `--timeout`, this one
   spares the baseline, so a cold cache is not cut off.
5. **Budget a shard on what each phase is permitted to cost, not on how fast phases were observed
   to run.** One mutant costs at most 240 seconds and the baseline's tests at most 180, so 420 of
   the ten minutes are bounded by configuration whatever the diff does. Items 1 to 4 are chosen
   together for that sum; none may be changed without redoing it.
6. **Name the remainder rather than claiming to bound it.** Job overhead, the live-test preflight,
   mutant enumeration, and the baseline's *build* are what no cap of ours reaches. A populated
   shard pays all of it whatever slice it draws, so sharding cannot reduce it. Item 5 leaves it
   180 seconds against about 40 measured on a one-package diff, and that reserve is the extent
   of the claim.
7. Keep everything DEC-473 kept: the ten-minute job limit, the complete source diff, the selected
   packages, the live-test preflight, the unmutated baseline, the test command, and the
   zero-survivor rule. `cargo xtask check` stays unsharded as the complete local oracle.
8. **When a shard runs out of time on its mutant, expand the matrix; never the job's ten-minute
   limit, the diff, or the tests.** When the remainder is what overran, item 6 applies instead,
   and the matrix is not the answer: every populated shard pays the same remainder.
9. **Every term of a shard budget is either capped by configuration, or shown by command to be
   beyond a mutant's reach, or named as diff-dependent with its reserve and its failure mode
   stated.** The measurements behind it are taken on `ubuntu-24.04` over repeated runs. This
   rule exists because every draft before it was wrong a different way: the first
   scaled from a four-core developer box and was out by a factor of two and a half; the second
   divided a nine-mutant total by nine and priced a shard at its average mutant instead of its
   critical path; the third priced a mutation-sensitive phase at what the harness does when
   nothing goes wrong; the fourth asserted a phase was beyond a mutant's reach without checking,
   and a reviewer produced the counterexample in one command; the fifth generalised a floor
   measured on nine mutants in one crate to diffs of any width; the sixth, correcting it, reached
   for a ceiling twice more and had both refused; the seventh named a remedy for the floor
   without checking that it reached the floor; and the eighth, correcting *that*, generalised a
   one-package measurement into "the baseline never runs the reference harness", which is false
   for the very diff the matrix is sized on. **A measurement of one diff describes that diff.** A
   term nobody has measured at its worst is recorded as unmeasured; a remedy nobody has checked
   is not named at all; and a claim with "never" or "always" in it is a command to run, since the
   eight drafts above contain no counterexample a reviewer found by reasoning alone.

## Rationale

DEC-497 runs the whole reference harness for any mutation of a crate it is built on, so a mutant
of a core crate costs its own package's tests plus the whole of `mandate-refcases`. #652's review
asked for the arithmetic, then for it to be measured, then for it to be measured properly, then
for the part a mutation controls to be bounded rather than extrapolated, then for the claim
about which parts a mutation controls to be checked rather than argued, and last for the part
that is neither capped nor out of reach to stop pretending to be either; then, once it had
stopped, for the remedy it offered for that part to be checked too; and last for which packages
that part even covers, which turned out to depend on the slice. Each answer replaced the refuted
claim with a fresh one, and the round after refused that one too. This answer stops making them.
A shard has four cost terms, **three of them capped by configuration** — a mutant's build, a
mutant's tests, and the unmutated baseline's tests — and a fourth named for what it is, with no
bound and no remedy claimed.

### What the runner measured

[#657](https://github.com/kunwarshivam/mandate/pull/657) is a throwaway branch built to produce
these numbers: three untested `pub fn`s in `mandate-canon`, nine mutants, none caught, all in a
single shard. An uncaught mutant runs the whole harness to completion, so it measures the
harness's natural pace. The same nine ran four times, twice at one worker and twice at two, with
the timeout lifted so nothing was cut off. Ranges cover all nine mutants in each run; the ninth
is the tail, which runs alone once the others are done, and at two workers it is visibly cheaper
for it.

| Workers | Run | Test phase | Build | Wall clock per mutant | Nine mutants |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a | 77–83s | 16–27s | 99s | 14m49s |
| 1 | b | 63–66s | 16–25s | 82s | 12m17s |
| 2 | a | 61–139s | 21–55s | 83s | 12m29s |
| 2 | b | 90–181s | 24–71s | 108s | 16m15s |

**Why one worker (item 2).** These samples show no throughput difference between the two
settings: 82 and 99 seconds a mutant at one worker, 83 and 108 at two, where the gap between the
two settings is smaller than the gap between repeated runs of either. Two runs each cannot
support a claim about throughput in either direction, and none is needed. What the samples do
show, well outside that spread, is that two workers contend: the per-mutant test phase runs to
181 seconds at two workers and to 83 at one. One worker is chosen for that — it keeps a single
mutant short and predictable, and it makes a shard's duration the sum of its mutants rather than
a sequence of waves.

**Why 180 seconds (item 3).** The timeout has to sit above every legitimate harness run, or it
turns a judged mutant into a `TIMEOUT` and fails a green PR. The slowest legitimate test phase
measured at one worker is 83 seconds, and repeated runs of the same configuration differ by about
a quarter, so 180 is 2.2 times the slowest observation and roughly nine times the observed
run-to-run spread above it. It is not a claim that only hangs reach the cap: a mutation that
makes the code slow without hanging can reach it too, and item 5 is what makes that affordable
rather than a surprise.

### The budget, and what bounds each of its terms

A shard spends its time on four things: job overhead and the live-test preflight, then
cargo-mutants' mutant enumeration and its unmutated baseline, whose build and test phases price
separately, then for each mutant a build and a test phase. **Three of those are capped: both of
a mutant's phases and the baseline's tests. What is left is the baseline's build, enumeration,
the preflight, and job overhead, and this decision claims no bound for it.**

Which cap reaches which phase is not symmetric and is not guessable; both directions below are
read off the tool.

**A mutant's test phase is capped at 180 seconds.** This is the term the first three drafts got
wrong by pricing it at a measurement: a mutant can loop longer, retry more, or be caught only
after the suite has ground through it, and nothing about the observed pace bounds that.
`--timeout` does, because cargo-mutants kills the test run there and records a `TIMEOUT`.

**A mutant's build is capped at 60 seconds**, by `--build-timeout` (`MUTANT_BUILD_TIMEOUT`).
cargo-mutants leaves that option off by default and warns that build times vary, and the draft
before this one took that default and argued no cap was needed — that a mutant only ever edits
an expression inside a function, so it changes what the program does at run time rather than the
work the compiler does. **That argument is false, and #652's round 8 disproved it with a
counterexample.** Operators are mutated wherever they appear, top-level `const` items included:

```
$ cargo mutants --list      # on `pub const N: usize = 1024 % 7;  pub static T: [u8; N] = ...`
src/lib.rs:1:27: replace % with /
src/lib.rs:1:27: replace % with +
```

`1024 % 7` is 2 and `1024 + 7` is 1031, so the array that `N` sizes grows five hundredfold, and
with other constants by far more. That is codegen and link work, not const
evaluation, so the `long_running_const_eval` lint does not catch it, and a mutant can make a
build as slow as it likes. Sixty seconds stands against the 16 to 27 seconds that mutant builds
took over the two runs at one worker, the setting item 2 picks.

The flakiness cargo-mutants warns about is why that option is off by default, and it is contained
here because this cap, unlike `--timeout`, reaches mutants alone. Measured directly: with
`--build-timeout 3`, a twelve-second baseline build completed and was reported `ok`, while every
mutant build was cut off at three seconds. So a cold cache compiling the workspace from scratch
is never cut off. A mutant's own build is not purely incremental on that baseline — the baseline
compiles the mutated package and the mutant compiles the reference harness alongside it, 9
seconds against 18 on the shard measured below — but the 16-to-27-second range the cap stands
against was measured on mutants that paid exactly that extra, so it already includes it.

**The baseline's test phase is capped at 180 seconds too, by the same `--timeout`.** The draft
before this one had the baseline out of `--timeout`'s reach as well as `--build-timeout`'s, by
analogy with the build. That is wrong, and the command says so: rerun the shard below with
`--timeout 5` and the baseline is killed mid-suite and the whole run refused.

```
$ cargo mutants --in-diff … --timeout 5 --build-timeout 60 --shard 12/96 --sharding slice
# the 96-shard matrix of the draft this was measured against; shard 12 held the refcases mutant
SIGTERM [0.118s] (46/691) mandate-refcases mandate::research::tests::every_input_object_rejects_…
*** result: Timeout
ERROR cargo test failed in an unmutated tree, so no mutants were tested
```

So the two caps divide differently, and which is which has to be read off the tool rather than
guessed: `--timeout` covers every test phase, the baseline's included; `--build-timeout` covers
every build phase *except* the baseline's. That asymmetry is the whole of the budget's shape.

**What is left is the remainder, it depends on the diff, and no cap of ours covers it.** Round 9
refused an earlier draft's claim that setup and the baseline are beyond a diff's reach, and was
right twice over. Unmutated means *this PR's* source, not `main`'s, so the baseline's cost is the
cost of building and testing exactly the packages the diff touches. And the baseline is not the
whole of it: ahead of it every shard runs the live-test preflight (`cargo nextest list` per
mutated package, DEC-139), and cargo-mutants enumerates the complete mutant list before `--shard`
selects a slice of it.

Call the uncapped part the **remainder**: job overhead, preflight, enumeration, and the
baseline's build.

- **It grows with the diff, and this decision does not bound it.** The preflight compiles and
  lists each mutated package, cargo-mutants builds its own baseline over those same packages, and
  enumeration generates and serialises a candidate per mutation site. No ceiling is claimed for
  it. Three were tried and withdrawn, each in the round after it was written; what a maximally
  wide diff costs is an open question, and the honest answer is that nobody has measured one.
- **Sharding cannot reduce it**, which is round 9's sharpest point. Every *populated* shard pays
  it in full. An empty slice is the exception: cargo-mutants returns before the baseline, which
  is why a shard with no mutant to test costs 10 seconds and not 50.
- **Which packages it covers is decided by the shard's own mutants, not by `--test-package`.**
  This is round 14's finding and it reverses what rounds 12 and 13 recorded. Two runs of the same
  gate, differing only in which slice they draw:

  ```
  shard 0, mutant in mandate-journal
    baseline:  cargo nextest run --package=mandate-journal@0.0.0
               Starting 106 tests across 8 binaries     Summary [ 1.364s]   9s build
  shard 12, mutant in mandate-refcases
    baseline:  cargo nextest run --package=mandate-refcases@0.0.0
               Starting 691 tests across 13 binaries    Summary [31.961s]  19s build
  ```

  Both runs passed `--test-package=mandate-journal --test-package=mandate-refcases`; the baselines
  differ by a factor of twenty-three because cargo-mutants selects the baseline's packages from
  the mutants in the slice. So a shard whose mutants live in `mandate-refcases` pays the whole
  reference harness before its first mutant — and E6-13's 169-mutant diff, which this matrix is
  sized on, mutates `mandate-refcases` among others. DEC-473 measured exactly that: "the unmutated
  baselines consumed about one to two minutes" on that diff. Item 3's cap is what holds that piece
  down; the build under it is not capped, and is part of the remainder.
- **Measured on `ubuntu-24.04`, on this decision's own matrix and its one-package diff**: 24
  seconds of job overhead (set-up, checkout, toolchain, cache), and a populated shard's
  `cargo xtask ci mutants` step of 54 seconds, of which 39 were its mutant and 1 the baseline's
  tests, leaving 14 for the preflight, enumeration, and a 9-second baseline build. **About 40
  altogether**, on a diff touching one package. The widest measurement anyone has is DEC-473's,
  baselines of one to two minutes on E6-13, of which item 3 now caps the test phase and the build
  falls here; nobody has separated those two on that diff.

The budget therefore reserves whatever the capped phases leave:

```
k × (60 + 180) + 180 ≤ 600 − remainder      so k = 1 leaves the remainder 180 seconds
```

The second term is the baseline's capped test phase; `k` is the mutants in the slice. At `k = 2`
the capped phases alone come to 660 and overrun the limit before the remainder is counted at all,
which is why **one mutant per shard** is item 1 rather than a nicety. `--sharding slice` gives
shard `i` the half-open range `[i × c, (i+1) × c)` for `c = ceil(N / S)`: early slices are full,
one may be partial, and trailing slices are empty, which is not an even spread. A slice holds at
most `c`, so one mutant a shard needs `S ≥ N`. The widest safety-critical diff measured so far is
E6-13's 169, so **a hundred and ninety-two** — double the ninety-six an earlier draft of this
decision chose, clear of 169, and inside GitHub's matrix ceiling of 256.

420 of the ten minutes are now bounded by configuration whatever the diff does. The 180 seconds
left over is a reserve against a remainder measured at about 40 on a one-package diff — not a
proof that it fits any diff, and no such proof is offered. A diff wide enough to exceed it reddens the shard; it never
passes a mutant that nothing judged.

In normal operation a shard is far cheaper than any of this. A caught mutant stops at nextest's
first failure, so #652's own shards, one caught mutant each, finished in 72 to 88 seconds
end to end, and the shards with no mutant to test finished in 44.

A hundred and ninety-two jobs is eight times the twenty-four DEC-473 set and `main` runs today.
On a public repository GitHub's standard runners cost nothing, so the price is concurrency and
not money. How short the jobs are depends on the diff and not on the matrix: a one-mutant change
leaves 191 shards exiting after setup, while E6-13's 169 would populate 169 of the 192, which is
the case the matrix exists for and the one to price it on.

Concurrency was the open question, since the matrix does not run all at once, and the first run
of it answers that too. On this decision's own one-mutant diff the 192 shards took 28 to 82
seconds each and 7,147 seconds of runner time between them, while the whole `ci` workflow
finished in **7 minutes 18 seconds** end to end: about sixteen shards at a time. The aggregate is
not a required check's timeout — `full` waits on the matrix and is itself a one-minute job — but
it is the latency a PR author feels, and DEC-464's reason for the ten-minute rule. A diff with
many mutants spends longer, which is the point: the matrix buys per-shard headroom, not a shorter
wall clock.

It does multiply one thing that is not free. Every shard downloads cargo-mutants from GitHub's
release assets, so the matrix makes a hundred and ninety-two attempts where DEC-473 made
twenty-four, and a
transient failure in any one of them reddens a required check that DEC-464 forbids retrying
automatically. This is not hypothetical: the run on this decision's own branch lost five shards
to `curl: (22) The requested URL returned error: 500` within the same second. So every
checksum-verified download in `ci.yml` now passes `--retry 5 --retry-delay 2
--retry-all-errors`. The checksum check is unchanged and still decides what is installed; the
retry only decides how many times a transient 500 is allowed to waste a job.

### What the measurement found on the way

The first #657 run reported nine `TIMEOUT`s, every one at a twenty-second cap, and no verdicts at
all. cargo-mutants sets its per-mutant test timeout to five times the unmutated baseline's test
phase, floored at twenty seconds, and it runs that baseline over **the slice's own mutated
packages**: the `--test-package=mandate-refcases` that DEC-497 adds applies to the mutants, and
reaches the baseline only when a mutant of that package is in the slice. Here the baseline
measured a second of `mandate-canon` tests, the floor gave twenty seconds, and every mutant that
reached the reference harness blew through it.

That failure is closed, not silent — `cargo mutants` exits 3, which `mutants_outcome` refuses
outright rather than reading as a verdict — but it would have failed every honest change whose
mutants the harness catches slowly. Hence item 3.

### When the budget is wrong

Whatever runs a shard out of time fails loudly and never quietly: the shard hits GitHub's hard
timeout, the job fails, the `full` aggregate fails, and the PR cannot merge. No mutant is ever
reported caught because its shard ran out of time. So being wrong here costs a red check, never a
gap in the gate.

Being wrong is not the same as exceeding 192 mutants, though. The budget is a worst case, and a
caught mutant stops at nextest's first failure, so a 200-mutant diff whose mutants are all caught
quickly finishes comfortably. What the matrix guarantees is narrower than its converse, and worth
stating exactly: up to 192 mutants, **the three capped phases cannot exceed 420 of a shard's 600
seconds, however badly its mutant behaves**. Whether the shard survives still depends on the
remainder fitting the other 180, and item 6 is the admission that nothing here guarantees that.

What to do when one does overrun depends on which term it was, and the two are not symmetric.
Too many mutants in a slice is answered by item 8: widen the matrix. A remainder past its reserve
has no answer here — item 6 says only what not to do, because more shards each paying the same
remainder changes nothing. That case needs its own decision, taken against a measurement of the
diff that caused it. Saying so is the point of item 6: a decision that named a remedy it had not
checked would be the same mistake as a decision that named a bound it had not measured, and
rounds 13 and 14 are what each of those mistakes cost.

### A disagreement this surfaced, not resolved here

Checking a shard's budget against the other jobs turned up a conflict between the ten-minute rule and
the workflow that implements it. [ADR-0001](../../adr/0001-engineering-setup.md) ES-12 says
"every required check and every job it aggregates has a hard timeout of ten minutes or less",
and DEC-464 made that the enforceable gate. But `ci.yml`'s `fast` job, one of the two required
checks, carries `timeout-minutes: 15`.

Neither reading is taken here. Lowering `fast` to ten minutes tightens the rule but is not
risk-free — the job runs near seven minutes today, so the cap could redden honest changes — and
raising the ADR to fifteen weakens a stated gate, which DEC-79 reserves to the founder. DEC-176
lets an agent settle only a reading that adds no risk, and neither of these does. Recorded for
the founder; this decision's own jobs (`mutants`, `full-checks`) are at ten.

## Alternatives considered

- **Raise the ten-minute job limit.** Refused for the reason DEC-473 refused it: it weakens the
  latency gate DEC-464 made enforceable, and a limit tuned to today's suite is a number that rots.
- **Size the matrix on the measured pace rather than on the timeout** (the draft #652's round 6
  refused). `k ≤ 4` and 48 shards follow from pricing a mutant at the 107 seconds the harness took
  when nothing went wrong. But the timeout permits far more than that, so the 48-shard matrix held
  192 mutants only while every one of them behaved. A capacity that is conditional on the thing
  being measured is not a capacity.
- **Leave `--build-timeout` at its default of none, and argue the build is beyond a mutant's
  reach** (the draft #652's round 8 refused). The argument was that every mutation genre edits an
  expression inside a function, so a mutant changes run-time behaviour and not the compiler's
  work. It is false: operators are mutated in top-level `const` items too, and a `const` can size
  an array. The lesson is item 9's — "no mutant can reach this" is a claim to check with a
  command, not to reason to.
- **Keep two workers and bound the critical path by waves.** `ceil(k / 2)` waves, each as long as
  its slowest mutant, is the correct arithmetic for two workers, and round 5 was right that an
  earlier draft did not use it. But two workers double a mutant's latency for no throughput these
  measurements can see, so the right fix is to remove the waves rather than bound them.
- **Keep ninety-six shards and lower the test timeout to fit two mutants** (the draft #652's
  round 14 refused, by refuting the arithmetic it rested on). Two mutants and a capped baseline
  need `2 × (60 + T) + T ≤ 600 − remainder`; at a remainder of 50 that is `T ≤ 143`, which is 1.7
  times the slowest legitimate test phase against a run-to-run spread of about a quarter. The
  margin that would halve the job count is the margin that protects green PRs from a red check
  nobody can reproduce, and on a public repository the jobs are free. Worse, it buys that margin
  from the one term the gate must never be stingy with: `T` is what stands between a slow honest
  mutant and a `TIMEOUT` that reports nothing.
- **Call the floor beyond a mutation's reach, since the baseline is unmutated** (the draft
  #652's round 9 refused). Unmutated means this PR's source, not `main`'s, so the baseline's
  cost is the diff's cost; and the preflight and the mutant enumeration ahead of it scale with
  the diff too. Generalising a floor measured on nine mutants in one crate was the same mistake
  as the drafts before it, one term further down. It is now named rather than bounded.
- **Leave the per-mutant timeout derived, and run the reference package in the baseline too.** It
  would make the derived number honest, but it charges every run a full harness pass before the
  first mutant — on every shard, including the ones whose mutants are nowhere near the harness —
  and it would put the per-mutant term back on a measurement, the mistake items 3 to 5 exist to
  undo.
- **Narrow the external oracles so a core mutation does not run every suite.** Tried in three
  shapes and withdrawn in DEC-497: it saves little and buys a failure mode that reports a mutant
  caught when no oracle ran.
- **Make the reference harness cheaper.** Filed as E1-6, and worth doing: it is what puts a core
  crate's mutant test phase at 83 seconds, so it is the lever on item 3's cap and through it on
  the matrix, and on a refcases shard it shortens the baseline's capped test phase as well. It is
  a lever on capped phases, not on the remainder, which is the baseline's *build* and the setup
  around it. Three drafts put this differently and two were wrong: the floor's only lever (round
  13 refused it), no lever on the floor at all (round 14 refused that), and a lever on the
  remainder (round 15 refused that). Either way it does not belong in a PR about configuration
  references.
- **Larger runners or an external CI service.** Refused for DEC-473's reason: it introduces
  spending or a new service, which DEC-79 reserves to the founder, when the native matrix holds.
