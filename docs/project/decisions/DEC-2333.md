## Context

§5.5 gives the kill switch's sequence as "apply the final mode first (`paused` for mandate limits;
`stopped` for an owner kill switch) → cancel → confirm → close". For the account scope, the cancel
is the broker's cancel-all and the close is close-position per instrument. The first draft of the
account scope (PR #2183) sent close-position in the same step as cancel-all, with no confirm. A
protective sell still working beside a close-position can fill too and sell the same shares twice,
leaving a short position (`AGENTS.md` rule 12; v1 has no short sales). Today the executor cannot
read the cancel-all's answer: `BrokerOutcome::AccountWideAccepted` and the open-orders listing
answer E7-3's stub.

## Invariants first

- The final mode is applied, and the switch journaled, before anything is sent (`AGENTS.md` rules
  5 and 13).
- No close-position goes while a sell the broker may still fill works in that instrument.
- A cancel-all is never resent without its own idempotency key.
- No instrument is left unprotected beyond §5.4's bound without the owner being told.
- Protection placed at that bound never covers shares a sell not confirmed cancelled may still
  sell.
- Nothing re-places protection beside a close-position that has gone out.

## Decision

1. **Sequence.** Final mode → `KillSwitchActivated` → cancel-all → confirm → close-position, each
   step journaled before it is sent (§5.5). The final mode is the initiator's
   (`Initiator::final_mode`, DEC-100): `stopped` for an owner's or a platform operator's switch,
   `paused` for a mandate limit's. It is one `AgentModeApplied` for `*`, every agent of the account,
   those the executor has not yet seen included.
2. **What confirms.** The broker's `AccountWideAccepted` for the cancel-all, then a fresh
   open-orders read that shows no working sell in an instrument. A close-position goes only for an
   instrument so confirmed, one instrument at a time; an instrument with a sell still working
   waits.
3. **An Unknown cancel-all.** If the cancel-all's answer is lost, the executor reads open orders by
   listing; it never resends the cancel-all without its own idempotency key.
   - Any order of ours the read shows still working is cancelled by its id and confirmed, a buy as
     much as a sell: a buy left working adds the risk the switch exists to remove. An instrument
     with a sell still working closes once that cancel is confirmed.
   - With no read, the instrument waits. No order is `Unknown` here: only the cancel-all's answer
     is lost, so the hold is the broker's, which rule 13 allows. It is bounded by reads, not by a
     clock: every scheduled reconciliation, and a restart's startup reconciliation, is a read, and
     the first the broker answers confirms the instrument or names what still works. The owner is
     alerted at §5.4's bound (below) and at each reconciliation the broker leaves unanswered, as
     reconciliation already alerts. A hold that outlasts reads the broker answers cannot arise. If
     the founder reads a lost answer with a reachable broker otherwise, that call is theirs (rule
     13, DEC-79); until then this, the conservative reading, stands: hold, and alert.
   - **What the wait journals** (`ProtectionChanged`, §9.5). A protective order the cancel-all
     cancels starts its instrument's interval, `unprotected_start` naming no order, where none is
     open. At `max_unprotected_s` the interval journals `interval_limit` and the owner is alerted.
     It ends with `unprotected_end` when the close leaves nothing held (A1b). Nothing is placed in
     between, but for this item's re-placement at the bound (A1b).
   - **What the owner is told.** The notice is opaque (rule 6): the `protection` kind,
     `attention_needed`, executor key `unprotected_interval_limit` (notifications spec §5). Its
     subject is the `interval_limit` record, which names the instrument, so the owner sees in the
     app that the position stays unprotected until its close goes out; the notice itself says
     nothing more.
   - The wait is an unprotected interval bounded by `max_unprotected_s` (§5.4, "Bounded
     unprotected intervals", default 60 seconds). At the bound, §5.4 applies as for any interval:
     protection is placed again for the held quantity less every sell in the instrument **not
     confirmed cancelled**, and the owner is alerted. A sell counts as working until a read shows it
     gone or the broker confirms its cancel: the cancel-all's lost answer confirms nothing, so the
     protection that rested before the switch, every `Unknown` order, and every sell the last read
     showed still count. Where that leaves nothing, nothing is placed: §5.4 sizes against the held
     quantity and does not say what an unconfirmed cancel counts as, so this reading tightens it
     (rule 12; v1 has no short sales). When a later read confirms the instrument, that protection
     is cancelled by id and confirmed before its close-position goes. A1b's tests pin it, its
     first among them a lost cancel-all at the bound with the original stop still counted, so
     nothing is placed beside it.
4. **Not taken: closing at once.** Sending close-position in the same step, on the reading that a
   broker's close-position cancels the symbol's open orders itself, is a broker-specific claim that
   loosens §5.5. It is not taken.
5. **Never re-placed beside a close.** Once an instrument's close-position has gone out, nothing
   re-places protection there: neither `re_cover`, nor the buffer day's re-placement, nor
   `stop_after` (#2183's finding). An instrument leaves that set once it is no longer held long, and
   every instrument once the account's kill-switch mode is lifted.
6. **Slices.** Each is its own DEC-77 pair:
   - **A1a:** the cancel side: the sequence up to the cancel-all, its `AccountWideAccepted` answer,
     and the open-orders read. PR #2183 becomes A1a's tests.
   - **A1b:** the close on confirmation, item 3's waits, and its re-placement at the bound sized
     against every sell not confirmed cancelled. Until A1b, nothing is placed in an instrument the
     switch closes, at the bound or anywhere else: no `re_cover`, buffer-day re-placement,
     `stop_after`, `bracket` OCO, or `re_place`, and §5.4's bound journals and alerts but never
     sells beside the close.
7. **No owner path binds the account scope before A2b.** Where any held instrument may not close at
   the market now, the account switch still answers the stub (`Unimplemented`), which the executor
   process treats as fatal. No path sends it one today: the process applies agent-scoped switches
   only (Follow-ups). The API, the runtime and the process must not route an owner's account switch
   to an executor until A2b handles the out-of-session case, so the stub is never reached.

## Consequences

- A1a and A1b replace the single A1 slice in DEC-2309's plan; A2a, A2b, A3 and A4 follow.
- `AccountWideAccepted` and `OpenOrders` stop answering E7-3's stub for the account scope.

## Follow-ups

- **A workspace switch arrives today as per-agent switches.** The API accepts the workspace scope
  (`mandate-api-server`), the runtime fans it to each deployment in the workspace
  (`Deployment::in_scope`), and `mandate-executord`'s `apply` copies each one into the core as
  `KillScope::Agent` for its agent. It never reaches an executor as an account-wide switch. A4
  starts by finding the component that sees every deployment, so it can report one that never
  applied its copy (DEC-2309 item 3).

## Alternatives considered

- **Close in the same step as the cancel-all** (item 4). Rejected: it skips §5.5's confirm.
- **Close anyway after one read on an Unknown cancel-all.** Rejected: a read that never comes back
  would close beside a sell that may still fill.
- **Resend the cancel-all on a lost answer.** Rejected: without its own idempotency key a resend
  cannot be told from the first.
