# Weekly critique, 9 October 2026

The first run of the weekly critique ([plan.md](plan.md), section A): screenshots of every screen
and one question per screen, "what is the worst thing on this screen?". Each finding names that one
thing and the smallest fix; nothing else on the screen is judged. Like the
[review of 7 October](review-2026-10-07.md), this file is a working list, not a spec (DEC-511 item
6): a finding becomes a plan item, and one that changes a rule in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) takes a
DEC first.

Each finding is tied to a [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) rule or to the plan's premise: the owner, the
owl, the rules and the record. Nothing here loosens a safety behaviour (Stop always visible, the
paper badge, the disclosure, approvals), proposes compliance wording, or proposes a platform trade
recommendation. Where a fix touches the safety surface ([DEC-516](../../docs/project/decisions/DEC-516.md)
item 1), the finding says so.

## How the pictures were taken

`npm run shots` on `main` at `862b69c5`, against `npm run dev`, with Chromium 1194
(`SHOTS_CHROMIUM`). That is 98 viewport captures with no failures: the golden path (9 screens) and
every listed screen (12) at 390 and 1440px in light and dark, then Home and Agent 1 under each of
the seven stress scenarios at 1440px light. The pictures are not committed; run the same command to
see them.

Two things about the pictures limit what they show. Both are findings (C-27, C-28):

- The fixture scenario panel sits over the bottom right of every desktop capture and over the
  bottom of every phone capture. It hides Skip on the request at 1440px and the pinned Approve and
  Skip on a phone.
- The Agent 1 captures under the stress scenarios show an agent the scenario leaves alone. Paused,
  reconciliation, unknown order and the stale prices all happen to Agent 2.

## Across every screen

**C-1. DESIGN.md describes a palette the product does not ship.** Every screen, both themes.
DESIGN.md's front matter says "Ink and Volt (DEC-214)". Its Colors table gives volt hex values,
and its Meaning Rule says "Volt is your mandate and the account's line". The product ships Azure
and Sun ([DEC-217](../../docs/project/decisions/DEC-217.md), [COLOR.md](../COLOR.md),
`src/lib/palette.ts`, whose header says it supersedes DEC-214's volt). There is no volt ramp in
the code, and DEC-217 also made the hero line trend-coloured, so it is green on a gain and red on a
loss. DESIGN.md says gain and loss colour signed figures only. The pictures match DEC-217. The
rules file contradicts its own opening claim that "every value here is shipped".
This is the worst thing in the product, not on one screen. *The rules*: the file every change
is checked against must describe what ships, or no change can be checked against it.
*Fix:* rewrite the Colors section, the front matter and the Meaning Rule's colour words to Azure
and Sun and the trend-coloured hero line, citing DEC-217. *DEC:* yes. AGENTS.md says to stop and
ask when code and docs disagree, so the founder confirms that DEC-217 stands before DESIGN.md
changes. The DEC then records that DESIGN.md follows DEC-217.

## The golden path

**C-2. Welcome, 390px, both themes.** The first phone screen shows the window chrome, the ASCII
wordmark, the owls and the subhead. "Sign the guestbook", "See why it traded" and "Sign in" are all
below the fold. On a phone the desktop's Sign in icon is not there either, so a returning owner has
to scroll past the wordmark to get in. *The owner*: the page's one job for someone who already has a
place is the way in. *Fix:* on phones, put the three buttons directly under the subhead and the
beta line after them. *DEC:* no. DEC-467 chose the letters, not the order.

**C-3. Sign in, both widths.** The logon window carries the brand owl only as a 12px title-bar icon
on the Great Wave, so the two public pages do not yet share a face. The finding is the same as the
review's R-23, and plan section H already holds the fix ("Sign-in carries the owls"). Nothing new
is proposed. Sign-in is on the safety surface (DEC-516), so the H item runs in the safety lane.
*The owl.*

**C-4. Set up an agent, both widths.** The composer is a blank box with no label, and its send
button's lower shadow draws an arc that reads as a loading spinner. Under one paragraph of
instructions the screen looks like it is still loading, not asking. *The owner*: this is where the
owner writes the rules, and nothing on the screen says "type here". *Fix:* a visible label above
the field, for example "Your answer", and a flat send button. Placeholders, example prompts and
example amounts stay out (`new-agent.test.tsx`, DEC-476, DEC-477). *DEC:* no.

**C-5. Home with a request, 1440px.** The same request appears twice on Home: once in the volt card
under Needs you ("Agent 2 asks to buy 2 XYZ…"), and again as the first row of the Decisions rail
("Asked you · Buy 2 XYZ at $141.30"). DESIGN.md's Needs you line says "a request appears once on
Home". *The owner*: one thing is asking, and the screen shows it as two. *Fix:* while a request is
open in Needs you, Home's Decisions rail leaves out its "Asked you" row. The audit's Gate decisions
and the agent's Decisions tab keep it. This is the narrowest step of plan section C's "One
request, one place". *DEC:* no, because it carries out a stated rule.

**C-6. The request, both widths.** "Why you are asked" reads: Your rule "low_score": ask when the
combined model score is below 0.65. The owner's rule appears by its machine id, in snake case
and in quotes. The same id shows on Home's Decisions rail, the Approvals rail ("large_orders",
"low_score"), the thread's request card and the audit. *The rules*: the premise is rules the owner
wrote, and here the owner meets them as a variable name. *Fix:* show the rule's sentence alone
("Your rule: ask when the combined model score is below 0.65") wherever the owner reads it, and
keep the id in the record and the audit. The text is the product's own wording, not disclosure
text. *DEC:* no. The approval flow is on the safety surface, so this runs in the safety lane.

**C-7. Agent overview, 1440px.** The mandate card's chip says "Trading". Directly under it, the
next level reads "Daily loss limit $9,849.00 · $274.45 below equity now. Selling only until a new
risk day." That reads as the agent's current mode and contradicts the chip. The mandate tab and
the thread's Headroom rail both prefix the same phrase with "At the limit:"; the overview card
does not. *The rules*: what the agent may do now and what it will do at a limit must never read the
same. *Fix:* the overview card says "At the limit: selling only until a new risk day", as the
other two do. *DEC:* no.

**C-8. Agent decisions, 1440px.** On every desktop tab except Overview, nothing says what the agent
may do now. The desktop header carries the owl, the name and the paper badge, and the mode chip
lives only inside the Overview's mandate card. The phone header has the chip on every tab.
*The owl*: an agent's mode is the first thing the owner should see on its page (DEC-512). *Fix:*
put the mode chip beside the name in the desktop agent header, as the phone does. *DEC:* no.

**C-9. Agent mandate, both widths.** The ladder is drawn twice. A horizontal rail of ticks runs
left to right, then a vertical list of the same levels runs from the top. They are one ladder in
two orientations, and the pale azure field holds both. This is plan section F's "The ladder drawn
once" (R-22); nothing new is proposed. The critique ranks it as the worst thing on the strongest
screen. *The rules.*

**C-10. Stop sheet, both widths.** The agent rows under "One agent" give a name, a slug and a mode
chip, with no owl. This is the one list of agents in the product without their owls, and it is the
one where the owner chooses which agent to stop. *The owl*: everywhere else an owner tells agents
apart by their owl. *Fix:* the agent's 32px owl at the start of each row, in its mode's frame.
Nothing else in the sheet changes (its order, its weights, the passkey). *DEC:* no. This is on the
safety surface, so it runs in the safety lane.

## Every screen

**C-11. Agents, 1440px and 390px.** Each sparkline draws the daily loss limit as a dashed volt rule
across its foot, directly under the line's lowest point. At $274, $181 and $100 above their limits,
all three agents look as if they touched their limit today. The pale region is meant to "sit as a
sliver at the foot while the limit is far below" (`sparkline.tsx`, DEC-515). The dashed rule on
top of the sliver turns that into a line the curve sits on. *The rules*: the row must show how
close the agent is to its limit, and it shows every agent at its limit. *Fix:* when the limit is
below the line's range, draw the pale sliver without the dashed rule. The rule appears only once
the limit is inside the range. *DEC:* no.

**C-12. Approvals, 1440px.** The "What sends you a request" rail repeats one paragraph three times,
once per agent, word for word apart from the wait ("Your rules "large_orders", "low_score" ask
you, and so does anything no rule covers…"). *The rules*: three copies of the same rules read as
three different rules. *Fix:* group agents that share rules into one paragraph naming them (Agent
1 and Agent 2 wait 10 min, Agent 3 waits 15 min). Rule ids follow C-6. *DEC:* no.

**C-13. Alerts, both widths.** On a calm day the screen lists four feeds with timestamps and no
word that all is well, so four rows under "Alerts" read as four alerts. The right edges of the
as-of column also differ by row. *The owner*: silence is a feature (plan E), and this screen should
say it. *Fix:* when every feed answers and no agent is restricted, the page opens with the
all-clear line Home uses, and the timestamps right-align on one edge. *DEC:* no.

**C-14. Positions, 390px.** The table is wider than the phone. Value and Unrealized are off the
right edge, so the screen's most important figures are hidden, and BTC/USD's protection line wraps
into a seven-line ribbon in the instrument column. *The owner*: a phone that cannot show what a
position is worth is not a remote control. *Fix:* below 64rem, each position is a two-line row: the
instrument and value on the first line, quantity, mark and protection on the second. The
"Unrealized paper P&L, simulated" note stays as it is. *DEC:* no.

**C-15. Messages, both widths.** The Owlhead copilot's row uses the brand owl, whose feather colour
is picked at random on each page load from the colours an agent's owl may take (DEC-452). On this
load it was Agent 1's blue, so the copilot and Agent 1 sat two rows apart wearing the same face.
The header owl did the same on Home and Agents. *The owl*: an owl is an agent, so a second owl of
the same colour reads as a second agent. *Fix:* draw the brand owl from a colour no agent owl
uses, or exclude the colours of the workspace's agents from the pick. *DEC:* yes, amending DEC-452,
and for the founder: "Choose the color at random each time" is the founder's own instruction.

**C-16. An agent's thread, 390px.** The thread header packs back, owl, name, mode chip, a slug cut
to "btc-acc…", "Desk" and the paper badge into one row. "Desk" sits alone with no "Chat" beside
it, so it reads as a word, not a switch. *The owner.* *Fix:* drop the slug from the phone thread
header, since the owl and the name already say which agent it is. Name "Desk" in the vocabulary
decision plan section D already holds ("Three nouns"). *DEC:* no for the slug; the name goes
through the D item.

**C-17. Audit, 1440px.** The dense screens start their content at 40px, while the page's own
"Fixture data" tag, and every calm screen, start at 120px. Moving from More to Audit makes the
left edge jump, and the tag floats away from the column it labels. *The record*: the screens that
hold the record look like a different product. *Fix:* the dense column keeps the calm page's left
edge and only widens to the right, or the tag follows the dense column. Pick whichever keeps one
left edge across both densities. *DEC:* no.

**C-18. Gate decisions, 1440px dark.** "Allowed" is a filled chip whose fill sits a step from the
page in dark mode. Its shape disappears, and the verdict column looks like an empty slot beside
three outlined chips (Not allowed, Asked you, Waiting). *The record*: the one verdict that means
"the gate passed it" is the one you cannot see. *Fix:* give the Allowed chip a hairline edge in
both themes, keeping one width and its place first in the row (DEC-512), and add the chip's fill
on the background to the contrast pairs. *DEC:* no.

**C-19. Timeline, 1440px.** Today's entries show a time and the older ones a date inline ("Sep 25,
2026, 08:15"). Nothing marks where today ends, so the jump from 08:02 to 25 September is easy to
miss. *The record*: a record read without its days misleads. *Fix:* a day heading between days
("Today", "25 September"), with times alone under each. *DEC:* no.

**C-20. Workspace (settings), 1440px.** The whole page is one sentence of what is coming: policies,
members, notifications, clients, disclosures, billing, deployment and profile. More still lists it.
The subtitle also calls the workspace's policies "Rules", the owner's word for the mandate.
*The rules*, and plan C's "Remove the unbuilt" (DEC-513), whose letter this meets (an index page
names what is coming in one line) but not its point. *Fix:* leave Workspace out of More until one
section is built. Until then, the subtitle does not say "Rules". *DEC:* no.

**C-21. Command palette, both widths.** With the palette open, the dock's Stop sits under the
palette's 40% scrim. The More sheet, by contrast, is not modal "so Stop stays pressable"
(reference.md). The palette lists "Stop…" last, below the fold on a phone. Whether the dimmed
Stop takes a press was not checked here, and a modal palette normally makes the page inert. *The Control Rule*: Stop is "one tap from anywhere… never hidden". *Fix:* first
verify with `e2e/stop-visible.spec.ts` that Stop takes a press with the palette open. If it does
not, keep the frame's Stop above the palette's scrim, as the More sheet does. This tightens the
rule and loosens nothing. *DEC:* no. This is on the safety surface, so it runs in the safety lane.

**C-22. Home, 1440px, both themes.** The account chart is blank in four of the ten desktop Home

*DEC:* no.

## The stress scenarios

**C-23. Drawdown, Home and Agent 1, 1440px.** The restriction reads "Drawdown: sizes scaled since
15:12:40" while the screen's as-of is 14:05:18. The time is from 26 September (the fixture sets
the date). It is printed without its date, so it reads as a time in the future. *The record*: a
time on a record that cannot be placed is wrong, not merely terse. *Fix:* any time not from today
carries its date, through one formatter shared with the timeline (C-19). *DEC:* no.

**C-24. Drawdown, Agent 1, 1440px.** The selling-only restriction says "Ends when: You acknowledge
it with step-up…" and "Who acts: You". Neither line, nor anything near it, says where the owner
does that. *The owner*: a restriction only the owner can end must say where it ends. *Fix:* "Who
acts" names the place and links to it if it is built, keeping its step-up; if it is not built, it
says so. *DEC:* no. Acknowledgement resumes risk, so this runs in the safety lane, and the fix
only names a place; it adds no new path.

**C-25. Unknown order, Home, 1440px.** Stop is loud (filled ink) for a risk reason, but nothing in
Needs you says what the reason is. Needs you holds only the approval. The held exit appears in the
Decisions rail as "Held · Sell 5 QRS", the fourth thing down. In drawdown and reconciliation, by
contrast, Needs you carries the agent's condition ("Agent 1: drawdown: selling only", "Agent 2:
checking with the broker"). *The owl, and the Control Rule's "loud only for a risk reason"*: a loud
Stop should have its reason on the same screen. *Fix:* Needs you carries "Agent 2: an order's state
is unknown" as it does the other conditions (DEC-513). *DEC:* no.

**C-26. Stale, Home, 1440px.** The status strip marks "Stale" and "Down" as solid ink chips, but ink
means the account's actions, a stopped agent and Stop. The Meaning Rule says system states carry no
meaning colour, and the chart footer's own "Stale" on the same screen is outlined. *The Meaning
Rule.* *Fix:* the strip's state chips outlined, like the footer's. *DEC:* no.
