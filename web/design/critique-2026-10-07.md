# A critique panel, 7 October 2026

An exercise the founder asked for: the product and the website criticised as designers of the
first rank would criticise them. The voices are imagined, built from each designer's public work
and writing; nothing here is a quotation and none of them has seen Owlhead. The point is the
standard, not the attribution. Based on the screenshots in
[review-2026-10-07.md](review-2026-10-07.md). What we do about it is at the end.

## Ryo Lu (Cursor; before that Notion): the interface should disappear

1. **What is Home for?** It is trying to be five screens: a balance, a chart, an inbox, a feed and
   a portfolio. A great home screen answers one question and gets out of the way. The question an
   owner has is "do I need to do anything?" and the answer is a rail item at the same weight as a
   feed entry. Make the answer the screen. When nothing needs them, the screen should be nearly
   empty, and that emptiness is the product working.
2. **Too much chrome for too little content.** A header with a wordmark, a workspace switcher, a
   breadcrumb and a command bar; a dock with nine items; a page title with a badge; then the
   content. Notion's lesson: every fixed element is a tax on every screen. The dock is good. The
   header can be the owl and the search; the rest goes.
3. **The mandate is the product and it is a sidebar.** The one thing Owlhead has that nothing else
   has sits in a pale box on the right with blue-on-blue bars. If it were mine, the mandate would
   be the agent page, and the chart would be inside it.
4. **Pick one world.** The landing page is a place; the product is a template with owls in it.
   Either the product inherits the desktop's confidence (bevels, a real title bar, the letter) or
   the landing page is a poster for a product that looks nothing like it. The current answer,
   "two fonts borrowed for two labels", is neither.
5. **Three ways to talk to it.** Messages, the copilot (⌘J) and the set-up chat are three chat
   surfaces with three composers and three voices. One conversation, in one place, that can set up
   an agent, answer about it and pause it. Fewer, better.
6. **Names.** Agent, mandate, deployment, workspace, connection, account, environment: seven nouns
   on an owner's first screen. An owner has an agent with rules on their account. Three nouns. The
   rest are the engineer's and should not leak.

## Karri Saarinen (Linear): quality is the feature

1. **Six doors that do not open.** Connections, Surveillance, Export, Verify chain, Trace and the
   copilot's "coming" rows. Linear ships nothing it cannot ship well; an unbuilt page is a promise
   broken on first click. Remove them from the navigation until they exist.
2. **Opinionated defaults, no scenario panel.** The scenario and role switcher is a dev tool, but
   it also reveals that the product has no opinion about what a first-run owner sees. Decide the
   first screen for an owner with one agent and no requests and make that perfect.
3. **Lists are the product.** The agent rows, the approvals, the decisions: these should be the
   crispest thing on screen, 40px rows, one line, keyboard-navigable, with a consistent right
   column. Today each list has a different shape, the badges differ per list, and none is
   keyboard-walkable.
4. **Speed is a design decision.** No budget, no prefetch, a skeleton on load. Set a per-route
   budget and treat a regression like a bug.
5. **Consistency over cleverness.** One badge style. One tag style for provenance. One button
   (done). One row height per density. The exceptions (an outlined "Not allowed" beside a filled
   "Allowed") are where the product looks unfinished.

## Emil Kowalski (Vercel, Linear; motion): motion should feel inevitable

1. **The 700ms draw-in is long.** Once per session it is a nice entrance; it is also 700ms before
   the owner can read the one thing they came for. 400ms, or draw in only on the very first visit.
2. **A dollar figure rolling for 520ms while the market ticks every second** means the figure is
   animating more than it is resting. Number Flow is right for a value that changes on an action
   (a range change) and wrong for a live tick. Roll on action, snap on tick.
3. **The pulsing last point is the one thing on the screen moving by itself**, and the file says
   nothing in the frame moves on its own. It is inside the chart, so it is technically allowed. It
   still trains the eye to look at a dot, and a dot is not information. Cut it.
4. **Nothing explains where things went.** Open an agent: the page cross-fades. Approve a request:
   it vanishes. Shared-element transitions for the owl and the name, and an exit that slides a
   resolved request into Resolved, would do more for perceived quality than every spring in the
   file.
5. **The tab-bar spring with 10% bounce** is fine; the range pill's 350ms is a touch slow for a
   control under the thumb. 250.

## Rauno Freiberg (Vercel; craft and interaction): the details are the design

1. **Focus is invisible.** Three tabs from the top of Home and no ring. On a product whose whole
   argument is control, the keyboard must be first class.
2. **Two pixel densities.** The owls are drawn on a 16-pixel grid at about 3px per pixel; the
   Pixelarticons are a 24px grid at 1px per pixel. Beside each other they look like two products.
   Either the icons scale to the owls' pixel (a 2x render) or the owls shrink to the icons'.
3. **The "Normal" chip has a checkbox glyph.** It reads as a control the owner can toggle. A state
   is a dot or a word, never a box with a tick.
4. **The info symbol is a different shape language** (a rounded square with an i) from the pixel
   icons around it. One system.
5. **Hover states are the well; press states are a 0.97 scale.** Good. Now the same on every row,
   chip and dock item, and a visible active state on the range control under the thumb.
6. **Axis labels overlap the line on the phone chart**, the "High-water mark" tag sits on the data.
   A chart on a phone needs no axis; the hero number is the axis.
7. **The 1996 browser's windows cascade on top of each other and hide the page.** The desktop is
   the best idea on the site and its window manager is the least finished part of it.

## Susan Kare (the Macintosh icons; pixel art): a pixel is a decision

1. **Five owls, one drawing.** Colour alone tells them apart; at 24px on a phone row it barely
   does. Each agent's owl could carry one feature (a tuft, a different brow, a rounder belly) so
   the silhouette tells them apart in greyscale.
2. **The owl has no expression it can change.** It is a fine drawing and it only ever does one
   thing. Four frames (awake, watching, asleep, stopped) on the same grid is a day's work and is
   the difference between a logo and a character.
3. **The ASCII wordmark and the pixel owls are two eras.** Figlet is a teletype; the owls are a
   Macintosh. The Pixelify wordmark in the title bar is the one that belongs with the owls. One era.
4. **Pixel icons must sit on the pixel grid**: a 24px icon drawn at 1px per pixel and rendered at
   a fractional position blurs. Snap every icon box to whole pixels and never scale between 24 and
   48.

## A research lead: watch five people

1. **"Allowed" next to "Asked you for approval"** will be read as "it bought it". Watch one owner
   misread it and the badge system redesigns itself.
2. **Nobody will know what "Normal" means** until they see "Exits only". States should be named
   by what the agent may do: "Trading", "Selling only", "Paused", "Stopped".
3. **The set-up asks for a z-score.** Five owners, five blank stares. Default it and say it can be
   changed.
4. **The summary is 2,500px long** and ends with the button. Owners will scroll to the bottom and
   press Create without reading. Make the short version the one they confirm.
5. **"PAPER · simulated funds" is on every screen** and will be invisible by day two. A
   disclosure that is everywhere is nowhere. Keep it in the header, remove it from every title.

## What we do better, in order

Each is a story; the ones that change a rule take a DEC. Items already in the review or the
direction file are cited rather than repeated.

1. **Home answers one question.** Needs you becomes the screen when something needs the owner,
   and the owl asleep with "Nothing needs you" when nothing does; the money, the agents and the
   decisions sit below, quieter. (Lu 1, Saarinen 2; review 2, 11; direction 6.)
2. **The mandate becomes the agent page.** The ladder and the headroom as the main column, the
   chart inside it. (Lu 3; review 6; direction moment 10.)
3. **One state system.** Modes renamed by what the agent may do; one badge style in one column;
   the mode chip a dot and a word, never a checkbox; "Waiting for you" distinct from "Allowed".
   (Saarinen 5, Freiberg 3, research 1 and 2; review 3.)
4. **Remove the unbuilt.** Every "next slice" page and row out of the dock, the palette and the
   More sheet until it exists. (Saarinen 1; review 17.)
5. **One conversation.** Messages, the copilot and set-up share one composer, one voice and one
   place; the copilot becomes the Owlhead thread. (Lu 5.)
6. **One pixel grid.** Decide the pixel size and make the owls, the icons, the info symbol and the
   hourglass share it; four frames per owl, and one distinguishing feature per agent. (Freiberg
   2 and 4, Kare 1 to 4; direction idea 1.)
7. **Motion tuned, not added.** Draw-in to 400ms or first visit only; roll on action, snap on
   tick; the pulsing point removed; shared-element transitions for the owl and name; resolved
   requests exit into Resolved. (Kowalski 1 to 5; direction craft 4.)
8. **Keyboard and focus.** A visible ring on every stop; `j`/`k`, `a`/`s`, `g h`, `?`. (Freiberg
   1; direction craft 7.)
9. **Less chrome.** The header reduced to the owl, the search and the account; the breadcrumb
   gone on top-level screens; the paper badge in the header only. (Lu 2, research 5; review 8.)
10. **Three nouns.** Agent, rules, account. Deployment, workspace, connection, environment and
    mandate-as-a-word leave the owner's screens; they stay in the audit and settings where they
    belong. (Lu 6.)
11. **The desktop's window manager.** Windows beside, not on top; maximise; the taskbar switches;
    one era of type. (Freiberg 7, Kare 3; direction website 12.)
12. **A speed budget in CI.** (Saarinen 4; direction craft 1.)

The panel agrees on one thing above the rest: the product has a premise most products would kill
for, and the screens do not yet trust it. Every item above removes something that is in the way of
the owl, the rules and the record.
